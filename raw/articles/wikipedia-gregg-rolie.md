Musical artist

**Gregg Alan Rolie** (born June 17, 1947) is an American keyboardist, singer and songwriter. Rolie was a founding member and original lead vocalist of both [Santana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santana_(band) "Santana (band)") and [Journey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journey_(band) "Journey (band)"). He also helmed rock group [the Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Storm_(American_band) "The Storm (American band)"), performed in [Ringo Starr & His All-Starr Band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ringo_Starr_&_His_All-Starr_Band "Ringo Starr & His All-Starr Band") until 2021, and since 2001 with his Gregg Rolie Band. Rolie is a two-time inductee of the [Rock and Roll Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_and_Roll_Hall_of_Fame "Rock and Roll Hall of Fame"), having been inducted both as a member of Santana in 1998 and as a member of Journey in 2017.

## Career

Rolie was born in [Seattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle "Seattle"), [Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_(state) "Washington (state)"), United States. Prior to Santana, he played with a group called William Penn and His Pals while attending [Cubberley High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellwood_P._Cubberley_High_School "Ellwood P. Cubberley High School") in [Palo Alto, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palo_Alto,_California "Palo Alto, California") 1965, his senior year.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregg_Rolie#cite_note-1)

In 1966, Rolie joined [Carlos Santana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Santana "Carlos Santana") and others to form the Santana Blues Band, which was later shortened simply to [Santana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santana_(band) "Santana (band)"). As a co-founding member of Santana, Rolie was part of the band's first wave of success, including an appearance at the [Woodstock Music and Art Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodstock_Festival "Woodstock Festival") in 1969[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregg_Rolie#cite_note-2) and central roles in several hit albums. He was their original lead vocalist, with his voice appearing on well-known Santana songs such as " [Black Magic Woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Magic_Woman "Black Magic Woman")" (US #4), " [Oye Como Va](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oye_Como_Va "Oye Como Va")", " [No One To Depend On](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_One_To_Depend_On "No One To Depend On")" and " [Evil Ways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evil_Ways_(Santana_song) "Evil Ways (Santana song)")". He also became well known for his skill on the [Hammond B3 organ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammond_organ "Hammond organ"), with solos on many of the aforementioned hits. He has song-writing credits on many tracks from this period. However, persistent differences with Carlos Santana regarding the musical direction of the band led Rolie to leave in 1972.

In 1973 Rolie joined a new band with ex-Santana guitarist [Neal Schon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neal_Schon "Neal Schon"). This became [Journey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journey_(band) "Journey (band)"). Starring in a lineup that featured Schon, [Aynsley Dunbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aynsley_Dunbar "Aynsley Dunbar"), [George Tickner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Tickner "George Tickner"), and [Ross Valory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Valory "Ross Valory"), he was keyboardist for the band's first six albums. On _[Journey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journey_(Journey_album) "Journey (Journey album)")_ and _[Look into the Future](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Look_into_the_Future "Look into the Future")_, he was lead vocalist, and on _[Next](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_(Journey_album) "Next (Journey album)")_ he shared those duties with guitarist Neal Schon. After [Steve Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Perry_(musician) "Steve Perry (musician)") joined the band in 1977, Rolie sang co-lead vocals on several songs on the albums _[Infinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinity_(Journey_album) "Infinity (Journey album)")_, _[Evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution_(Journey_album) "Evolution (Journey album)")_, and _[Departure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departure_(Journey_album) "Departure (Journey album)")_. He started to feel burned out by the non-stop schedule of touring, plus felt his creativity was drained, and soon announced he was leaving the band. Since his departure was amicable, he hand picked his successor, [Jonathan Cain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Cain "Jonathan Cain").

After leaving Journey in 1980, Rolie released several solo albums, including the eponymous _Gregg Rolie_ in 1985. This album featured the song " [I Wanna Go Back](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Wanna_Go_Back "I Wanna Go Back")", which later became a hit for [Eddie Money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Money "Eddie Money"), and included contributions from [Carlos Santana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Santana "Carlos Santana"), [Peter Wolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Wolf "Peter Wolf"), [Neal Schon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neal_Schon "Neal Schon"), and [Craig Chaquico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Chaquico "Craig Chaquico"). A second solo effort, _Gringo_, was released in 1987.

Rolie formed [the Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Storm_(American_band) "The Storm (American band)") in 1991 with [Steve Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Smith_(musician) "Steve Smith (musician)") and [Ross Valory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Valory "Ross Valory") of Journey. The band also included singer Kevin Chalfant and guitarist Josh Ramos. Prior to their inaugural tour in support of their first album, Steve Smith left the band and was replaced by Ron Wikso.

Similar to his work with Santana and Journey, Rolie played keyboards and was a co-lead vocalist on several tracks of the band's first, eponymous, album, which spent 17 weeks[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregg_Rolie#cite_note-3) on the _[Billboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_(magazine) "Billboard (magazine)")_ albums chart peaking at #133[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregg_Rolie#cite_note-StormHist-4) and spawned the hit singles "I've Got a Lot to Learn About Love,"[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregg_Rolie#cite_note-5) and "Show Me the Way."[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregg_Rolie#cite_note-StormHist-4) Despite this success, [Interscope Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interscope_Records "Interscope Records") shelved the band's second album, _Eye Of The Storm_, which was recorded in 1993. It was finally released in 1996 in Europe on the Music For Nations label, in 1997 in Japan on the Avex Trax/Bateknuckle label, and in 1998 in the United States on the Miramar label.

In 1998, Rolie and other former members of Santana, including Neal Schon, briefly reunited as [Abraxas Pool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraxas_Pool "Abraxas Pool"), releasing one album.

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a4/Rolie-07.jpg/500px-Rolie-07.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Rolie-07.jpg) Rolie in 2007.

When Schon left to lead a re-formed Journey later that year, Rolie and Ron Wikso began work in 1999 on a Gregg Rolie solo CD titled _Roots_, which eventually led to the forming of the Gregg Rolie Band. Besides Rolie and Wikso, _Roots_ featured appearances by [Neal Schon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neal_Schon "Neal Schon"), [Alphonso Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alphonso_Johnson "Alphonso Johnson"), [Dave Amato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Amato "Dave Amato"), [Adrian Areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Areas?action=edit&redlink=1 "Adrian Areas") and [Michael Carabello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Carabello "Michael Carabello"). The Gregg Rolie Band saw [Kurt Griffey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Griffey "Kurt Griffey") taking over guitar duties and the addition of [Wally Minko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wally_Minko?action=edit&redlink=1 "Wally Minko") as a second keyboardist. They recorded a live CD at Sturgis called _Rain Dance_, which was released in 2009.

In 2010, Rolie released _Five Days_ and subsequently formed a duo with [Alan Haynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Haynes "Alan Haynes"), which eventually led to the formation of the Gregg Rolie Quartet, with the addition of long time collaborator/drummer, Ron Wikso and bassist Evan "Sticky" Lopez.

From 2012 to 2021 he toured as a member of [Ringo Starr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ringo_Starr "Ringo Starr") and his [All Starr Band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ringo_Starr_&_His_All-Starr_Band "Ringo Starr & His All-Starr Band") performing Santana hits " [Black Magic Woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Magic_Woman "Black Magic Woman")", " [Evil Ways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evil_Ways_(Santana_song) "Evil Ways (Santana song)")" and "Everybody's Everything".[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregg_Rolie#cite_note-6) The band also included [Toto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toto_(band) "Toto (band)") guitarist [Steve Lukather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Lukather "Steve Lukather"), [Todd Rundgren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_Rundgren "Todd Rundgren"), [Richard Page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Page_(musician) "Richard Page (musician)"), [Mark Rivera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Rivera "Mark Rivera") and [Gregg Bissonette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregg_Bissonette "Gregg Bissonette").

On February 2, 2013 Carlos Santana confirmed that he would reunite his classic lineup, most of whom played Woodstock with him in 1969. Santana said of Rolie, "I'm pretty sure Gregg's going to do it." Speaking in 2012 of such a reunion, Rolie told Radio.com "it's just a matter of putting it together and going and doing it. I would do it. I think it's a great idea. People would love it. It could be great!"[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregg_Rolie#cite_note-7) In 2016, as part of Santana's original line-up they released their fourth album, titled _[Santana IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santana_IV "Santana IV")_.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregg_Rolie#cite_note-8)

On February 9, 2018, Rolie reunited with Schon for a charity show at San Francisco's The Independent, benefiting North Bay Fire Relief. The group also featured former Journey drummer [Deen Castronovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deen_Castronovo "Deen Castronovo") (who also sang some of the vocals) and bassist Marco Mendoza of [The Dead Daisies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dead_Daisies "The Dead Daisies").[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregg_Rolie#cite_note-9) In 2019 Rolie reunited with Schon, Castronovo and Mendoza for four more concert dates.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregg_Rolie#cite_note-10)
