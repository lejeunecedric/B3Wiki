_**Machine Head**_ is the sixth studio album by English rock band [Deep Purple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Purple "Deep Purple"). It was recorded in December 1971 in [Montreux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreux "Montreux"), Switzerland, and released on 30 March 1972, by [Purple Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purple_Records "Purple Records"). It is the band's third studio album to feature the [Mark II line-up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Deep_Purple_members "List of Deep Purple members") of [Ritchie Blackmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ritchie_Blackmore "Ritchie Blackmore"), [Ian Gillan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Gillan "Ian Gillan"), [Roger Glover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Glover "Roger Glover"), [Jon Lord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord "Jon Lord"), and [Ian Paice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Paice "Ian Paice").

Previous recording sessions had been slotted into the group's gigging schedule. This time, Deep Purple wanted to dedicate time to record an album away from the typical studio environment, hoping it would result in a sound closer to their live shows. They used the [Rolling Stones Mobile Studio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stones_Mobile_Studio "Rolling Stones Mobile Studio") for recording and block-booked the [Montreux Casino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreux_Casino "Montreux Casino") as a venue, but during a [Frank Zappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Zappa "Frank Zappa") and [the Mothers of Invention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mothers_of_Invention "The Mothers of Invention") concert immediately before the sessions, the casino burned to the ground after an audience member fired a flare gun into the ceiling. After a week of searching for an alternative venue, including a session at a nearby theatre that was abandoned due to noise complaints, the band managed to book the [Grand Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B4tel_des_Alpes-Grand_H%C3%B4tel "Hôtel des Alpes-Grand Hôtel"), closed for the winter, and converted it into a [live room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recording_studio#Layout "Recording studio") suitable for recording. These events, particularly the casino fire, became the inspiration for the song " [Smoke on the Water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoke_on_the_Water "Smoke on the Water")".

_Machine Head_ became Deep Purple's most commercially successful album, topping the charts in several countries, including the UK. Influential in the development of [heavy metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_metal_music "Heavy metal music"), it continues to be viewed favourably by music critics and has been reissued several times. It is widely considered one of the greatest and most influential rock albums of all time.

## Background

By 1971, Deep Purple had been touring for two years and earlier studio albums, such as _[Deep Purple in Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Purple_in_Rock "Deep Purple in Rock")_ (1970) and _[Fireball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fireball_(album) "Fireball (album)")_ (1971) were recorded in between live shows.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_Head_(album)#cite_note-FOOTNOTERobinsonGlover19974-4) The band felt that prior studio work did not sound as good as their live performances, and wanted to record in a stage environment. Drummer [Ian Paice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Paice "Ian Paice") recorded his kit in the corridor for some of the _Fireball_ sessions, believing it sounded better, and he wanted to find an alternative recording environment away from a typical sound-proofed studio.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_Head_(album)#cite_note-FOOTNOTERobinsonGlover19975,_17-5) Deep Purple were advised to record outside the United Kingdom as they would not have to pay as much [income tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_tax "Income tax").[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_Head_(album)#cite_note-FOOTNOTERobinsonGlover199717-6)

The group toured the UK from September to October 1971 and previewed some new material that was ultimately released on _Machine Head_, in particular "Highway Star" and "Lazy".[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_Head_(album)#cite_note-FOOTNOTERobinsonGlover19974-4)[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_Head_(album)#cite_note-7) They then began a tour of the United States, but it was cancelled two gigs in when singer [Ian Gillan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Gillan "Ian Gillan") contracted [hepatitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatitis "Hepatitis"). Guitarist [Ritchie Blackmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ritchie_Blackmore "Ritchie Blackmore") started to think about possible solo work for the future, teaming up with Paice and [Phil Lynott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Lynott "Phil Lynott") for a brief period as a possible side-project,[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_Head_(album)#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBloom201578-8) and Deep Purple as a whole looked forward to a dedicated block of time to record, getting away from the pressures of touring.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_Head_(album)#cite_note-FOOTNOTERobinsonGlover19975-9)[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_Head_(album)#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThompson2004121-10)

Deep Purple initially planned to record _Machine Head_ at [Montreux Casino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreux_Casino "Montreux Casino") in Switzerland in December 1971. They had booked the [Rolling Stones Mobile Studio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stones_Mobile_Studio "Rolling Stones Mobile Studio") and made hotel reservations.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_Head_(album)#cite_note-11) The casino was a large arena, containing several entertainment facilities. The band had performed there in May 1971, and befriended [Claude Nobs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Nobs "Claude Nobs"), founder and general manager of the [Montreux Jazz Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreux_Jazz_Festival "Montreux Jazz Festival").[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_Head_(album)#cite_note-FOOTNOTERobinsonGlover19975-9) Among others, [Led Zeppelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Led_Zeppelin "Led Zeppelin"), [Pink Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pink_Floyd "Pink Floyd"), and [Black Sabbath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sabbath "Black Sabbath") had all performed at the casino. It closed for refurbishments each winter, allowing it to be used as a recording venue. Deep Purple arrived there on 3 December 1971. After a final concert, they would have the location to themselves.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_Head_(album)#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThompson2004125%E2%80%93126-12) In return, the band tentatively proposed to perform a show at the casino, which would have allowed them to release a [double album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_album "Double album"), half live and half studio.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_Head_(album)#cite_note-FOOTNOTERobinsonGlover19975-9)

## Recording

> We were sitting in this bar restaurant about a quarter of a mile from the casino, and it was blazing. The wind was coming down off the mountains and taking the smoke and flames across the lake.

— [Ian Gillan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Gillan "Ian Gillan"), discussing the origin of " [Smoke on the Water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoke_on_the_Water "Smoke on the Water")"[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_Head_(album)#cite_note-FOOTNOTERobinsonGlover19976-13)

The last gig at Montreux Casino was a matinée performance by [Frank Zappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Zappa "Frank Zappa") and [the Mothers of Invention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mothers_of_Invention "The Mothers of Invention"). At the time, the group included singers [Flo & Eddie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flo_&_Eddie "Flo & Eddie"), multi-instrumentalist [Ian Underwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Underwood "Ian Underwood") and drummer [Aynsley Dunbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aynsley_Dunbar "Aynsley Dunbar").[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_Head_(album)#cite_note-FOOTNOTERobinsonGlover199719%E2%80%9320-14) During the show, a member of the audience fired a [flare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flare "Flare") into the building's roof.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_Head_(album)#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThompson2004126-15) Although the audience were initially unaware of the incident because the roof was covered by a false bamboo ceiling, about an hour into the set, people started seeing sparks emerging. The group stopped playing; according to bassist [Roger Glover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Glover "Roger Glover"), Zappa said that "no-one should panic, but ..... FIRE!" After this, management ordered a controlled evacuation. There were no fatalities, but a group were briefly trapped in the casino's basement before being rescued by Nobs. Shortly after everyone had been safely evacuated, the building went up in flames.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_Head_(album)#cite_note-FOOTNOTERobinsonGlover199720-16)

Nobs relocated Deep Purple to the Pavilion, a nearby theatre, where they recorded the basic tracks for a song provisionally named "Title No.1". Glover recalled waking up one morning saying the title "Smoke on the Water" out loud. From this, Gillan wrote the lyrics which describe the experience in Montreux.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_Head_(album)#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThompson2004127-17) A photograph of the burning Montreux Casino was included in the album's gatefold cover.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_Head_(album)#cite_note-18)

> We had [the Rolling Stones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rolling_Stones "The Rolling Stones")' [mobile recording unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stones_Mobile_Studio "Rolling Stones Mobile Studio") sitting outside in the snow ... once we got to the truck for a playback, even if we didn't think it was a perfect take, we'd go, 'Yeah, that's good enough.' Because we just couldn't stand going back again.

— [Ritchie Blackmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ritchie_Blackmore "Ritchie Blackmore")[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_Head_(album)#cite_note-19)

The Pavilion proved to be impractical to record in, as nearby residents complained to the police about the noise. Although roadies tried to prevent the police entering by holding the doors shut, the band were quickly evicted.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_Head_(album)#cite_note-FOOTNOTERobinsonGlover199720%E2%80%9321-20) They searched for other recording locations and settled with the empty [Grand Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B4tel_des_Alpes-Grand_H%C3%B4tel "Hôtel des Alpes-Grand Hôtel"), on the edge of Montreux. With the mobile unit parked at the main entrance, Deep Purple set up at the end of a corridor off the main lobby where the hallway came to a "T".[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_Head_(album)#cite_note-FOOTNOTERobinsonGlover199723-21) An assortment of equipment and sound-insulating mattresses were installed, which meant the band had to walk through bedrooms and across balconies to get to the recording van. This proved so arduous that Deep Purple stopped listening to playbacks of their recordings, instead performing until they were satisfied.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_Head_(album)#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThompson2004128-22) A [closed-circuit television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed-circuit_television "Closed-circuit television") system was set up so the band could communicate with engineer [Martin Birch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Birch "Martin Birch") and allow staff in the studio control room to see them.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_Head_(album)#cite_note-FOOTNOTERobinsonGlover19977-23)
