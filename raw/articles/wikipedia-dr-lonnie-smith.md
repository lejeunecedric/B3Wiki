Musical artist

**Lonnie Smith** (July 3, 1942 – September 28, 2021), styled as **Dr. Lonnie Smith**, was an American [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz "Jazz") [Hammond B3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammond_organ "Hammond organ") organist who was a member of the [George Benson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Benson "George Benson") quartet in the 1960s. He recorded albums with saxophonist [Lou Donaldson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Donaldson "Lou Donaldson") for [Blue Note](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Note_Records "Blue Note Records") before being signed as a solo act. He owned the label Pilgrimage, and was named the year's best organist by the [Jazz Journalists Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_Journalists_Association "Jazz Journalists Association") nine times.

## Early life

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Dr._Lonnie_Smith&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life")\]

Smith was born in [Lackawanna, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lackawanna,_New_York "Lackawanna, New York"), on July 3, 1942.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-gilbreath-1) He was raised by his mother and stepfather,[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-NYT_obit-2) and the family had a vocal group and radio program. He stated that his mother was a major influence on him musically, as she introduced him to [gospel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_music "Gospel music"), [classical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_music "Classical music"), and [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz "Jazz") music.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-manheim2005-3)

## Career

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Dr._Lonnie_Smith&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Career")\]

Smith was part of several vocal ensembles in the 1950s, including the Teen Kings, with saxophonist [Grover Washington Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grover_Washington_Jr. "Grover Washington Jr.") and his brother Darryl on drums.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-4) Art Kubera, the owner of a local music store, gave Smith his first organ, a [Hammond B3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammond_organ "Hammond organ").[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-feather-5)

### George Benson Quartet

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Dr._Lonnie_Smith&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: George Benson Quartet")\]

Smith's affinity for [R&B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhythm_and_blues "Rhythm and blues") mixed with his own personal style as he became active in the local music scene. He moved to New York City in 1965,[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-blake2015-6) where he met [George Benson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Benson "George Benson"), the guitarist for [Jack McDuff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_McDuff "Jack McDuff")'s band. Benson and Smith connected on a personal level, and the two formed the George Benson Quartet, featuring Lonnie Smith, in 1966.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-AllMusic_bio-7)[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-NEA-8)

### Solo career; _Finger Lickin' Good_

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Dr._Lonnie_Smith&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Solo career; Finger Lickin' Good")\]

After two albums under Benson's leadership, _[It's Uptown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It's_Uptown "It's Uptown")_ and _[Cookbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_George_Benson_Cookbook "The George Benson Cookbook")_, Smith recorded his first solo album ( _Finger Lickin' Good Soul Organ_) in 1967,[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-9) with George Benson (and guest [Melvin Sparks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melvin_Sparks "Melvin Sparks")) on guitar, [Ronnie Cuber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronnie_Cuber "Ronnie Cuber") on baritone saxophone, and Marion Booker on drums. This combination remained stable for the next five years.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-AllMusic_bio-7)

After recording several albums with Benson, Smith became a solo recording artist and subsequently recorded over 30 albums under his own name. Numerous prominent jazz artists joined Smith on his albums and in his live performances, including [Lee Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Morgan "Lee Morgan"), [David "Fathead" Newman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_%22Fathead%22_Newman "David \"Fathead\" Newman"), [King Curtis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Curtis "King Curtis"), [Terry Bradds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Bradds "Terry Bradds"), [Blue Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Mitchell "Blue Mitchell"), [Joey DeFrancesco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco "Joey DeFrancesco"), and [Joe Lovano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Lovano "Joe Lovano").[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-feather-5)

### Blue Note Records

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Dr._Lonnie_Smith&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Blue Note Records")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/49/Dr.Lonnie_SmithDSC0063b.jpg/250px-Dr.Lonnie_SmithDSC0063b.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Dr.Lonnie_SmithDSC0063b.jpg) Smith in 2009

In 1967, Smith met [Lou Donaldson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Donaldson "Lou Donaldson"), who put him in contact with [Blue Note Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Note_Records "Blue Note Records"). Donaldson asked Smith and Benson to record with him on his Blue Note album _[Alligator Bogaloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alligator_Bogaloo "Alligator Bogaloo")_, which was recorded in April 1967 and included [Melvin Lastie Sr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melvin_Lastie "Melvin Lastie") and Leo Morris (a.k.a. [Idris Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idris_Muhammad "Idris Muhammad")).[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-10) Blue Note signed Smith in 1968, and he released five albums on the label,[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-11) including _[Think!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Think!_(Lonnie_Smith_album) "Think! (Lonnie Smith album)")_ (with Lee Morgan, David Newman, Melvin Sparks, and Marion Booker)[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-AllMusic_bio-7) and _[Turning Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turning_Point_(Lonnie_Smith_album) "Turning Point (Lonnie Smith album)")_ (with Lee Morgan, [Bennie Maupin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bennie_Maupin "Bennie Maupin"), Melvin Sparks, and Idris Muhammad).[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-12)

Smith's next album, _[Move Your Hand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Move_Your_Hand "Move Your Hand")_, was recorded at the [Club Harlem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Harlem "Club Harlem") in [Atlantic City, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_City,_New_Jersey "Atlantic City, New Jersey"), in August 1969.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-13) The album's reception allowed his reputation to grow beyond the Northeast. He recorded another studio album, _[Drives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drives_(album) "Drives (album)")_, and a live album (unreleased at the time),[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-AllMusic_bio-7) _[Live at Club Mozambique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_at_Club_Mozambique_(Lonnie_Smith_album) "Live at Club Mozambique (Lonnie Smith album)")_ (recorded in [Detroit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit "Detroit") on May 21, 1970),[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-14) before leaving Blue Note.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-AllMusic_bio-7)

Smith recorded one album in 1971 for [Creed Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creed_Taylor "Creed Taylor")'s [CTI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CTI_Records "CTI Records") subsidiary label Kudu,[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-AllMusic_bio-7) CTI having already signed George Benson. After a break from recording, he then spent most of the mid-1970s with producer [Sonny Lester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonny_Lester "Sonny Lester") and his [Groove Merchant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groove_Merchant "Groove Merchant") label,[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-Bryant-15)[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-Brandle-16) then with Lester's new group of LRC labels.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-17) It resulted in four albums, with the music output veering between jazz, [soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul_music "Soul music"), [funk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funk "Funk"), [fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion "Jazz fusion"), and even an odd [disco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco "Disco")-styled track.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-discography-18)

Smith rejoined Blue Note in March 2015. He released his first Blue Note album in 45 years, titled _Evolution_, which was released January 29, 2016, featuring special guests [Robert Glasper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Glasper "Robert Glasper") and Joe Lovano.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-19) His next Blue Note album, _All in My Mind_, was recorded live at the [Jazz Standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_Standard_(jazz_club) "Jazz Standard (jazz club)") in New York City (celebrating his 75th birthday with longtime musical associates – guitarist [Jonathan Kreisberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Kreisberg "Jonathan Kreisberg") and drummer [Johnathan Blake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnathan_Blake "Johnathan Blake")), and released January 12, 2018.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-20) His third Blue Note album of this era, _Breathe_, was also recorded live and released March 26, 2021. It features [Iggy Pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iggy_Pop "Iggy Pop") on two studio vocal tracks: " [Why Can't We Live Together](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Why_Can't_We_Live_Together "Why Can't We Live Together")" and " [Sunshine Superman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunshine_Superman_(song) "Sunshine Superman (song)")".

## Personal life

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Dr._Lonnie_Smith&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Personal life")\]

Smith had six children.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-NYT_obit-2)

Smith died of [pulmonary fibrosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonary_fibrosis "Pulmonary fibrosis") on September 28, 2021, in [Fort Lauderdale, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Lauderdale,_Florida "Fort Lauderdale, Florida"), at the age of 79.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-NYT_obit-2)[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-Bryant-15)

## Public image

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Dr._Lonnie_Smith&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Public image")\]

Starting in the 1970s, Smith added the "Dr." title to his name.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-AllMusic_bio-7) The origin of the moniker is unclear and was not an [academic title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate "Doctorate"). One theory is that fellow musicians called Smith this due to his ability to "doctor up" their music.[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-:1-38) Another is that he adopted the title in an attempt to differentiate himself from other musicians.[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-39) Smith himself gave the following explanation:

> But I’m a doctor of music. I’ve been playing long enough to operate on it, and I do have a degree, and I _will_ operate on you. I’m a neurosurgeon. If you need something done to you, I can do it. But when I go up on that stand, the only thing I’m thinking of is music. I’m thinking to touch you with that music. I don’t think about the turban, I don’t think about the doctor – I just think about how I’m going to touch you.[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-:2-40)

Smith was well known for wearing a [turban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turban "Turban").[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-AllMusic_bio-7)[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-:2-40) He stated that the turban had no religious significance and was something he had worn since he was young.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-NYT_obit-2)[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-:1-38) Matt Collar of [AllMusic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AllMusic "AllMusic") suggested the turban was a theatrical gesture to his spiritual views on music,[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-AllMusic_bio-7) but Smith himself said he did not know why he started wearing a turban and referenced the iconic headwear of [Sun Ra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Ra "Sun Ra") and [Sonny Rollins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonny_Rollins "Sonny Rollins")'s mohawk.[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-:1-38)[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith#cite_note-:2-40)
