Musical artist

**Larry Young** (also known as **Khalid Yasin**\[ **Abdul Aziz**\]; October 7, 1940 – March 30, 1978)[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Young_(musician)#cite_note-LarkinJazz-1) was an American jazz organist and occasional pianist. Young's early work was strongly influenced by the [soul jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul_jazz "Soul jazz") of [Jimmy Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Smith_(musician) "Jimmy Smith (musician)"), but he later pioneered a more experimental, [modal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modal_jazz "Modal jazz") approach to the [Hammond B-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammond_B-3 "Hammond B-3").[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Young_(musician)#cite_note-AMG-2)

## Biography

Born and raised in [Newark, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newark,_New_Jersey "Newark, New Jersey"), United States, Young attended [Newark Arts High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newark_Arts_High_School "Newark Arts High School"), where he began performing with a vocal group and a jazz band.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Young_(musician)#cite_note-3) He was also the cousin of the drummer [Jimmie Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmie_Smith_(musician) "Jimmie Smith (musician)").[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Young_(musician)#cite_note-4)

Young played with various [R&B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhythm_and_blues "Rhythm and blues") bands in the 1950s, before gaining jazz experience with [Jimmy Forrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Forrest_(musician) "Jimmy Forrest (musician)"), [Lou Donaldson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Donaldson "Lou Donaldson"), [Kenny Dorham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenny_Dorham "Kenny Dorham"), [Hank Mobley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hank_Mobley "Hank Mobley") and [Tommy Turrentine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Turrentine "Tommy Turrentine").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Young_(musician)#cite_note-LarkinJazz-1) Recording as a leader for [Prestige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prestige_Records "Prestige Records") from 1960, Young made a number of soul jazz discs, _[Testifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Testifying_(album) "Testifying (album)")_, _[Young Blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Blues "Young Blues")_ and _[Groove Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groove_Street "Groove Street")_.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Young_(musician)#cite_note-LarkinJazz-1) When Young signed with [Blue Note](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Note_Records "Blue Note Records") around 1964, his music began to show the marked influence of [John Coltrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Coltrane "John Coltrane").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Young_(musician)#cite_note-LarkinJazz-1) In this period, he produced his most enduring work. He recorded several times as part of a trio with guitarist [Grant Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grant_Green "Grant Green") and drummer [Elvin Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvin_Jones "Elvin Jones"),[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Young_(musician)#cite_note-LarkinJazz-1) who were occasionally augmented by additional players. Most of these albums were released under Green's name, though _[Into Somethin'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Into_Somethin' "Into Somethin'")_ (with [Sam Rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Rivers_(jazz_musician) "Sam Rivers (jazz musician)") on saxophone) became Young's Blue Note debut.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Young_(musician)#cite_note-LarkinJazz-1) _[Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unity_(Larry_Young) "Unity (Larry Young)")_, recorded in 1965, remains his best-known album; it features a front line of [Joe Henderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Henderson "Joe Henderson") and the young [Woody Shaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woody_Shaw "Woody Shaw").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Young_(musician)#cite_note-LarkinJazz-1) Subsequent albums for Blue Note ( _Contrasts_, _Of Love and Peace_, _Heaven On Earth_, _Mother Ship_) also drew on elements of the 1960s avant-garde and utilised local musicians from Young's hometown of Newark. Young then became a part of some of the earliest [fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion "Jazz fusion") groups: first on [_Emergency!_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency!_(album) "Emergency! (album)") with [the Tony Williams Lifetime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tony_Williams_Lifetime "The Tony Williams Lifetime") (with [Tony Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Williams_(drummer) "Tony Williams (drummer)") and [John McLaughlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McLaughlin_(musician) "John McLaughlin (musician)")) and also on [Miles Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles_Davis "Miles Davis")'s _[Bitches Brew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitches_Brew "Bitches Brew")_.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Young_(musician)#cite_note-LarkinJazz-1) His sound with Lifetime was made distinctive by his often very percussive approach and regular heavy use of guitar and synthesizer-like effects. He is also known for a jam he recorded with rock guitarist [Jimi Hendrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimi_Hendrix "Jimi Hendrix"), which was released after Hendrix's death on the album, _[Nine to the Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine_to_the_Universe "Nine to the Universe")_.

In March 1978, he checked into a hospital for stomach pains.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Young_(musician)#cite_note-5) He died there on March 30, 1978, while being treated for what is said to have been pneumonia.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Young_(musician)#cite_note-6) However, the actual cause of his death is unclear.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Young_(musician)#cite_note-AMG-2)[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Young_(musician)#cite_note-7)
