Musical artist

**John Douglas** " **Jon**" **Lord** (9 June 1941–16 July 2012)[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-1) was an English keyboardist and composer. In 1968, Lord co-founded the [hard rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_rock "Hard rock") band [Deep Purple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Purple "Deep Purple"). Lord performed on most of the band's most popular songs; he and drummer [Ian Paice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Paice "Ian Paice") were the only continuous members in the band between 1968 and 1976, and also from its revival in 1984 until his retirement in 2002. He also played for the bands [Whitesnake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitesnake "Whitesnake"), [Paice Ashton Lord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paice_Ashton_Lord "Paice Ashton Lord"), [the Artwoods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Artwoods "The Artwoods"), [the Flower Pot Men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Flower_Pot_Men "The Flower Pot Men") and [Santa Barbara Machine Head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Barbara_Machine_Head "Santa Barbara Machine Head").

Lord became known for his pioneering work in fusing rock with classical or [baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_music "Baroque music") forms, especially with Deep Purple. His distinctive organ playing during Deep Purple's hard rock period was essential to the band's signature heavy sound and contributed to the early development of [heavy metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_metal_music "Heavy metal music"). On 11 November 2010, he was inducted as an [Honorary Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_Fellow "Honorary Fellow") of [Stevenson College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stevenson_College,_Edinburgh "Stevenson College, Edinburgh") in Edinburgh, Scotland. On 15 July 2011, he was awarded an honorary [Doctor of Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Music "Doctor of Music") degree at [De Montfort Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Montfort_Hall "De Montfort Hall") by the [University of Leicester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leicester "University of Leicester"). Lord was posthumously inducted into the [Rock and Roll Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_and_Roll_Hall_of_Fame "Rock and Roll Hall of Fame") on 8 April 2016 as a member of Deep Purple.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-2)

## Early life

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jon_Lord&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life")\]

### Growing up in Leicester

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jon_Lord&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Growing up in Leicester")\]

Lord was born in [Leicester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leicester "Leicester") on 9 June 1941 to Miriam (1912–1995; née Hudson) and Reginald Lord, and lived in the city until he was 20.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-3)[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-4) His father was an amateur saxophonist and encouraged Lord to play music from an early age. He studied classical piano from the age of five, with a local teacher, Frederick Allt, and this focus on a classical grounding to his material was a recurring trademark in his work, in composition, in arranging and in his instrumental solos on piano, organ and electronic keyboards. In particular his influences ranged from [Johann Sebastian Bach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Sebastian_Bach "Johann Sebastian Bach") (a constant reference in his music and in his keyboard improvisation) to Medieval popular music and the English tradition of [Edward Elgar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Elgar "Edward Elgar"). He attended [Wyggeston Grammar School for Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyggeston_Grammar_School_for_Boys "Wyggeston Grammar School for Boys") between 1952 and 1958 where he gained [O Level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GCE_Ordinary_Level "GCE Ordinary Level") passes in French, music and mathematics, participated in amateur dramatics and the school choir alongside his organ and piano studies, and then worked as a clerk in a solicitor's office for two years.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-5)

Lord absorbed the [blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blues "Blues") sounds that played a key part in his rock career, principally the raw sounds of the great American jazz and blues organists, such as [Wild Bill Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_Bill_Davis "Wild Bill Davis"), [Jimmy Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Smith_(musician) "Jimmy Smith (musician)"), [Jimmy McGriff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_McGriff "Jimmy McGriff") ("All About My Girl") and [Jack McDuff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_McDuff "Jack McDuff") ("Rock Candy"), as well as the stage showmanship of [Jerry Lee Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Lee_Lewis "Jerry Lee Lewis") and performers like [Buddy Holly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddy_Holly "Buddy Holly"), whom he saw perform at the De Montfort Hall in Leicester in March 1958.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-6) The jazz-blues organ style of black R&B organ players in the 1950s and 1960s, using the trademark blues-organ sound of the [Hammond organ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammond_organ "Hammond organ") (B3 and C3 models) and combining it with the [Leslie speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_speaker "Leslie speaker") system (the well-known Hammond-Leslie speaker combination), were seminal influences on Lord. Lord also stated that he was heavily influenced by the organ-based [progressive rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_rock "Progressive rock") played by [Vanilla Fudge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanilla_Fudge "Vanilla Fudge") after seeing that band perform in Great Britain in 1967, and earlier by the personal direction he received from British organ pioneer [Graham Bond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_Bond "Graham Bond").[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-7)

### Move to London

Lord moved to London in 1959–60, intent on an acting career and enrolling at the [Central School of Speech and Drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_School_of_Speech_and_Drama "Central School of Speech and Drama"), in London's [Swiss Cottage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Cottage "Swiss Cottage"). Following a celebrated student rebellion he became a founder of [Drama Centre London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drama_Centre_London "Drama Centre London"), from where he graduated in 1964. Small acting parts followed, including in the British TV series, _[Emergency Ward 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_Ward_10 "Emergency Ward 10")_, and Lord continued playing the piano and the organ in [nightclubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nightclub "Nightclub") and as a session musician to earn a living. He started his band career in London in 1960 with the jazz ensemble [The Bill Ashton Combo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Ashton_(jazz_musician) "Bill Ashton (jazz musician)"). Ashton became a key figure in jazz education in Britain, creating what later became the [National Youth Jazz Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Youth_Jazz_Orchestra "National Youth Jazz Orchestra"). Between 1960 and 1963, Lord and Ashton both moved on to Red Bludd's Bluesicians (also known as The Don Wilson Quartet), the latter of which featured the singer [Arthur "Art" Wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Wood "Art Wood"), brother of guitarist [Ronnie Wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronnie_Wood "Ronnie Wood"). Wood had previously sung with [Alexis Korner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexis_Korner "Alexis Korner")'s [Blues Incorporated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blues_Incorporated "Blues Incorporated") and was a junior figure in the British blues movement.

Somewhere around this time, Lord altered his first name spelling from "John" to the perhaps more contemporary "Jon".

In this period, Lord's session credits included playing the keyboards in the number one song " [You Really Got Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You_Really_Got_Me "You Really Got Me")" by [The Kinks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Kinks "The Kinks"), released in 1964; however in a _Guitar World_ interview, Kinks frontman [Ray Davies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Davies "Ray Davies") stated it was actually [Arthur Greenslade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Greenslade "Arthur Greenslade") playing piano on that particular track.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-8)

Following the break-up of Redd Bludd's Bluesicians in late 1963, Wood, Lord, and the drummer Red Dunnage put together a new band, The Art Wood Combo. This also included Derek Griffiths (guitar) and Malcolm Pool (bass guitar). Dunnage left in December 1964 to be replaced by [Keef Hartley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keef_Hartley "Keef Hartley"), who had previously replaced [Ringo Starr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ringo_Starr "Ringo Starr") in ' [Rory Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rory_Storm "Rory Storm") and the Hurricanes'. This band, later known as " [The Artwoods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Artwoods "The Artwoods")", focused on the organ as the bluesy, rhythmic core of their sound, in common with the contemporary bands [The Spencer Davis Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Spencer_Davis_Group "The Spencer Davis Group") (with [Steve Winwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Winwood "Steve Winwood") on organ) and [The Animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Animals "The Animals") (with [Alan Price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Price "Alan Price") on organ). They made appearances on the BBC's _[Saturday Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday_Club_(BBC_radio) "Saturday Club (BBC radio)")_ radio show and on such TV programs as _[Ready Steady Go!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ready_Steady_Go! "Ready Steady Go!")_. They also performed abroad, and appeared on the first _Ready Steady Goes Live_, promoting their first single the [Lead Belly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lead_Belly "Lead Belly") song "Sweet Mary" — but significant commercial success eluded them. Their only charting single was "I Take What I Want", which reached number 28 on 8 May 1966.

This band regrouped in 1967 as the "St. Valentine's Day Massacre". This was an attempt to cash in on the 1930s gangster craze set off by the American film _[Bonnie and Clyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonnie_and_Clyde_(film) "Bonnie and Clyde (film)")_. Hartley left the band in 1967 to join [John Mayall & the Bluesbreakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Mayall_&_the_Bluesbreakers "John Mayall & the Bluesbreakers"). Lord next founded the blues-influenced [Santa Barbara Machine Head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Barbara_Machine_Head "Santa Barbara Machine Head"), featuring Art's younger brother, [Ronnie Wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronnie_Wood "Ronnie Wood"), writing and recording three powerful keyboard-driven instrumental tracks, giving a preview of the future style of [Deep Purple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Purple "Deep Purple"). Soon thereafter, Lord went on to cover for the keyboard player Billy Day in [The Flower Pot Men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Flower_Pot_Men "The Flower Pot Men"), where he met the bass guitarist [Nick Simper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Simper "Nick Simper") along with drummer [Carlo Little](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_Little "Carlo Little") and guitarist [Ged Peck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ged_Peck "Ged Peck"). Lord and Simper then toured with this band in 1967 to promote its hit single " [Let's Go to San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Let's_Go_to_San_Francisco "Let's Go to San Francisco")", but the two never recorded with this band.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

### Formation of Deep Purple

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jon_Lord&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Formation of Deep Purple")\]

In early 1967, through his roommate [Chris Curtis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Curtis_(musician) "Chris Curtis (musician)") of the [Searchers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Searchers_(band) "The Searchers (band)"), Lord met businessman [Tony Edwards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Edwards_(manager) "Tony Edwards (manager)") who was looking to invest in the music business alongside partners Ron Hire and John Coletta (HEC Enterprises). Session guitarist [Ritchie Blackmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ritchie_Blackmore "Ritchie Blackmore") was called in and met Lord for the first time, but Chris Curtis's erratic behaviour led the trio nowhere. Edwards was impressed enough by Jon Lord to ask him to form a band after Curtis faded out. Said Edwards, "I couldn't really cope with \[Curtis\] but I had a great rapport with Jon Lord; here was somebody sensible, somebody I could communicate with on my level."[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-9) Simper was contacted, and Blackmore was recalled from [Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg "Hamburg"). Although top British player [Bobbie Clarke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobbie_Clarke "Bobbie Clarke") was the first choice as drummer, during the auditions for a singer, [Rod Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rod_Evans "Rod Evans") of "The Maze" came in with his own drummer, [Ian Paice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Paice "Ian Paice"). Blackmore, who had been impressed by Paice's drumming when he met him in 1967, set up an audition for Paice as well. The band was called the "Roundabout" at first and began rehearsals at Deeves Hall in Hertfordshire. In March 1968, this became the "Mark 1" line-up of " [Deep Purple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Purple "Deep Purple")": Lord, Simper, Blackmore, Paice, and Evans.

Lord also did session work in the late 1960s with the band "Boz", along with Blackmore (guitar) and Paice (drums), given that Boz was being produced by [Derek Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derek_Lawrence "Derek Lawrence") who produced Deep Purple's first three albums. Boz was led by [Boz Burrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boz_Burrell "Boz Burrell") (vocals/guitar, later bassist for [King Crimson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Crimson "King Crimson") and [Bad Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Company "Bad Company")) and also included [Chas Hodges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chas_Hodges "Chas Hodges") (bass guitar).[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-10)

## Deep Purple (1968-1976/1984-2002)

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jon_Lord&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Deep Purple (1968-1976/1984-2002)")\]

### 1968–1970

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jon_Lord&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: 1968–1970")\]

It was in these three years that Lord's trademark keyboard sound emerged. Ignoring the emergence of the [Moog synthesizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moog_synthesizer "Moog synthesizer"), as pioneered in rock by such players as [Keith Emerson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson "Keith Emerson"), Lord began experimenting with a keyboard sound produced by driving the Hammond organ through [Marshall amplifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Amplification "Marshall Amplification") in an effort to match the attack and volume of Blackmore's guitar. Lord's version was heavier than a blues sound, and it often featured distortion and a far harder, industrial type sound that became the trademark Jon Lord organ sound. [Rick Wakeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Wakeman "Rick Wakeman") publicly expressed admiration for Lord's mould-breaking work on the organ.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-11) This delivered a rhythmic foundation to complement Blackmore's speed and virtuosity on lead guitar. Lord also loved the sound of an [RMI 368 Electra-Piano and Harpsichord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RMI_368_Electra-Piano_and_Harpsichord "RMI 368 Electra-Piano and Harpsichord"), which he used on such songs as "Demon's Eye" and "Space Truckin'".

In 1973, Lord's original Hammond C3 malfunctioned and he bought another from [Christine McVie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christine_McVie "Christine McVie") of [Fleetwood Mac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleetwood_Mac "Fleetwood Mac"). Also around this time, Lord and his keyboard technician, Mike Phillips, combined his Hammond C3 Organ with the RMI. Lord kept this particular Hammond C3 until his retirement from the band in 2002, when he passed it to successor [Don Airey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Airey "Don Airey"). That instrument was retired from stage use a few years later, as it had become "pretty knackered" according to Airey.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-12)

By pushing the Hammond-Leslie sound through Marshall amplification, Lord created a growling, heavy, mechanical sound which allowed him to compete with Blackmore as a soloist, with an organ that sounded as prominent as the lead guitar. Said one reviewer, "many have tried to imitate \[Lord's\] style, and all failed."[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-vanderlee1-13) Said Lord himself, "There's a way of playing a Hammond \[that's\] different. A lot of people make the mistake of thinking that you can play a Hammond with a piano technique. Well, you can, but it _sounds_ like you are playing a Hammond with a piano technique. Really, you have to learn how to play an organ. It's a [legato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legato "Legato") technique; it's a technique to achieve legato on a non-legato instrument."[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-14)

In early Deep Purple recordings, Lord had appeared to be the leader of the band.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-15) Despite the cover songs " [Hush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hush_(Billy_Joe_Royal_song) "Hush (Billy Joe Royal song)")" and " [Kentucky Woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky_Woman "Kentucky Woman")" becoming hits in North America, Deep Purple never made chart success in the UK until the _[Concerto for Group and Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concerto_for_Group_and_Orchestra "Concerto for Group and Orchestra")_ album dented the UK charts in early 1970. Lord's willingness later to play many of the key rhythm parts gave Blackmore the freedom to let loose both live and on record.

On Deep Purple's second and third albums, Lord began indulging his ambition to fuse rock with classical music. An early example of this is the song "Anthem" from the album _[The Book of Taliesyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Book_of_Taliesyn "The Book of Taliesyn")_ (1968), but a more prominent example is the song "April" from the band's [self-titled third album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Purple_(album) "Deep Purple (album)") (1969). The song is recorded in three parts: (1) Lord and Blackmore only, on keyboards and acoustic guitar, respectively; (2) an orchestral arrangement complete with strings; and (3) the full rock band with vocals. Lord's ambition enhanced his reputation among fellow musicians, but caused tension within the group. Simper later said, "The reason the music lacked direction was Jon Lord fucked everything up with his classical ideas."[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-16) Blackmore agreed to go along with Lord's experimentation, provided Blackmore was given the lead on the next band album.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-vanderlee1-13)

The resulting Lord-composed _Concerto For Group and Orchestra_ was one of rock's earliest attempts to fuse two distinct musical idioms. Performed live at the [Royal Albert Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Albert_Hall "Royal Albert Hall") on 24 September 1969 (with new band members [Ian Gillan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Gillan "Ian Gillan") and [Roger Glover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Glover "Roger Glover"), Evans and Simper having been fired), it was recorded by the [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC") and later released as an album in December 1969. _Concerto_ gave Deep Purple their first highly publicised taste of mainstream fame and gave Lord the confidence to believe that his experiment and his compositional skill had a future, as well as giving Lord the opportunity to work with established classical figures, such as conductor Sir [Malcolm Arnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malcolm_Arnold "Malcolm Arnold"), who brought his skills to bear by helping Lord realise the work and to protect him from the inevitable disdain of the older members of the orchestra.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

### 1970–1976

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jon_Lord&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: 1970–1976")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9b/Deep_Purple%2C_John_Lord_1970.jpg/330px-Deep_Purple%2C_John_Lord_1970.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Deep_Purple,_John_Lord_1970.jpg) Lord with [Deep Purple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Purple "Deep Purple") on tour at the [Niedersachsenhalle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niedersachsenhalle "Niedersachsenhalle"), [Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanover "Hanover"), Germany, 1970

Purple began work on _[Deep Purple in Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Purple_in_Rock "Deep Purple in Rock"),_ released by their new label [Harvest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvest_Records "Harvest Records") in 1970 and now recognised as one of [hard rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_rock "Hard rock")'s key early works. Lord and Blackmore competed to out-dazzle each other, often in classical-style, midsection 'call and answer' improvisation (on tracks like "Speed King" and "Bloodsucker"), something they employed to great effect live. Ian Gillan said that Lord provided the idea on the main organ riff for " [Child in Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_in_Time "Child in Time")" although the riff was also based on [It's a Beautiful Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It's_a_Beautiful_Day "It's a Beautiful Day")'s 1969 psychedelic hit song "Bombay Calling".[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-17) Lord's experimental solo on "Hard Lovin' Man" (complete with police-siren interpolation) from this album was his personal favourite among his Deep Purple studio performances.

Deep Purple released another six studio albums between 1971 ( _[Fireball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fireball_(album) "Fireball (album)")_) and 1975 ( _[Come Taste the Band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Come_Taste_the_Band "Come Taste the Band")_). Gillan and Glover left in 1973 and Blackmore in 1975, and the band disintegrated in 1976. The highlights of Lord's Purple work in the period include the 1972 album _[Machine Head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_Head_(album) "Machine Head (album)")_ (featuring his rhythmic underpinnings on " [Smoke on the Water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoke_on_the_Water "Smoke on the Water")" and " [Space Truckin'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Truckin' "Space Truckin'")", plus the organ solos on " [Highway Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_Star_(song) "Highway Star (song)")", " [Pictures of Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pictures_of_Home "Pictures of Home")" and " [Lazy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazy_(Deep_Purple_song) "Lazy (Deep Purple song)")"), the sonic bombast of the _[Made in Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Made_in_Japan_(Deep_Purple_album) "Made in Japan (Deep Purple album)")_ live album (1972), an extended, effect-laden solo on "Rat Bat Blue" from the _[Who Do We Think We Are](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who_Do_We_Think_We_Are "Who Do We Think We Are")_ album (1973), and his overall playing on the _[Burn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burn_(Deep_Purple_album) "Burn (Deep Purple album)")_ album from 1974.

[Roger Glover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Glover "Roger Glover") would later describe Lord as a true "Zen-archer soloist", someone whose best keyboard improvisation often came at the first attempt. Lord's strict reliance on the Hammond C3 organ sound, as opposed to the synthesizer experimentation of his contemporaries, places him firmly in the jazz-blues category as a band musician and far from the progressive-rock sound of [Keith Emerson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson "Keith Emerson") and [Rick Wakeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Wakeman "Rick Wakeman"). Lord rarely ventured into the synthesizer territory on Purple albums, often limiting his experimentation to the use of the [ring modulator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_modulation "Ring modulation") with the Hammond, to give live performances on tracks like "Space Truckin'" a distinctive 'spacey' sound. Instances of his Deep Purple synthesizer use (he became an endorser of the [ARP Odyssey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARP_Odyssey "ARP Odyssey")) include "'A' 200", the final track from _Burn_, and "Love Child" on the _Come Taste the Band_ album.

In early 1973 Lord stated: "We're as valid as anything by Beethoven."[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-NME_Rock_'N'_Roll_Years-18)

## As a composer

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jon_Lord&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: As a composer")\]

Lord continued to focus on his classical aspirations alongside his Deep Purple career. The [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC"), buoyed by the success of the Concerto, commissioned him to write another piece and the resulting "Gemini Suite" was performed by Deep Purple and the Light Music Society under [Malcolm Arnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malcolm_Arnold "Malcolm Arnold") at the [Royal Festival Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Festival_Hall "Royal Festival Hall") in September 1970, and then in [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich "Munich") with the [Kammerorchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Chamber_Orchestra "Munich Chamber Orchestra") conducted by [Eberhard Schoener](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eberhard_Schoener "Eberhard Schoener") in January 1972. It then became the basis for Lord's first solo album, _[Gemini Suite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemini_Suite "Gemini Suite")_, released in November 1972, with vocals by [Yvonne Elliman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yvonne_Elliman "Yvonne Elliman") and [Tony Ashton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Ashton "Tony Ashton") and with the [London Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Symphony_Orchestra "London Symphony Orchestra") backing a band that included [Albert Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Lee "Albert Lee") on guitar.

Lord's collaboration with the highly experimental and supportive Schoener resulted in a second live performance of the Suite in late 1973 and a new Lord album with Schoener, entitled _[Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windows_(Jon_Lord_album) "Windows (Jon Lord album)")_, in 1974. It proved to be Lord's most experimental work and was released to mixed reactions. However, the dalliances with [Bach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._S._Bach "J. S. Bach") on _Windows_ and the pleasure of collaborating with Schoener resulted in perhaps Lord's most confident solo work and perhaps his strongest orchestral album, _[Sarabande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarabande_(album) "Sarabande (album)")_, recorded in Germany in September 1975 with the [Philharmonia Hungarica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philharmonia_Hungarica "Philharmonia Hungarica") conducted by Schoener. Composed of eight pieces (from the opening sweep of Fantasia to the Finale), at least five pieces form the typical construction of a baroque dance suite. The key pieces ( [Sarabande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarabande "Sarabande"), [Gigue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigue "Gigue"), [Bouree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bouree "Bouree"), [Pavane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavane "Pavane") and [Caprice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capriccio_(music) "Capriccio (music)")) feature rich orchestration complemented sometimes by the interpolation of rock themes, played by a session band comprising [Pete York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_York "Pete York"), [Mark Nauseef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Nauseef "Mark Nauseef") and [Andy Summers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Summers "Andy Summers"), with organ and synthesizers played by Lord.

In March 1974, Lord and Paice had collaborated with friend [Tony Ashton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Ashton "Tony Ashton") on _[First of the Big Bands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_of_the_Big_Bands "First of the Big Bands")_, credited to 'Tony Ashton & Jon Lord' and featuring a rich array of session talent, including [Carmine Appice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmine_Appice "Carmine Appice"), Ian Paice, [Peter Frampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Frampton "Peter Frampton"), and [Pink Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pink_Floyd "Pink Floyd") saxophonist/sessioner [Dick Parry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Parry "Dick Parry"). They performed much of the set live at the [London Palladium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Palladium "London Palladium") in September 1974.

This formed the basis of Lord's first post-Deep Purple project [Paice Ashton Lord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paice_Ashton_Lord "Paice Ashton Lord"), which lasted only a year and spawned a single album, _[Malice in Wonderland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malice_in_Wonderland_(Paice_Ashton_Lord_album) "Malice in Wonderland (Paice Ashton Lord album)")_ in 1977, recorded at [Musicland Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musicland_Studios "Musicland Studios") at the Arabella Hotel in Munich. A second album was begun but subsequently abandoned. He created an informal group of friends and collaborators including Ashton, Paice, [Bernie Marsden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernie_Marsden "Bernie Marsden"), [Boz Burrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boz_Burrell "Boz Burrell") and later, Bad Company's [Mick Ralphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mick_Ralphs "Mick Ralphs"), [Simon Kirke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Kirke "Simon Kirke") and others. Over the same period, Lord guested on albums by [Maggie Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maggie_Bell "Maggie Bell"), [Nazareth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazareth_(band) "Nazareth (band)") and even folk artist [Richard Digance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Digance "Richard Digance").

Lord also guested as one of several keyboard players on the live performance of David Bedford's _The Odyssey_. The composer and musician ( _The Orchestral Tubular Bells_ (1975), _Star's End_ (1974), _The Rime Of The Ancient Mariner_ (1975)), performed a live concert of a musical version of Homer's play, at the Royal Albert Hall on 25 January 1977. [Andy Summers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Summers "Andy Summers"), who found fame in [The Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Police "The Police"), played guitar on the _Odyssey_ studio recording and asked Lord to be one of the keyboard players for this concert. The set began with a cut down live version of _Sarabande_, on which Summers had played on the studio recording session in September 1975.

Eager to pay off a huge tax bill upon his return the UK in the late-1970s (Purple's excesses included their own tour jet and a home Lord rented in Malibu from actress [Ann-Margret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann-Margret "Ann-Margret") and where he wrote the _Sarabande_ album), Lord joined former Deep Purple band member [David Coverdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Coverdale "David Coverdale")'s new band, [Whitesnake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitesnake "Whitesnake") in August 1978 (Ian Paice joined them in 1980 and stayed until 1982).

## Whitesnake, 1978–1984

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jon_Lord&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Whitesnake, 1978–1984")\]

Lord's job in Whitesnake was largely limited to adding colour to round out a blues-rock sound that already accommodated two lead guitarists, [Bernie Marsden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernie_Marsden "Bernie Marsden") and [Micky Moody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micky_Moody "Micky Moody"). He added a [Yamaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamaha_Corporation "Yamaha Corporation") [CP-70 electric piano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_grand_piano "Electric grand piano") to his set-up and finally a huge bank of synthesizers onstage courtesy of Moog ( [Minimoog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimoog "Minimoog"), Opus, [Polymoog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymoog "Polymoog")) so he could play the 12-bar blues the band often required and recreate string section and other effects. Such varied work is evident on tracks like " [Here I Go Again](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Here_I_Go_Again "Here I Go Again")", "Wine, Women and Song", "She's a Woman" and "Till the Day I Die". A number of singles entered the UK chart, taking the now 30-something Lord onto _[Top of the Pops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_of_the_Pops "Top of the Pops")_ with regularity between 1980 and 1983. He described himself as a poorly paid hired hand, but fans saw little of this discord and Whitesnake's commercial success kept him at the forefront of readers' polls as heavy rock's foremost keyboard maestro. His dissatisfaction (and Coverdale's eagerness to revamp the band's line-up and lower the average age to help crack the US market) led to the reunion of Deep Purple Mk II in 1984.

Jon Lord's last Whitesnake concert took place in the [Swedish TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_in_Sweden "Television in Sweden") programme _Måndagsbörsen_ on 16 April 1984.

While with Whitesnake, Lord also recorded two solo albums. 1982's _Before I Forget_ featured a largely conventional eight-song line-up, no orchestra and with the bulk of the songs being either mainstream rock tracks ("Hollywood Rock And Roll", "Chance on a Feeling"), or – specifically on side two – songs sung by the mother and daughter duo [Vicki Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicki_Brown_(singer) "Vicki Brown (singer)") and [Sam Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Brown_(singer) "Sam Brown (singer)") and vocalist [Elmer Gantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmer_Gantry's_Velvet_Opera "Elmer Gantry's Velvet Opera") as well as piano and synthesiser instrumentals such as "Burntwood", named after Lord's stately Oxfordshire home at the time. The album also boasted the cream of British rock talent, including the session drummer (and [National Youth Jazz Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Youth_Jazz_Orchestra "National Youth Jazz Orchestra") alumnus) [Simon Phillips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Phillips_(drummer) "Simon Phillips (drummer)"), [Cozy Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cozy_Powell "Cozy Powell"), [Neil Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Murray_(British_musician) "Neil Murray (British musician)"), [Simon Kirke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Kirke "Simon Kirke"), [Boz Burrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boz_Burrell "Boz Burrell") and [Mick Ralphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mick_Ralphs "Mick Ralphs").

Additionally, Lord was commissioned by producer Patrick Gamble for [Central Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_Central "ITV Central") to write the soundtrack for their 1984 TV series, _[Country Diary of an Edwardian Lady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_Diary_of_an_Edwardian_Lady "Country Diary of an Edwardian Lady")_ using themes composed by Lord. Lord became firmly established as a member of UK rock's " [Oxfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxfordshire "Oxfordshire") mansion aristocracy" – with a home, Burntwood Hall, set in 23.5 acres (9.5ha) at [Goring-on-Thames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goring-on-Thames "Goring-on-Thames"), complete with its own cricket pitch and a hand-painted Challen baby [grand piano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_piano "Grand piano") previously owned by [Shirley Bassey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirley_Bassey "Shirley Bassey"). He was asked to guest on albums by friends [George Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Harrison "George Harrison") ( _[Gone Troppo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gone_Troppo "Gone Troppo")_ from 1982) and Pink Floyd's [David Gilmour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Gilmour "David Gilmour") (1984's _[About Face](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/About_Face_(album) "About Face (album)")_), Cozy Powell ( _Octopuss_ in 1983) and to play on an adaptation of [Kenneth Grahame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Grahame "Kenneth Grahame")'s 1908 classic, _[The Wind in the Willows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wind_in_the_Willows "The Wind in the Willows")_. He composed and produced the score for _[White Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Fire_(film) "White Fire (film)")_ (1984), which consisted largely of two songs performed by Limelight.

In the 1980s he was also a member of an all-star band called [Olympic Rock & Blues Circus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Rock_&_Blues_Circus?action=edit&redlink=1 "Olympic Rock & Blues Circus (page does not exist)"). Some musicians, including Lord, took part in Pete York's TV musical extravaganza _Superdrumming_ between 1987 and 1989.

## Later work, 1984–2006

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jon_Lord&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Later work, 1984–2006")\]

Lord's re-emergence with Deep Purple in 1984 resulted in huge audiences for the reformed "Mk II" line-up, including 1985's second largest grossing tour in the US and an appearance in front of 80,000 rain-soaked fans headlining [Knebworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concerts_at_Knebworth_House "Concerts at Knebworth House") on 22 June 1985, all to support the _[Perfect Strangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_Strangers_(Deep_Purple_album) "Perfect Strangers (Deep Purple album)")_ album. Playing with a rejuvenated Purple (including spells at a health farm to get the band, including Lord, into shape) and being onstage and in the studio with Blackmore, gave Lord the chance to push himself once again. His ' [rubato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubato "Rubato")' classical opening sequence to the album's opener, " [Knocking at Your Back Door](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knocking_at_Your_Back_Door "Knocking at Your Back Door")" (complete with F-Minor to G polychordal harmony sequence), gave Lord the chance to do his most powerful work for years, including the song " [Perfect Strangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_Strangers_(Deep_Purple_song) "Perfect Strangers (Deep Purple song)")". Further Deep Purple albums followed, often of varying quality, and by the late-1990s, Lord was clearly keen to explore new avenues for his musical career.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/10/Jon_sunflower_2007.JPG/250px-Jon_sunflower_2007.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Jon_sunflower_2007.JPG) Lord performing for [The Sunflower Jam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sunflower_Jam "The Sunflower Jam"), London, 2007

In 1997, he created perhaps his most personal work to date, _[Pictured Within](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pictured_Within "Pictured Within")_, released in 1998 with a European tour to support it. Lord's mother Miriam had died in August 1995 and the album is inflected at all stages by Lord's sense of grief. Recorded largely in Lord's home-away-from-home, the city of [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne "Cologne"), the album's themes are [Elgarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Elgar "Edward Elgar") and alpine in equal measure. Lord signed to [Virgin Classics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Classics "Virgin Classics") to release it, and perhaps saw it as the first stage in his eventual departure from Purple to embark on a low-key and altogether more gentle solo career. One song from _Pictured Within_, entitled "Wait A While", was later covered by [Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_people "Norwegian people") singer [Sissel Kyrkjebø](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sissel_Kyrkjeb%C3%B8 "Sissel Kyrkjebø") on her 2003/2004 album _My Heart_.

Lord finally retired from Deep Purple amicably after their UK tour in February 2002, preceded by a knee injury that eventually resolved itself without surgery and which brought keyboardist [Don Airey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Airey "Don Airey") into the band, initially as his temporary replacement. He said subsequently, "Leaving Deep Purple was just as traumatic as I had always suspected it would be and more so – if you see what I mean". He even dedicated a song to it on 2004's solo effort, _Beyond the Notes_, called "De Profundis". The album was recorded in [Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn "Bonn") with producer [Mario Argandoña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Argando%C3%B1a?action=edit&redlink=1 "Mario Argandoña (page does not exist)")\[ [es](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario%20Argando%C3%B1a "es:Mario Argandoña")\] between June and July 2004.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cb/Tony_jon1990.jpg/250px-Tony_jon1990.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Tony_jon1990.jpg) [Tony Ashton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Ashton "Tony Ashton") (right) with Lord at a gig at the Hotel Post, March 1990

_Pictured Within_ and _Beyond the Notes_ provide the most personal work by Lord. He collaborated with former [ABBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABBA "ABBA") superstar and family friend Frida ( [Anni-Frid Lyngstad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anni-Frid_Lyngstad "Anni-Frid Lyngstad")) on the 2004 track, "The Sun Will Shine Again" (with lyrics by [Sam Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Brown_(singer) "Sam Brown (singer)")), and performed with her across Europe. He subsequently also performed European concerts to première the 2007-scheduled orchestral piece _Boom of the Tingling Strings_.

In 2003 he also returned to his beloved R&B and blues heritage to record an album of standards in Sydney, with Australia's [Jimmy Barnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Barnes "Jimmy Barnes"), entitled _Live in the Basement_, by Jon Lord and the [Hoochie Coochie Men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoochie_Coochie_Men "Hoochie Coochie Men"). Lord was also happy to support the Sam Buxton Sunflower Jam Healing Trust and in September 2006, performed at a star-studded event to support the charity led by Ian Paice's wife, Jacky (twin sister of Lord's wife Vicky). Featured artists on stage with Lord included [Paul Weller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Weller_(singer) "Paul Weller (singer)"), [Robert Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Plant "Robert Plant"), [Phil Manzanera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Manzanera "Phil Manzanera"), Ian Paice and Bernie Marsden.

## Final work, after 2006

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jon_Lord&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Final work, after 2006")\]

Two Lord compositions, _[Boom of the Tingling Strings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boom_of_the_Tingling_Strings "Boom of the Tingling Strings")_ and "Disguises (Suite for String Orchestra)", were recorded in [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark "Denmark") in 2006 and released in April 2008 on [EMI Classics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMI_Classics "EMI Classics"). Both featured the [Odense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odense "Odense") orchestra, conducted by Paul Mann. Additionally, a second [Hoochie Coochie Men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoochie_Coochie_Men "Hoochie Coochie Men") album was recorded in July 2006 in London. This album, _Danger – White Men Dancing_, was released in October 2007. His _[Durham Concerto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham_Concerto "Durham Concerto")_, commissioned by [Durham University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham_University "Durham University") for its 175th anniversary celebrations, received its world premiere on 20 October 2007 in [Durham Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham_Cathedral "Durham Cathedral") by the [Royal Liverpool Philharmonic Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Liverpool_Philharmonic_Orchestra "Royal Liverpool Philharmonic Orchestra"), and featured soloists Lord on Hammond Organ, [Kathryn Tickell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathryn_Tickell "Kathryn Tickell") on [Northumbrian pipes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northumbrian_pipes "Northumbrian pipes"), Matthew Barley on cello and [Ruth Palmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Palmer "Ruth Palmer") on violin.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-19) It became a hit in [Classic FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_FM_(UK) "Classic FM (UK)")'s "Hall of Fame", alongside his piano concerto _Boom of the Tingling Strings_.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

Lord played piano on [George Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Harrison "George Harrison")'s posthumously released _[Brainwashed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brainwashed_(George_Harrison_album) "Brainwashed (George Harrison album)")_ album (2002) and became an important member of Harrison's social circle at [Friar Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friar_Park "Friar Park") in Oxfordshire (Lord by now living first at Yewden Lodge in Hambleden and then at the time of his death at Hill House, in Fawley, Henley-On-Thames), the two having first met at Abbey Road studios in the early 1970s.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-20) He was also a close friend of Sir [John Mortimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Mortimer "John Mortimer"), the English barrister, dramatist, screenwriter, author and creator of British television series _[Rumpole of the Bailey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumpole_of_the_Bailey "Rumpole of the Bailey")_, whom he had accompanied on many occasions during Mortimer's performances of "Mortimer Miscellany." In 2007, Lord joined Derek Griffiths, Colin Martin and Malcolm Pool at an Artwoods reunion at the ART Tribute night, at York House in Twickenham. Ali Mackenzie took over Art Wood's role on vocals, and Chris Hunt played drums. They were joined on stage by guitarist Ronnie Wood and vocalist Geno Washington. Lord released his solo album _[To Notice Such Things](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/To_Notice_Such_Things "To Notice Such Things")_ on 29 March 2010.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-Blabbermouth1-21) Titled after the main work — a six-movement suite for solo flute, piano and string orchestra — the album was inspired by, and was dedicated to the memory of Sir John Mortimer, who died in January 2009. On its first day of release, the album entered [Amazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_(company) "Amazon (company)")'s Movers And Shakers index, reaching No. 12 at the end of the day.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-JonLord.org1-22) Six days later it entered the UK's official classical chart at No. 4.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-autogenerated1-23) Lord had been commissioned to compose a concerto for Hammond organ and orchestra with special parts for tympani. The piece was to be premiered with the [Oslo Philharmonic Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Philharmonic_Orchestra "Oslo Philharmonic Orchestra") with Tom Vissgren on [tympani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tympani "Tympani") in Oslo, Norway in Spring 2012. With [Vladimir Ashkenazy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Ashkenazy "Vladimir Ashkenazy") and [Josef Suk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josef_Suk_(violinist) "Josef Suk (violinist)"), Lord was one of three artistic sponsors of [Toccata Classics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toccata_Classics "Toccata Classics").

Between 2008 and 2011, Lord performed at arts festivals ranging from Lichfield and Shipley in the UK to the Virada Cultural Festival in Brazil. On 1 September 2009, he opened the Fall Arts Salon at the ancient [Roman Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_theatre_of_Philippopolis "Roman theatre of Philippopolis") in [Plovdiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plovdiv "Plovdiv"), Bulgaria, performing with the [Plovdiv Philharmonic Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plovdiv_Philharmonic_Orchestra "Plovdiv Philharmonic Orchestra") under conductor [Nayden Todorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nayden_Todorov "Nayden Todorov").[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-24)[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-25) On 5 November 2009, Lord performed at the Palace Hall in [Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucharest "Bucharest"), Romania, with the [Rousse Philharmonic Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rousse_Philharmonic "Rousse Philharmonic"), again conducted by Nayden Todorov; the concert was released in 2011 as a live album and DVD, and subsequently reissued on vinyl.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-26)[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-27)[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-28) On 30 October 2010, Lord returned to Bulgaria for a concert at the [National Palace of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Palace_of_Culture "National Palace of Culture") in [Sofia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia "Sofia"), with the [Sofia Philharmonic Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia_Philharmonic_Orchestra "Sofia Philharmonic Orchestra") once again conducted by Nayden Todorov.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-29)

In July 2011, Lord performed his final live concert appearance, the Sunflower Jam at the Royal Albert Hall, where he premiered his joint composition, "It's not as big as it was", with [Rick Wakeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Wakeman "Rick Wakeman").[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-30) At that point, they had begun informal discussion on recording an album together. Up until 2011, Lord had also been working on material with recently formed rock [supergroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supergroup_(music) "Supergroup (music)") [WhoCares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WhoCares "WhoCares"), also featuring singer [Ian Gillan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Gillan "Ian Gillan") from [Deep Purple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Purple "Deep Purple"), guitarist [Tony Iommi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Iommi "Tony Iommi") from [Black Sabbath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sabbath "Black Sabbath"), second guitarist [Mikko Lindström](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikko_Lindstr%C3%B6m "Mikko Lindström") from [HIM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIM_(Finnish_band) "HIM (Finnish band)"), bassist [Jason Newsted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Newsted "Jason Newsted") formerly from [Metallica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallica "Metallica"), and drummer [Nicko McBrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicko_McBrain "Nicko McBrain") from [Iron Maiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Maiden "Iron Maiden"), specifically the composition "Out of My Mind," in addition to new compositions with [Steve Balsamo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Balsamo "Steve Balsamo") and a Hammond Organ concerto.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-31)[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-32) Lord subsequently cancelled a performance of his " [Durham Concerto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham_Concerto "Durham Concerto")" in Hagen, Germany,[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-33) for what his website said was a continuation of his medical treatment (the concert, scheduled for 6 July 2012, would have been his return to live performance after treatment).[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-34)

Lord's [Concerto for Group and Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concerto_for_Group_and_Orchestra "Concerto for Group and Orchestra") was effectively recommissioned by him, recorded in Liverpool and at [Abbey Road Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbey_Road_Studios "Abbey Road Studios") across 2011 and under post-production in 2012 with the [Liverpool Philharmonic Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_Philharmonic_Orchestra "Liverpool Philharmonic Orchestra") performing, conducted by long-time collaborator, conductor Paul Mann. The recording was at completion at the time of Lord's death, with Lord having been able to review the final master recordings. The album and DVD were subsequently released in 2012.

## Personal life

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jon_Lord&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Personal life")\]

Lord has a younger brother, Stephen. Lord's first marriage, from 1969 to 1975, was to Judith Feldman, with whom he had one daughter, Sara. Lord's second wife, Victoria Frances Gibbs (died 2025), was a former girlfriend of Deep Purple bandmate [Glenn Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Hughes_(musician) "Glenn Hughes (musician)") and twin sister of Ian Paice's wife, Jacqueline. The couple had a daughter, Amy.[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-35)

## Illness and death

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jon_Lord&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Illness and death")\]

In July 2011, Lord was diagnosed with [pancreatic cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pancreatic_cancer "Pancreatic cancer"). After treatment in both England and in Israel,[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-36) he died on 16 July 2012 at the [London Clinic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Clinic "London Clinic") following a [pulmonary embolism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonary_embolism "Pulmonary embolism").[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-37)[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-rsdeath-38)[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-BBCDeath-39)[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-40) His internment was at the new churchyard of Saint Mary the Virgin Church in [Hambleden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hambleden "Hambleden").

## Influence and legacy

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jon_Lord&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Influence and legacy")\]

[Lars Ulrich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_Ulrich "Lars Ulrich"), founding member and drummer in [Metallica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallica "Metallica") commented, "Ever since my father took me to see them in 1973 in Copenhagen, at the impressionable age of 9, Deep Purple has been the most constant, continuous and inspiring musical presence in my life. They have meant more to me than any other band in existence, and have had an enormous part in shaping who I am. We can all be guilty of lightly throwing adjectives like 'unique,' 'one-of-a-kind' and 'pioneering' around when we want to describe our heroes and the people who've moved us, but there are no more fitting words than those right now and there simply was no musician like Jon Lord in the history of hard rock. Nobody. Period. There was nobody that played like him. There was nobody that sounded like him. There was nobody that wrote like him. There was nobody that looked like him. There was nobody more articulate, gentlemanly, warm, or fucking cooler that ever played keyboards or got anywhere near a keyboard. What he did was all his own."[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-41)

Interviewed in 2012 for [Blabbermouth.net](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blabbermouth.net "Blabbermouth.net"), Motörhead frontman [Lemmy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemmy "Lemmy") said: "It's just a shame, because Jon Lord was, to a large extent, responsible for me being in rock and roll. He was in a band called the Artwoods years ago, with [Ronnie Wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronnie_Wood "Ronnie Wood")'s brother Arthur. They were sort of a jazz-blues band, I guess. They played at the place in Wales where I was living,[\[a\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-42) this dingy little boozer, and I was talking to Jon and, like an idiot, he gave me his address in London. So, of course, I went down there and he wasn't there, but he was living at Art's mother's house where Ronnie Wood who was in a band called [The Birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Birds_(band) "The Birds (band)") was living and they let me crash on the couch.... I saw him late last year in a hotel in Germany in [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne "Cologne"). He was over there doing some orchestral stuff, and we talked in the bar for awhile.... I'm glad I saw him, since he's since departed."[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-43)

Also in 2012, music critic Terence Towles Canote wrote shortly after Lord's death: "While many of his contemporaries would turn to the Moog synthesiser, Jon Lord continued to rely primarily on the Hammond organ. Of course, it must be pointed out that Jon Lord could make a Hammond organ do things that other keyboardists could only dream about. Jon Lord's playing not only stood out from the pack, it also held up over time. As a youth in the Eighties I could listen to old Deep Purple songs and they would not sound dated at all. It is an incredible achievement for any artist to create works that are essentially timeless, and that is precisely what Jon Lord did."[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-44)

Former keyboard player of the rock band [Yes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yes_(band) "Yes (band)"), [Rick Wakeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Wakeman "Rick Wakeman"), who was a friend of Lord's, said he was "a great fan" and added "We were going to write and record an album before he became ill. His contribution to music and to classic rock was immeasurable and I will miss him terribly." In mid-2013, Wakeman presented a BBC One East Midlands-produced TV programme about Lord and his connection to the town of his birth.[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-45)

Vocalist [Anni-Frid Lyngstad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anni-Frid_Lyngstad "Anni-Frid Lyngstad") of [Abba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abba "Abba") described Jon Lord as her "dearest friend" and paid him tribute at the 2013 Zermatt Unplugged music festival, an annual event for which she and Lord served as patrons. "He was graceful, intelligent, polite, with a strong integrity," she said. "(He) had a strong empathy and a great deal of humour for his own and other people's weaknesses."[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-46)

Keyboardist [Keith Emerson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson "Keith Emerson") said of Lord's death, "Jon left us now but his music and inspiration will live forever. I am deeply saddened by his departure."[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-47) In a later interview in November 2013, he added, "In the early years I remember being quite jealous of Jon Lord – may he rest in peace. In September 1969 I heard he was debuting his "Concerto For Group & Orchestra" at the [Royal Albert Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Albert_Hall "Royal Albert Hall"), with none other than [Malcolm Arnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malcolm_Arnold "Malcolm Arnold") conducting. Wow! I had to go along and see that. Jon and I ribbed each other, we were pretty much pals, but I walked away and thought: 'Shit, in a couple of weeks' time I'm going to be recording [The Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nice "The Nice")'s _[Five Bridges Suite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Bridges "Five Bridges")_ ... not at the Albert Hall but at the [Fairfield Halls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfield_Halls "Fairfield Halls"), [Croydon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croydon "Croydon")!' A much more prosaic venue. Later, Jon wanted me to play on his solo album, _[Gemini Suite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemini_Suite "Gemini Suite")_, but that was around the time [ELP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerson,_Lake_and_Palmer "Emerson, Lake and Palmer") were breaking big and we were touring. He was a lovely guy, a real gentleman."[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-48)

A tribute concert to Lord took place on 4 April 2014 at the Royal Albert Hall. Performers and presenters included [Deep Purple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Purple "Deep Purple"), [Bob Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Harris_(radio_presenter) "Bob Harris (radio presenter)"), [Paul Weller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Weller "Paul Weller"), [Bruce Dickinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Dickinson "Bruce Dickinson"), [Alfie Boe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfie_Boe "Alfie Boe"), [Jeremy Irons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Irons "Jeremy Irons"), [Rick Wakeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Wakeman "Rick Wakeman"), [Joe Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Brown_(singer) "Joe Brown (singer)"), [Glenn Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Hughes_(English_singer) "Glenn Hughes (English singer)"), [Kasia Laska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasia_Laska "Kasia Laska"), [Miller Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller_Anderson_(musician) "Miller Anderson (musician)") and [Steve Balsamo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Balsamo "Steve Balsamo"). Musical Director of the event was Paul [Wix Wickens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wix_Wickens "Wix Wickens") and the performance featured the Orion Orchestra conducted by Paul Mann.[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-49)

In December 2012 the Mayor of Leicester, Sir [Peter Soulsby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Soulsby "Peter Soulsby"), joined the campaign to honour Lord with a [blue plaque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_plaque "Blue plaque") at his childhood home at 120 Averill Road, where he lived until he was 20 years old, saying it would be "an important reminder of the city's contribution to the world of contemporary music."[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-50)

Lord was posthumously inducted into the [Rock and Roll Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_and_Roll_Hall_of_Fame "Rock and Roll Hall of Fame") as a member of Deep Purple in April 2016.[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-51)

In May 2019 Lord was posthumously awarded an [Ivor Novello Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivor_Novello_Award "Ivor Novello Award") for International Achievement along with the surviving members of the Deep Purple MK II line up, at a ceremony at the [Grosvenor House Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grosvenor_House_Hotel "Grosvenor House Hotel") in London.[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lord#cite_note-52)

The Concerto For Group and Orchestra is toured regularly with Paul Mann conducting. Most recently in March and April 2023, a European and Brazilian tour took place across 11 dates in cities including Bucharest, Sofia, Zagreb and São Paulo featuring [Bruce Dickinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Dickinson "Bruce Dickinson") performing the vocal parts. Members of Orquestra Sinfônica do Estado de São Paulo featured alongside Kaitner Z Doka on guitar, Bernhard Welz on drums, John O'Hara ( [Jethro Tull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jethro_Tull_(band) "Jethro Tull (band)")) on keyboards, Tanya O'Callaghan ( [Whitesnake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitesnake "Whitesnake")) on bass and Mario Argandoña on percussion.
