Musical artist

**Brian Albert Gordon Auger** (born 18 July 1939) is an English jazz rock and rock keyboardist who specialises in the [Hammond organ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammond_organ "Hammond organ").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Auger#cite_note-Ruhlmann-1)

Auger has worked with [Rod Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rod_Stewart "Rod Stewart"), [Tony Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Williams_(drummer) "Tony Williams (drummer)"), [Jimi Hendrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimi_Hendrix "Jimi Hendrix"),[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Auger#cite_note-2) [John McLaughlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McLaughlin_(musician) "John McLaughlin (musician)"), [Sonny Boy Williamson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonny_Boy_Williamson_II "Sonny Boy Williamson II"), [Eric Burdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Burdon "Eric Burdon"), and [CAB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAB_(band) "CAB (band)"). He has incorporated jazz, early British pop, [R&B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhythm_and_blues "Rhythm and blues"), [soul music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul_music "Soul music"), and rock into his sound. He has been nominated for a Grammy Award.

## Career

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Brian_Auger&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Career")\]

In 1965, Auger played on " [For Your Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_Your_Love "For Your Love")" by [the Yardbirds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Yardbirds "The Yardbirds") as a session musician.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Auger#cite_note-3)[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Auger#cite_note-4) He was brought in to play the Hammond organ, but settled for the only available keyboard, the harpsichord.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Auger#cite_note-5) That same year, Auger formed the group [the Steampacket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Steampacket "The Steampacket") with [Long John Baldry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_John_Baldry "Long John Baldry"), [Julie Driscoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julie_Driscoll "Julie Driscoll"), [Vic Briggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vic_Briggs "Vic Briggs"), and [Rod Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rod_Stewart "Rod Stewart").[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Auger#cite_note-6) Due to contractual problems there were no official recordings made by the band; nevertheless, nine tracks were laid down for promotional use in late 1965 and released as an LP in 1970 in France on the BYG label. They were released on a CD by Repertoire Records in 1990 (licensed from [Charly Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charly_Records "Charly Records")) as well as 12 live tracks from _Live at the Birmingham Town Hall, February 2, 1964_. Stewart left in early 1966 and soon thereafter the band broke up.

### The Trinity

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Brian_Auger&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: The Trinity")\]

With Driscoll and the band [Trinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Auger_and_the_Trinity "Brian Auger and the Trinity"), he went on to record a cover version of [David Ackles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ackles "David Ackles")' "Road to Cairo" and Bob Dylan's " [This Wheel's on Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/This_Wheel's_on_Fire_(song) "This Wheel's on Fire (song)")", which appeared on _Dylan Covered_. The latter track was a number 5 pop hit in the UK. In 1969 Auger, Driscoll, and Trinity performed in the United States on the NBC special _[33⅓ Revolutions Per Monkee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/33%E2%85%93_Revolutions_Per_Monkee "33⅓ Revolutions Per Monkee")_. Auger and Driscoll stopped performing together after the 1969 album _[Streetnoise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streetnoise "Streetnoise")_,[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Auger#cite_note-7) but reunited for the 1978 album _[Encore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encore_(1978_album) "Encore (1978 album)")_.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Auger#cite_note-8)

### Brian Auger's Oblivion Express

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Brian_Auger&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Brian Auger's Oblivion Express")\]

In 1970, he formed the [jazz fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion "Jazz fusion") ensemble Brian Auger's Oblivion Express shortly after abandoning the abortive "Wassenaar Arrangement" jazz rock commune in a small suburb of The Hague. Oblivion Express cultivated the talents of several notable musicians, including [Average White Band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Average_White_Band "Average White Band") drummers [Robbie McIntosh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_McIntosh_(drummer) "Robbie McIntosh (drummer)") and [Steve Ferrone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Ferrone "Steve Ferrone"), as well as guitarist [Jim Mullen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Mullen "Jim Mullen").\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

According to the 13 December 1970 issue of _[The Baltimore Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Baltimore_Sun "The Baltimore Sun")_, the line up comprised Brian Auger on keyboards, Jim Mullen on guitar, Barry Dean on bass, and Keith Bailey on drums. Another musician, saxophonist Alan Skidmore, was expected to join the group in the near future.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Auger#cite_note-9)

In 1971 Auger produced and appeared on [Mogul Thrash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mogul_Thrash "Mogul Thrash")'s only album, _[Mogul Thrash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mogul_Thrash_(album) "Mogul Thrash (album)")_. Two members of that band, Roger Ball and [Malcolm Duncan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malcolm_Duncan_(musician) "Malcolm Duncan (musician)"), would go on to form the [Average White Band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Average_White_Band "Average White Band").[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Auger#cite_note-10)[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Auger#cite_note-11)\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

### Maestro

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Brian_Auger&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Maestro")\]

Auger toured with [Kim Simmonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Simmonds "Kim Simmonds"), Gregg Errico, and [Tim Bogert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Bogert "Tim Bogert") in the mid-1980s in a band they called Maestro. No album resulted from this collaboration and tour. In 1986, he played keyboards for the Italian singer [Mango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mango_(singer) "Mango (singer)") on the album _[Odissea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odissea "Odissea")_.

#### Further activities

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Brian_Auger&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Further activities")\]

In 1989, Auger was musical director for the thirteen-part film retrospective series _Villa Fantastica_ made for German television. A live recording of the series, _[Super Jam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Jam_(album) "Super Jam (album)")_ (1990), features Auger on piano, [Pete York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_York "Pete York") on drums, [Dick Morrissey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Morrissey "Dick Morrissey") on tenor saxophone, Roy Williams on trombone, Harvey Weston on bass guitar, and singers [Zoot Money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoot_Money "Zoot Money") and [Maria Muldaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Muldaur "Maria Muldaur").

Auger toured with [Eric Burdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Burdon "Eric Burdon") in the early 1990s and recorded the live album _[Access All Areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Access_All_Areas_(Eric_Burdon_&_Brian_Auger_Band_album) "Access All Areas (Eric Burdon & Brian Auger Band album)")_ with him in 1993. Oblivion Express was revived in 2005 with recording and touring. The group featured Brian Auger, his son Karma Auger on drums, his daughter Savannah Auger on vocals, and Derek Frank on bass.

In 2012, Auger released _Language of the Heart_, one of the few solo albums of his career, produced by Tea. It features [Jeff "Skunk" Baxter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_%22Skunk%22_Baxter "Jeff \"Skunk\" Baxter") and [Julian Coryell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_Coryell "Julian Coryell") on guitars.

In 2014, Auger was invited by producer Gerry Gallagher to record with [El Chicano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Chicano "El Chicano") as well as [Alphonse Mouzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alphonse_Mouzon "Alphonse Mouzon"), [David Paich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Paich "David Paich"), [Alex Ligertwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Ligertwood "Alex Ligertwood"), [Ray Parker Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Parker_Jr. "Ray Parker Jr."), Lenny Castro, [Vikki Carr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikki_Carr "Vikki Carr"), [Pete Escovedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Escovedo "Pete Escovedo"), [Peter Michael Escovedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Michael_Escovedo "Peter Michael Escovedo"), [Salvador Santana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Santana "Salvador Santana"), [Jessy J](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessy_J "Jessy J"), [Marcos J. Reyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcos_J._Reyes "Marcos J. Reyes"), [Siedah Garrett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siedah_Garrett "Siedah Garrett"), [Walfredo Reyes Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walfredo_Reyes_Jr. "Walfredo Reyes Jr."), and [Spencer Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spencer_Davis "Spencer Davis"). In the same year, Brian Auger and Oblivion Express played at the KJAZZ festival in Los Angeles, and toured in Japan and Europe with Karma Auger on drums, daughter Ali Auger on vocals, [Alex Ligertwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Ligertwood "Alex Ligertwood") on vocals, Yarone Levy on guitar, Les King on bass, and Travis Carlton on bass.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Auger#cite_note-12)
