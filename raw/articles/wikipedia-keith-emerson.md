**Keith Noel Emerson** (2 November 1944–11 March 2016) was an English keyboardist, songwriter, composer and record producer. He played keyboards in a number of bands before finding his first commercial success with [the Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nice "The Nice") in the late 1960s.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-vh1-1) He became internationally famous for his work with the Nice, which included writing rock arrangements of classical music.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-lupisp5-2) After leaving the Nice in 1970, he was a founding member of [Emerson, Lake & Palmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerson,_Lake_&_Palmer "Emerson, Lake & Palmer") (ELP), one of the early [progressive rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_rock "Progressive rock") supergroups.

Emerson, Lake & Palmer were commercially successful through much of the 1970s, becoming one of the best-known progressive rock groups of the era.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-vh1-1) Emerson wrote and arranged much of ELP's music on albums such as _[Tarkus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarkus "Tarkus")_ (1971) and _[Brain Salad Surgery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain_Salad_Surgery "Brain Salad Surgery")_ (1973), combining his own original compositions with classical or traditional pieces adapted into a rock format.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-3) Following ELP's break-up at the end of the 1970s, Emerson pursued a solo career, composed several film soundtracks, and formed the bands [Emerson, Lake & Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerson,_Lake_&_Powell "Emerson, Lake & Powell")[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-vh1-1) and [3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3_(1980s_band) "3 (1980s band)") to carry on in the style of ELP.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-4) In the early 1990s, ELP reunited for two more albums and several tours before breaking up again in the late 1990s. Emerson also reunited The Nice in 2002 and 2003 for a tour.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-varietyobit-5)

During the 2000s, Emerson resumed his solo career, including touring with his own Keith Emerson Band featuring guitarist Dave Kilminster, then replaced by Marc Bonilla, and collaborating with several orchestras. He reunited with ELP bandmate [Greg Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Lake "Greg Lake") in 2010 for a duo tour, culminating in a one-off ELP reunion show in London to celebrate the band's 40th anniversary.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-6) Emerson's last album, _The Three Fates Project_, with Marc Bonilla and Terje Mikkelsen, was released in 2012.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-varietyobit-5) Emerson reportedly suffered from depression, and since 1993 developed nerve damage that hampered his playing, making him anxious about upcoming performances. He died of a self-inflicted gunshot wound on 11 March 2016 at his home in [Santa Monica, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Monica,_California "Santa Monica, California").[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-savage-7)[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-lynch-8)[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-grinberg-9)

Emerson is widely regarded as one of the greatest keyboard players of the progressive rock era.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-vh1-1)[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-10)[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-11)[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-12) [AllMusic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AllMusic "AllMusic") describes Emerson as "perhaps the greatest, most technically accomplished keyboardist in rock history".[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-Allmusic-13) In 2019, readers of _[Prog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prog_(magazine) "Prog (magazine)")_ voted him the greatest keyboard player in progressive rock.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-14)

## Early life

Emerson was born on 2 November 1944 in [Todmorden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todmorden "Todmorden"), [West Riding of Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Riding_of_Yorkshire "West Riding of Yorkshire"). The family had been evacuated from southern England during World War II, after which they returned south and settled in [Goring-by-Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goring-by-Sea "Goring-by-Sea"), West Sussex.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-hanson-15)\[ _[pageneeded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citing_sources "Wikipedia:Citing sources")_\] Emerson attended West Tarring School (now [Thomas A'Becket Middle School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_A'Becket_Middle_School,_Worthing,_West_Sussex?action=edit&redlink=1 "Thomas A'Becket Middle School, Worthing, West Sussex")) in [Tarring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarring,_West_Sussex "Tarring, West Sussex").[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-WorthingDaily-16) His mother Dorothy was not musical, but his father Noel was an amateur pianist and taught Emerson basic piano. When Emerson was eight, his parents arranged formal tuition, learning to play and read music with "local little old ladies" until he was around thirteen, with whom he studied to [ABRSM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABRSM "ABRSM") Grade 7.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-EMM83-17)[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-hollyq-18)[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-19)[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-milano-20) Emerson's teacher put him in competitions at the Worthing Music Festival and suggested he finish studying music in London, but Emerson had little interest in classical music at the time and chose jazz piano.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-EMM83-17) His studies in [Western classical music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_classical_music "Western classical music") largely inspired his own style in his professional career which often incorporated jazz and rock elements.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-varietyobit-5)

Although Emerson did not own a record player, he enjoyed listening to music on the radio, particularly [Floyd Cramer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floyd_Cramer "Floyd Cramer")'s 1961 [slip note-style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appoggiatura "Appoggiatura") " [On the Rebound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Rebound "On the Rebound")" and the work of [Dudley Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dudley_Moore "Dudley Moore"). He used jazz sheet music from [Dave Brubeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Brubeck "Dave Brubeck") and [George Shearing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Shearing "George Shearing") and learned about jazz piano from books and [Andre Previn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andre_Previn "Andre Previn")'s version of _[My Fair Lady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Fair_Lady "My Fair Lady")_.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-EMM83-17) He also listened to [boogie-woogie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boogie-woogie "Boogie-woogie"), and to [country-style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music "Country music") pianists including [Joe "Mr Piano" Henderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_%22Mr_Piano%22_Henderson "Joe \"Mr Piano\" Henderson"), [Russ Conway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russ_Conway "Russ Conway") and [Winifred Atwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winifred_Atwell "Winifred Atwell"). Emerson later described himself: "I was a very serious child. I used to walk around with [Beethoven sonatas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano_sonatas_(Beethoven) "Piano sonatas (Beethoven)") under my arm. However, I was very good at avoiding being beaten up by the bullies. That was because I could also play [Jerry Lee Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Lee_Lewis "Jerry Lee Lewis") and [Little Richard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Richard "Little Richard") songs. So, they thought I was kind of cool and left me alone."[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-hollyq-18)

Emerson became interested in the [Hammond organ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammond_organ "Hammond organ") after hearing jazz organist [Jack McDuff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_McDuff "Jack McDuff") perform "Rock Candy", and the Hammond became his instrument of choice in the late 1960s. Emerson acquired his first Hammond organ, an L-100 model, at the age of 15 or 16, on [hire purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hire_purchase "Hire purchase") and a loan from his father.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-kbrdmag-21)[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-OTT84-22) He had saved money to buy a Bird electric organ with built-in speakers on each side, but then spotted a Hammond in the shop and thought it was a better purchase.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-EMM83-17) Emerson's initial plan was for a non-musical career while playing the piano on the side. Upon leaving school he worked at [Lloyds Bank Registrars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyds_Bank "Lloyds Bank") where he played the piano in the bar at lunch times and local pubs at nights. He was ultimately fired from the bank.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-P72-23)[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-WorthingDaily-16) Emerson played in a local 20-piece swing band run by [Worthing Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worthing_Borough_Council "Worthing Borough Council"), performing [Count Basie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Count_Basie "Count Basie") and [Duke Ellington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_Ellington "Duke Ellington") tunes. This led to the formation of the Keith Emerson Trio, with the group's drummer and bassist.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-EMM83-17)

## Career

### 1965–1970: Early groups and The Nice

While performing in the Worthing and Brighton area, Emerson played in John Brown's Bodies where members of [The T-Bones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Farr#With_the_T-Bones "Gary Farr"), the backing band of blues singer [Gary Farr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Farr "Gary Farr"), offered him a place in their group.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-EMM83-17)[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-P72-23) After a subsequent UK and European tour with the T-Bones, the band split. Emerson then joined [The V.I.P.'s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_V.I.P.'s_(band) "The V.I.P.'s (band)"),
which he described as a "purist blues band";[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-EMM83-17) his noted flamboyance began when a fight broke out during a performance in France. Instructed by the band to keep playing, he produced some explosion and machine gun sounds with the Hammond organ, which stopped the fight. His band members told him to repeat the stunt at the next concert,[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-P72-23)[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-Analog_Days-24)\[ _[pageneeded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citing_sources "Wikipedia:Citing sources")_\] where Emerson played the organ back to front.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Emerson#cite_note-EMM83-17)
