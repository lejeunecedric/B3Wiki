# Eddy Louiss (French Wikipedia, translated notes + English Wikipedia)

## Biographie (fr.wikipedia, summarized)

Eddy Louiss, born Édouard Louise on 2 May 1941 in Paris (14th arrondissement), died 30 June 2015 in Poitiers. French musician: jazz organist, singer and pianist. Of Martinican ancestry; his father Pierre Louiss was a trumpeter (the family name was changed from Louise to Louiss). Eddy studied trumpet and piano, played in his father's orchestra as a teenager, and played piano in Paris clubs in the early 1960s while studying.

As a singer he was a member of Les Double Six of Paris (1961–1963), with founder Mimi Perrin, Roger Guérin, Ward Swingle and Christiane Legrand. Adding organ to his instrumental palette, Louiss played in the 1960s with Johnny Griffin, Art Taylor, Dizzy Gillespie and Stan Getz. At the end of the decade he was part of the Kenny Clarke trio with guitarist René Thomas, and also recorded with Barney Kessel. He played and recorded with Claude Nougaro (13 years, 1964–1977), Jane Birkin, Henri Salvador and many others. He formed a big band, Multicolor Feeling (later Multicolor Feeling Fanfare), touring across Europe. He recorded in duo with Michel Petrucciani (1994, Conférence de presse — Victoires du jazz 1995) and Richard Galliano (2001, Face to Face).

EN wiki adds: Prix Django Reinhardt 1964; 1971 member of the Stan Getz quartet (with René Thomas and Bernard Lubat) recording Dynasty (Verve, 1971) and Communications '72; Our Kind of Sabi (1970) with John Surman, Niels-Henning Ørsted Pedersen, Daniel Humair; split from Nougaro in 1977 for a solo career, a decision his son Pierre described as "not that easy"; left leg amputated in the early 1990s after artery problems (fr wiki: foot amputated 2006 due to diabetic arterial complications; 50th-anniversary concert at the Olympia in 2010 with the rebirth of the Multicolor Feeling Fanfare, 60+ musicians); died 30 June 2015 in Poitiers after surgery complications, aged 74.
