Musical artist

**Joey DeFrancesco** (April 10, 1971–August 25, 2022) was an American [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz "Jazz") [organist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammond_organ "Hammond organ"), trumpeter, saxophonist, and occasional singer.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-Jzzabookrevitalize-3) He released more than 30 albums under his own name and recorded extensively as a sideman with such leading jazz musicians as trumpeter [Miles Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles_Davis "Miles Davis"), saxophonist [Houston Person](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston_Person "Houston Person"), and guitarist [John McLaughlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McLaughlin_(musician) "John McLaughlin (musician)").[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-GoMembMatrued-4)

DeFrancesco signed his first record deal at the age of 16 and over the years, recorded and toured internationally with [David Sanborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sanborn "David Sanborn"), [Arturo Sandoval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arturo_Sandoval "Arturo Sandoval"), [Larry Coryell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Coryell "Larry Coryell"), [Frank Wess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Wess "Frank Wess"), [Benny Golson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benny_Golson "Benny Golson"), [James Moody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Moody_(saxophonist) "James Moody (saxophonist)"), [Steve Gadd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Gadd "Steve Gadd"), [Danny Gatton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_Gatton "Danny Gatton"), [Elvin Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvin_Jones "Elvin Jones"), [Jimmy Cobb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Cobb "Jimmy Cobb"), [George Benson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Benson "George Benson"), [Pat Martino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Martino "Pat Martino"), [Tony Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Monaco "Tony Monaco"), [John Scofield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Scofield "John Scofield"), [Lee Ritenour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Ritenour "Lee Ritenour"), [Joe Lovano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Lovano "Joe Lovano"), and had prominent session work with a variety of musicians, including [Ray Charles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Charles "Ray Charles"), [Bette Midler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bette_Midler "Bette Midler"), [Janis Siegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janis_Siegel "Janis Siegel"), [Diana Krall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diana_Krall "Diana Krall"), [Jimmy Morrison").[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-5)[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-jazzgreats-6)[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-7)

## Early life and education

Joey DeFrancesco was born in [Springfield, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springfield_Township,_Delaware_County,_Pennsylvania "Springfield Township, Delaware County, Pennsylvania"), United States,[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-azcentral-8) on April 10, 1971,[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-NYT_obit-9)[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-Chinen-10) into a musical family that included three generations of jazz musicians. He was named after his grandfather, Joseph DeFrancesco, a jazz musician who played the saxophone and clarinet.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-jazzgreats-6) His father, ["Papa" John DeFrancesco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_DeFrancesco "John DeFrancesco"), was an organist who played nationally and received the [Oklahoma Jazz Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oklahoma_Jazz_Hall_of_Fame "Oklahoma Jazz Hall of Fame")'s Living Legend Award in 2013.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-11) DeFrancesco began playing the [organ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammond_organ "Hammond organ") at the age of four and was playing songs by [Jimmy Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Smith_(musician) "Jimmy Smith (musician)") verbatim by the time he was five. His father began taking him to gigs from the age of five, letting him sit in on sets.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-PhillyFamilyHarm-12) At the age of 10, DeFrancesco joined a band in [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia "Philadelphia") that included jazz musicians [Hank Mobley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hank_Mobley "Hank Mobley") and [Philly Joe Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philly_Joe_Jones "Philly Joe Jones"). He was considered a fixture at local [jazz clubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_club "Jazz club"), opening shows for [Wynton Marsalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wynton_Marsalis "Wynton Marsalis") and [B. B. King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._B._King "B. B. King").[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-PhilyLegendTour-13)

DeFrancesco attended the [Philadelphia High School for the Creative and Performing Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_High_School_for_the_Creative_and_Performing_Arts "Philadelphia High School for the Creative and Performing Arts"). There, he studied music specializing in piano and organ.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-PhillyFamilyHarm-12) During his high school years, he won numerous awards, including the Philadelphia Jazz Society McCoy Tyner Scholarship. He was a finalist in the first [Thelonious Monk International Jazz Piano Competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbie_Hancock_Institute_of_Jazz#Herbie_Hancock_Institute_of_Jazz_International_Competition "Herbie Hancock Institute of Jazz") (now of the [Herbie Hancock Institute of Jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbie_Hancock_Institute_of_Jazz "Herbie Hancock Institute of Jazz")).[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-Jzzabookrevitalize-3)

## Career

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/58/Joey_Defrancesco_North_Sea_Jazz.jpg/500px-Joey_Defrancesco_North_Sea_Jazz.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Joey_Defrancesco_North_Sea_Jazz.jpg) DeFrancesco playing at the [North Sea Jazz Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sea_Jazz_Festival "North Sea Jazz Festival") in [Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam "Rotterdam"), 2010

### Recording

DeFrancesco was 16 years old when he signed an exclusive recording contract with [Columbia Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Records "Columbia Records"). The following year, he released his first record, titled _All of Me_. His performance on _All of Me_ has been attributed as helping bring back the [organ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammond_organ "Hammond organ") to jazz music during the 1980s.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-Jzzabookrevitalize-3) That same year, he joined [Miles Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles_Davis "Miles Davis") and his band on a five-week concert tour in Europe.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-PDNPalJoey-14) He followed up with playing keyboards on Davis's album _[Amandla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amandla_(album) "Amandla (album)")_, which reached No. 1 on the Contemporary Jazz Albums chart in 1989.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-GoMembMatrued-4) DeFrancesco started playing the trumpet around the same time, inspired by the sound of Davis.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-15) He was originally spotted by Davis during a performance on the television show called _Time Out_.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-JazzTimesPhilyFlyer-16) He was performing on the set, along with high school classmate [Christian McBride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_McBride "Christian McBride") when Davis asked the show's host, "what's your organ player's name", referring to DeFrancesco.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-JazzTimesPhilyFlyer-16) DeFrancesco's recording deal with Columbia included five albums. In addition to _All of Me_, he released _[Where Were You?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Where_Were_You%3F "Where Were You?")_ (1990), _Part III_ (1991), _Reboppin'_ (1992), and _Live at the 5 Spot_ (1993).[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-discography-17)

### Touring

DeFrancesco began touring with his own quartet at the age of 18. In the early 1990s, he began collaborating with [John McLaughlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McLaughlin_(musician) "John McLaughlin (musician)"), former guitarist for Miles Davis and the leader of [Mahavishnu Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahavishnu_Orchestra "Mahavishnu Orchestra"). At the age of 22, he became a founding member of the group _The Free Spirits_, along with McLaughlin and drummer [Dennis Chambers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Chambers "Dennis Chambers"). He toured with the group for four years and was part of several recordings, including the albums _[Tokyo Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Live_(John_McLaughlin_album) "Tokyo Live (John McLaughlin album)")_ and _[After the Rain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/After_the_Rain_(John_McLaughlin_album) "After the Rain (John McLaughlin album)")_.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-18) DeFrancesco also played trumpet on the former album.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-19)

In 1999, DeFrancesco recorded his album _Incredible! Live at the San Francisco Jazz Festival_.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-Penguin9-20) The album featured a performance by his idol [Jimmy Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Smith_(musician) "Jimmy Smith (musician)"), who joined DeFrancesco for two songs.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-Penguin9-20) In 2004, DeFrancesco recorded _Legacy_, another album that featured Jimmy Smith.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-Penguin9-20) The album was Smith's last recording; he died the same year.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-GoMembMatrued-4)

DeFrancesco was nominated for a [Grammy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Awards "Grammy Awards") in 2004 for his album _Falling in Love Again_. His career shifted slightly in 2009 with the film _[Moonlight Serenade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moonlight_Serenade_(2009_film) "Moonlight Serenade (2009 film)")_, starring [Amy Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_Adams "Amy Adams") and [Alec Newman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alec_Newman "Alec Newman"). He played the role of "Frank D" in the film and was also credited as a composer and producer of the film. DeFrancesco was nominated for another Grammy Award in 2011 in the category [Best Contemporary Jazz Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Contemporary_Jazz_Album "Grammy Award for Best Contemporary Jazz Album") for _[Never Can Say Goodbye: The Music of Michael Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Never_Can_Say_Goodbye:_The_Music_of_Michael_Jackson "Never Can Say Goodbye: The Music of Michael Jackson")_;[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-GoMembMatrued-4) the album was released in 2010 as a tribute to [Michael Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Jackson "Michael Jackson"). Some other tribute albums of his include a tribute to [Frank Sinatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Sinatra "Frank Sinatra") titled _Joey DeFrancesco Plays Sinatra His Way_ and a tribute to [Jimmy Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Smith_(musician) "Jimmy Smith (musician)"). DeFrancesco also turned 40 in 2011, celebrating by releasing _40_, his 29th recording, which had success both on jazz charts domestically and in Europe.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-GoMembMatrued-4)

### Music style

DeFrancesco's music style was referred to as a swinging Philly sound which he "embellished with his own ferocity and improvisation."[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-JazzTimesPhilyFlyer-16) He played 200-plus nights a year throughout the course of his career, a feat that he cut back on as of 2013.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-ElsewhereNZ-21) He received numerous accolades for his performances, including being called the best [Hammond B3 organ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammond_organ "Hammond organ") player on the planet by _[JazzTimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JazzTimes "JazzTimes")_.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-22) _[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")_ described DeFrancesco as a "deeply authoritative musician, a master of rhythmic pocket, and of the custom of stomping bass lines beneath chords and riffs."[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-NYorkTimesAcc-1) The _[Chicago Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Tribune "Chicago Tribune")_ praised the musicianship of DeFrancesco, stating that "He dominated the instrument and the field as no one of his generation has."[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-Joey_DeFrancesco_-_About-23) DeFrancesco was also involved in musical instrument development, especially product designs and endorsements related to technological advancements in [digital keyboards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_keyboard "Electronic keyboard") and electronic organ both in the United States and internationally.[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-24)

### Multi-instrumentalist

As a multi-instrumentalist, DeFrancesco recorded on various [keyboards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keyboard_instrument "Keyboard instrument") (including acoustic and electric piano), and trumpet. Although best known as a jazz organist, he also performed as a singer and, since November 2018, played the [tenor saxophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenor_saxophone "Tenor saxophone").[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-NYT_obit-9)[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-Chinen-10)

## Awards and honors

DeFrancesco was a four time [Grammy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Awards "Grammy Awards")-nominee, with more than 30 recordings as a leader. In addition to Grammy nominations in 2004, 2010, and 2020, DeFrancesco was a 9-time winner of the _[DownBeat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DownBeat "DownBeat")_ Critics' Poll (for organ) and won the _DownBeat_ Readers Poll every year since 2005. He won a number of _[JazzTimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JazzTimes "JazzTimes")_ awards as well.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-25) DeFrancesco was an inaugural member of the [Hammond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammond_organ "Hammond organ") Hall of Fame, inducted in 2013 along with [Brian Auger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Auger "Brian Auger"), [Billy Preston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Preston "Billy Preston"), [Steve Winwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Winwood "Steve Winwood"), and his mentor, [Jimmy Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Smith_(musician) "Jimmy Smith (musician)").[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-26)

## Discography

Main article: [Joey DeFrancesco discography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco_discography "Joey DeFrancesco discography")

DeFrancesco's discography consists of albums released on the labels [Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Records "Columbia Records"), [Muse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muse_Records "Muse Records"), [Highnote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HighNote_Records "HighNote Records"), Big Mo, [Concord Jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concord_Jazz "Concord Jazz"), Doodlin', and [Mack Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mack_Avenue_Records "Mack Avenue Records").[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-PrestigeMuse-2)

## Personal life

In addition to his musician father and grandfather, DeFrancesco's brother, [Johnny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_DeFrancesco "Johnny DeFrancesco"), is a [blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blues "Blues") guitarist.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-PDNPalJoey-14)

DeFrancesco was married to his second wife, Gloria, until his death. She served as his manager.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-NYT_obit-9)[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-Masley-27) DeFrancesco had one daughter with his first wife, Ashley Blue DeFrancesco.

## Death

DeFrancesco died of a [heart attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myocardial_infarction "Myocardial infarction") on August 25, 2022, at the age of 51.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-NYT_obit-9)[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-Chinen-10)[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-Masley-27) The following week, at a concert in [Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston "Boston"), [Van Morrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Morrison "Van Morrison") paid tribute to his one-time collaborator, dedicating " [You're Driving Me Crazy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You're_Driving_Me_Crazy "You're Driving Me Crazy")" to him.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_DeFrancesco#cite_note-28)
