Musical artist

**Booker Taliaferro Jones Jr.**[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones#cite_note-bare-1) (born November 12, 1944) is an American musician, songwriter, record producer and arranger, best known as the frontman of the band [Booker T. & the M.G.'s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._&_the_M.G.'s "Booker T. & the M.G.'s"). He has also worked as a [session musician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Session_musician "Session musician") with many well-known artists of the 20th and 21st centuries, earning him a [Grammy Award for Lifetime Achievement.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Lifetime_Achievement_Award "Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award")[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones#cite_note-2)

With the death of guitarist [Steve Cropper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Cropper "Steve Cropper") on December 3, 2025, Jones is the last surviving original member of Booker T. & the M.G.’s.

## Background

Jones was born in [Memphis, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memphis,_Tennessee "Memphis, Tennessee"), on November 12, 1944. He was named after his father, Booker T. Jones Sr., who was named in honor of [Booker T. Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Washington "Booker T. Washington"), the educator. Jones Sr. was a science teacher at Memphis High School, providing the family with a relatively stable, lower middle-class lifestyle.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBowman199736-3)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/04/Booker_T_Jones_childhood_home_666_Edith_Ave_Memphis_TN_01.jpg/500px-Booker_T_Jones_childhood_home_666_Edith_Ave_Memphis_TN_01.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Booker_T_Jones_childhood_home_666_Edith_Ave_Memphis_TN_01.jpg) Booker T. Jones's childhood home on Edith Avenue in Memphis, Tennessee

Jones was musically a [child prodigy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_prodigy "Child prodigy"), playing the [oboe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oboe "Oboe"), [saxophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saxophone "Saxophone"), [trombone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trombone "Trombone"), [double bass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_bass "Double bass"), and piano at school and organ at church. Jones attended [Booker T. Washington High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Washington_High_School_(Memphis,_Tennessee) "Booker T. Washington High School (Memphis, Tennessee)"), the [alma mater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alma_mater "Alma mater") of [Rufus Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rufus_Thomas "Rufus Thomas"), and collaborated with future stars like [Isaac Hayes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Hayes "Isaac Hayes")'s writing partner [David Porter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Porter_(musician) "David Porter (musician)"), saxophonist [Andrew Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Love_(musician) "Andrew Love (musician)") of [the Memphis Horns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Memphis_Horns "The Memphis Horns"), soul singer/songwriter [William Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Bell_(singer) "William Bell (singer)"), and [Earth, Wind & Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth,_Wind_&_Fire "Earth, Wind & Fire")'s singer/songwriter [Maurice White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_White "Maurice White"). \[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

## Career

### 1960s

Jones's entry into professional music came at the age of 16, when he played [baritone saxophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baritone_saxophone "Baritone saxophone") on Satellite (soon to be [Stax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stax_Records "Stax Records")) Records' first hit, "Cause I Love You", by [Carla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carla_Thomas "Carla Thomas") and [Rufus Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rufus_Thomas "Rufus Thomas"). [Willie Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willie_Mitchell_(musician) "Willie Mitchell (musician)") hired Jones for his band, in which Jones started on sax and later moved to bass. It was here that he met [Al Jackson Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jackson_Jr. "Al Jackson Jr."), whom he brought to Stax.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBowman199737-4) Simultaneously, Jones formed a combo with [Maurice White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_White "Maurice White") and [David Porter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Porter_(musician) "David Porter (musician)"), in which he played guitar.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBowman199736-3)

While hanging around the Satellite Record Shop run by [Estelle Axton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estelle_Axton "Estelle Axton"), co-owner of Satellite Records with her brother [Jim Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Stewart_(record_producer) "Jim Stewart (record producer)"), Jones met record clerk [Steve Cropper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Cropper "Steve Cropper"), who would become one of the MGs when the group formed in 1962. Besides Jones on organ and Cropper on guitar, Booker T. and the MGs featured [Lewie Steinberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewie_Steinberg "Lewie Steinberg") on bass guitar (eventually replaced by [Donald "Duck" Dunn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_%22Duck%22_Dunn "Donald \"Duck\" Dunn")), and Al Jackson Jr. on drums. While still in high school, Jones co-wrote the group's classic [instrumental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrumental "Instrumental") " [Green Onions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Onions "Green Onions")", which was a massive hit in 1962.

Bob Altshuler wrote the sleeve notes on the first Booker T. & the M.G.'s album _[Green Onions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Onions "Green Onions")_ released by Stax Records in 1962:

\[His\] musical talents became apparent at a very early age. By the time he entered high school, Booker was already a semi-professional, and quickly recognized as the most talented musician in his school. He was appointed director of the school band for four years, and in addition, organized the school dance orchestra which played for proms throughout the Mid-South. In the classroom, he concentrated on the studies of music theory and harmony. ... Booker's multiple activities earned him a coveted honour, that of being listed in the students' "Who's Who of American High Schools." Booker's first instrument was the string bass, but he soon switched to the organ. Booker came to the attention of record executive Jim Stewart in Memphis, and while still in high school he worked as a staff musician for Stax Records, appearing as sideman on many recording dates for that label. It became obvious that one day Booker would be ready to record under his own name and several months later Booker's first recording session was set.

Over the next few years, Jones divided his time between studying classical music composition, composing and transposition at [Indiana University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University_Bloomington "Indiana University Bloomington"), playing with the MGs on the weekends back in Memphis,[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones#cite_note-5) serving as a [session musician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Session_musician "Session musician") with other Stax acts, and writing songs that became widely regarded as classics. He wrote, with [Eddie Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Floyd "Eddie Floyd"), " [I've Never Found a Girl (To Love Me Like You Do)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I've_Never_Found_a_Girl_(To_Love_Me_Like_You_Do) "I've Never Found a Girl (To Love Me Like You Do)")", [Otis Redding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otis_Redding "Otis Redding")'s "I Love You More Than Words Can Say", and, with William Bell, bluesman [Albert King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_King "Albert King")'s " [Born Under a Bad Sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Born_Under_a_Bad_Sign_(song) "Born Under a Bad Sign (song)")" (later popularized by the British rock group [Cream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cream_(band) "Cream (band)")).

### 1970s

In 1970, Jones moved to California and stopped playing sessions for Stax after becoming frustrated with Stax's treatment of the MGs as employees rather than musicians. Even though Jones was given the title of Vice President at Stax before leaving, as he put it, "There were titles given (to us) but we didn't actually make the decisions."[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBowman1997186-6) While still under contract to Stax, he appeared on [Stephen Stills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Stills "Stephen Stills")'s eponymous album (1970). The 1971 album _[Melting Pot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melting_Pot_(Booker_T_album) "Melting Pot (Booker T album)")_ would be the last [Booker T. & the M.G.'s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._&_the_M.G.'s "Booker T. & the M.G.'s") album issued on Stax.

He produced [Bill Withers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Withers "Bill Withers")'s 1971 debut album _[Just as I Am](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_as_I_Am_(Bill_Withers_album) "Just as I Am (Bill Withers album)")_ (on which Jones played guitar as well as keyboards), [Rita Coolidge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rita_Coolidge "Rita Coolidge")'s album _[Love Me Again](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_Me_Again_(album) "Love Me Again (album)")_ (1978) and [Willie Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willie_Nelson "Willie Nelson")'s album _[Stardust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stardust_(Willie_Nelson_album) "Stardust (Willie Nelson album)")_ (1978). Jones has also added his keyboard playing to artists ranging from the R&B/pop/blues of [Ray Charles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Charles "Ray Charles") to the folk rock/country rock of [Neil Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Young "Neil Young").\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

### 1980s

#### _The Best of You_

Jones released his _[Best of You](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Best_of_You_(album) "The Best of You (album)")_ album in 1980. It was his third solo release. It contained the songs, " You Got Me Spinnin'", "The Best Of You", "Cookie", " Pride and Joy", " Down To the Wire", " [Stand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand!_(song) "Stand! (song)")", " We Could Stay Together", and "Will You Be the One".[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones#cite_note-7) It was given a positive review in the 3 May issue of _[Record World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Record_World "Record World")_.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones#cite_note-8) It had another review by _Record World_ on the 17th of that month. The reviewer wrote that with the instrumental hits that Booker T. & The MGs had in the 1960s, who would have thought he possessed such a wonderful singing voice? And the advice to the reader was to discover this exceptional talent.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones#cite_note-9)

The song " [The Best of You](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Best_of_You "The Best of You")" was backed with "Let's Go Dancin'" and released as a single on A&M 2234-S in June 1980.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones#cite_note-10) It was a _Record World_ Single Pick for the week of 21 June. The reviewer wrote that Jones was in rare form with the song and his voice was shimmering alongside the prominent guitar. The strong crossover potential of the song was also noted.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones#cite_note-11)

"We Could Stay Together" which featured Jones and Rita Coolridge was backed with "The Best of You" and released as a single in Australia on A&M K-8030 in September.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones#cite_note-12) It was a chart hit there, peaking at No. 60.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones#cite_note-13) The song would later appear in re-mix form on the VA album, _Hot Classics, Issue 6_ that was released on Hot Tracks – HC-6 in 1987.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones#cite_note-14)[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones#cite_note-15)

A single, "You Got Me Spinning" bw "The Best of You" was released in New Zealand on A&M K 8946 in 1982.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones#cite_note-16) It was a hit there, spending a week in the chart, peaking at No. 44.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones#cite_note-17)

Another single, "Cookie" bw "Will You Be the One" was released on A&M 2279-S in October.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones#cite_note-18) It was one of the _Billboard_ Top Single Picks for the week of 18 October.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones#cite_note-19) It also had a positive review in the 25 October issue of _Record World_.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones#cite_note-20)

#### Further activities

Jones released the single, "I Want You" bw "You're the Best". Credited to Booker T., it was released on A&M 2374-S in September 1981.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones#cite_note-21) He made the charts that year with the song. It debuted at No. 65 in the _Record World_ Black Oriented Singles chart for the week of 7 November.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones#cite_note-22)

### 1990s–2020s

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/78/Booker_T_at_Petaluma_festival.jpg/500px-Booker_T_at_Petaluma_festival.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Booker_T_at_Petaluma_festival.jpg) Booker T. playing organ at the Petaluma Wine, Jazz and Blues Festival, August 2009

In 2009 he released a new solo album, _[Potato Hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potato_Hole "Potato Hole")_, recorded with the [Drive-By Truckers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drive-By_Truckers "Drive-By Truckers"),[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones#cite_note-23) and featuring [Neil Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Young "Neil Young"). He performed at the [Bonnaroo Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonnaroo_Music_Festival "Bonnaroo Music Festival") with Drive-By Truckers on June 6, 2009, with a set including most tracks from _Potato Hole_ as well as some Truckers tracks. On January 31, 2010, _Potato Hole_ [won the Best Instrumental Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/52nd_Annual_Grammy_Awards#Pop "52nd Annual Grammy Awards") award at the [52nd Annual Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/52nd_Annual_Grammy_Awards "52nd Annual Grammy Awards").

He is featured on the [Rancid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancid_(band) "Rancid (band)") album _[Let the Dominoes Fall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Let_the_Dominoes_Fall "Let the Dominoes Fall")_ (2009), playing a [Hammond B-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammond_organ "Hammond organ") on the track "Up to No Good".

Jones also played his B-3 on the track "If It Wasn't For Bad" from the [Elton John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elton_John "Elton John") and [Leon Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Russell "Leon Russell") 2010 collaboration album titled _[The Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Union_(Elton_John_and_Leon_Russell_album) "The Union (Elton John and Leon Russell album)")_. The track was nominated at the 53rd Annual Grammy Awards for Best Pop Collaboration with Vocals. \[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

In 2011, Jones released _[The Road from Memphis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Road_from_Memphis "The Road from Memphis")_. The backing band included [Questlove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Questlove "Questlove") (drums), ["Captain" Kirk Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_Kirk_Douglas "Captain Kirk Douglas") (guitar) and [Owen Biddle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_Biddle_(musician) "Owen Biddle (musician)") (bass) from [the Roots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Roots "The Roots") as well as former [Motown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motown "Motown") guitarist [Dennis Coffey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Coffey "Dennis Coffey") and percussionist Stewart Killen. The album features vocals by [Yim Yames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_James "Jim James"), [Matt Berninger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Berninger "Matt Berninger"), [Lou Reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Reed "Lou Reed"), [Sharon Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Jones "Sharon Jones") and Booker T. himself, as well as lyrics contributed by his daughter/manager Liv Jones. Jones also recorded with party band [the Gypsy Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gypsy_Queens "The Gypsy Queens") on their eponymous album.[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones#cite_note-24)

On February 12, 2012, _The Road from Memphis_ won at the [54th Annual Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/54th_Annual_Grammy_Awards "54th Annual Grammy Awards") for [Best Pop Instrumental Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/54th_Annual_Grammy_Awards#Pop "54th Annual Grammy Awards"). Jones holds a total of four [Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award "Grammy Award").

Jones received an honorary doctorate degree from Indiana University's [Jacobs School of Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobs_School_of_Music "Jacobs School of Music") at its 2012 undergraduate commencement. Jones originally attended Indiana University in the 1960s, even staying after his smash-hit [Stax Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stax_Records "Stax Records") recordings.

Jones was featured on organ for singer [Kelly Hogan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_Hogan "Kelly Hogan") on Hogan's 2013 release on Anti-Records, _I Like to Keep Myself in Pain_.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7c/Booker_T_%40_Kitchener_Bluesfest_HR-23.jpg/500px-Booker_T_%40_Kitchener_Bluesfest_HR-23.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Booker_T_@_Kitchener_Bluesfest_HR-23.jpg) Booker T. playing guitar at Kitchener Bluesfest in 2013

In June 2013, Jones released his 10th album, _[Sound The Alarm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_the_Alarm_(Booker_T._Jones_album) "Sound the Alarm (Booker T. Jones album)")_, on Stax Records after originally leaving the label more than 40 years previously in 1971. The album features guest artists [Anthony Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Hamilton_(musician) "Anthony Hamilton (musician)"), [Raphael Saadiq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raphael_Saadiq "Raphael Saadiq"), Jay James, [Mayer Hawthorne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayer_Hawthorne "Mayer Hawthorne"), [Estelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estelle_(musician) "Estelle (musician)"), [Vintage Trouble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vintage_Trouble "Vintage Trouble"), [Gary Clark Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Clark_Jr. "Gary Clark Jr."), [Luke James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luke_James_(singer) "Luke James (singer)"), and Booker's son Ted Jones.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones#cite_note-25) That summer, he performed at the TD Kitchener Blues Festival in [Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario "Ontario").

On September 1, 2017, Jones performed live at the Royal Albert Hall [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC") [Proms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Proms "The Proms") with [Jools Holland and his Rhythm & Blues Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jools_Holland's_Rhythm_and_Blues_Orchestra "Jools Holland's Rhythm and Blues Orchestra") in a tribute concert honoring the 50th anniversary of Stax Records alongside [Steve Cropper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Cropper "Steve Cropper"), [Sam Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Moore "Sam Moore"), [William Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Bell_(singer) "William Bell (singer)") and British artists [Beverley Knight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beverley_Knight "Beverley Knight"), [Ruby Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruby_Turner "Ruby Turner"), [James Morrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Morrison_(singer) "James Morrison (singer)") and [Tom Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Jones_(singer) "Tom Jones (singer)").

## Personal life

Jones was married to [Priscilla Coolidge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priscilla_Coolidge "Priscilla Coolidge") in 1969, sister of singer [Rita Coolidge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rita_Coolidge "Rita Coolidge"). He produced Priscilla's first album _Gypsy Queen_ in 1970; then the pair collaborated as a duo on three albums: 1971's _Booker T. & Priscilla_, 1972's _Home Grown_, and 1973's _Chronicles_, and Jones produced Priscilla's final solo album, _Flying_, in 1979, right as their marriage ended that year.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones#cite_note-26)

## Legacy

On March 1, 1995, Booker T. & the MGs won their first Grammy Award for [Best Pop Instrumental Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Awards_of_1995#Pop "Grammy Awards of 1995") for the song "Cruisin'". Jones still plays with the MGs and his own small combo called the Booker T. Jones Band. His current touring group includes Vernon "Ice" Black (guitar), Darian Gray (drums), and Melvin Brannon (bass).

Jones was inducted into [The Rock and Roll Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rock_and_Roll_Hall_of_Fame "The Rock and Roll Hall of Fame") in 1992, and was honored with a [Grammy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award "Grammy Award") for Lifetime Achievement on February 11, 2007.[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones#cite_note-27)

In 2007, Jones was also inducted into the [Musicians Hall of Fame and Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musicians_Hall_of_Fame_and_Museum "Musicians Hall of Fame and Museum") in [Nashville, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville,_Tennessee "Nashville, Tennessee").

On October 29, 2019, his memoir _Time Is Tight: My Life, Note by Note_ was released.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones#cite_note-28)[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones#cite_note-29)[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones#cite_note-30) The memoir was mentioned on [NPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPR "NPR")'s _[Fresh Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresh_Air "Fresh Air")_ on October 25, 2019.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones#cite_note-31)
