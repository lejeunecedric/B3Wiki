Musical artist

**Charles Earland** (May 24, 1941 – December 11, 1999) was an American jazz organist and pianist.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Earland#cite_note-AMG-1)

## Biography

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Charles_Earland&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Biography")\]

Earland was born in [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia "Philadelphia") and learned to play the saxophone in high school.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Earland#cite_note-AMG-1) He played [tenor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenor_saxophone "Tenor saxophone") with [Jimmy McGriff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_McGriff "Jimmy McGriff") at the age of 17 and in 1960 formed his first group. He started playing the organ after playing with [Pat Martino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Martino "Pat Martino"), and joined [Lou Donaldson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Donaldson "Lou Donaldson")'s band from 1968 to 1969.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Earland#cite_note-AMG-1)

The group that he led from 1970, including [Grover Washington, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grover_Washington,_Jr. "Grover Washington, Jr."), was successful, and he eventually started playing soprano saxophone and synthesizer. His hard, simmering grooves earned him the nickname "The Mighty Burner".

In 1978, Earland hit the disco/club scene with a track recorded on [Mercury Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_Records "Mercury Records") called "Let The Music Play", written by Randy Muller from the funk group [Brass Construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brass_Construction "Brass Construction"). The record was in the U.S. charts for five weeks and reached number 46 in the [UK Singles Chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Singles_Chart "UK Singles Chart").[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Earland#cite_note-British_Hit_Singles_&_Albums-2) With Earland's playing on [synthesizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthesizer "Synthesizer"), the track also has an uncredited female [vocalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singing "Singing"). He had several moderate _Billboard_ R&B chart hits in the mid-1970s and early 1980s on Mercury and later [Columbia Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Records "Columbia Records").

Earland traveled extensively from 1988 until his death in 1999, performing throughout the U.S. and abroad. One of the highlights of his latter years was playing at the [Berlin Jazz Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Jazz_Festival "Berlin Jazz Festival") in 1994. Among the musicians that performed with him at the Berlin Jazz Festival was the [Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alabama "Alabama")-born [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago") resident, Zimbabu Hamilton on the drums.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Earland#cite_note-3)

Earland died in [Kansas City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City,_Missouri "Kansas City, Missouri"), [Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri "Missouri"), of heart failure at the age of 58.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Earland#cite_note-AMG-1)
