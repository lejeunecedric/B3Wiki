_**Santana**_ is the debut studio album by American [Latin rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_rock "Latin rock") band [Santana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santana_(band) "Santana (band)"), released on August 22, 1969, by [Columbia Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Records "Columbia Records"). Over half of the album's length is composed of instrumental music, recorded by what was originally a purely free-form [jam band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jam_band "Jam band"). At the suggestion of manager [Bill Graham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Graham_(promoter) "Bill Graham (promoter)"), the band took to writing more conventional songs for more impact, but managed to retain the essence of [improvisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Improvisation "Improvisation") in the music.

The album was destined to be a major release, given a boost by the band's performance at the [Woodstock Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodstock_Festival "Woodstock Festival") just 6 days before its release to the public. The album's first single, " [Jingo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin-go-lo-ba "Jin-go-lo-ba")", was only a modest performer, spending eight weeks on the chart and reaching #56;[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santana_(1969_album)#cite_note-Santana-3) however, " [Evil Ways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evil_Ways_(Santana_song) "Evil Ways (Santana song)")", the second single taken from the album, was a [U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States") Top 10 hit, reaching #9 and spending thirteen weeks on the chart.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santana_(1969_album)#cite_note-Santana-3) The album spent more than two years (108 weeks) on the [_Billboard_ Top LPs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200 "Billboard 200") chart and peaked at #4 in November 1969.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santana_(1969_album)#cite_note-billboard-4) It also reached #34 on the [UK Albums Chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Albums_Chart "UK Albums Chart"). It has been mixed and released in both stereo and [quadraphonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadraphonic_sound "Quadraphonic sound"). The album cover features artwork by [Lee Conklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Conklin "Lee Conklin").

Professional ratings

In a contemporary review for _[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")_, [Langdon Winner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langdon_Winner "Langdon Winner") panned _Santana_ as "a masterpiece of hollow techniques" and "a speed freak's delight - fast, pounding, frantic music with no real content". He compared the music's effect to [methedrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methedrine "Methedrine"), which "gives a high with no meaning", finding Rolie and Santana's playing repetitively unimaginative, amidst a monotony of incompetent rhythms and inconsequential lyrics.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santana_(1969_album)#cite_note-11) _[Village Voice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village_Voice "Village Voice")_ critic [Robert Christgau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Christgau "Robert Christgau") shared Winner's sentiment in his "unreconstructed opposition to the methedrine school of American music. A lot of noise".[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santana_(1969_album)#cite_note-VV-10)

A retrospective _[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")_ review was more enthusiastic, finding _Santana_ "thrilling ... with ambition, soul and absolute conviction - every moment played straight from the heart".[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santana_(1969_album)#cite_note-RS2000-8) In 2003, the magazine ranked _Santana_ number 150 on their list of [the 500 greatest albums of all time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone's_500_Greatest_Albums_of_All_Time "Rolling Stone's 500 Greatest Albums of All Time"),[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santana_(1969_album)#cite_note-12) moving up to 149 in a 2012 revised list.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santana_(1969_album)#cite_note-13) [Colin Larkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Larkin_(writer) "Colin Larkin (writer)") deemed it an excellent example of [Latin rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_rock "Latin rock") in his _[Encyclopedia of Popular Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclopedia_of_Popular_Music "Encyclopedia of Popular Music")_ (2011).[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santana_(1969_album)#cite_note-Larkin-6)
