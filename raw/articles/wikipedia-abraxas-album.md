_**Abraxas**_ is the second studio album by the American [rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_music "Rock music") band [Santana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santana_(band) "Santana (band)"). It was released on September 23, 1970, by [Columbia Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Records "Columbia Records") and became the band's first album to top the [_Billboard_ 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200 "Billboard 200") in the United States.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraxas_(album)#cite_note-brill-1) In 2020, _[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")_ magazine ranked the album number 334 on its list of the " [500 Greatest Albums of All Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone's_500_Greatest_Albums_of_All_Time "Rolling Stone's 500 Greatest Albums of All Time")".[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraxas_(album)#cite_note-2)

## Title

The title of the album originates from a line in [Hermann Hesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Hesse "Hermann Hesse")'s 1919 book _[Demian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demian "Demian")_, quoted on the album's back cover: "We stood before it and began to freeze inside from the exertion. We questioned the painting, berated it, made love to it, prayed to it: We called it mother, called it whore and slut, called it our beloved, called it [Abraxas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraxas "Abraxas")..."[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraxas_(album)#cite_note-reiff-3)

## Songs

Carlos Santana had been interested in [Fleetwood Mac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleetwood_Mac "Fleetwood Mac")'s leader and songwriter [Peter Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Green_(musician) "Peter Green (musician)"), having seen him perform at the [Fillmore West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fillmore_West "Fillmore West") in [San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco "San Francisco"), and decided to cover the band's song " [Black Magic Woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Magic_Woman "Black Magic Woman")" (both had also been influenced as guitarists by [B.B. King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.B._King "B.B. King")). The band added a cover of [Gábor Szabó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A1bor_Szab%C3%B3 "Gábor Szabó")'s instrumental "Gypsy Queen" to the end.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraxas_(album)#cite_note-weinstein-4):34–35 " [Oye Como Va](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oye_Como_Va "Oye Como Va")" was a hit by [Tito Puente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tito_Puente "Tito Puente") in the early 1960s and the group played it live regularly, as they realized it was good for audiences to dance to.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraxas_(album)#cite_note-weinstein-4):35

" [Incident at Neshabur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incident_at_Neshabur "Incident at Neshabur")" was co-written by Santana and his friend Alberto Gianquinto, who played piano on the track. [Gregg Rolie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregg_Rolie "Gregg Rolie") played the other keyboards, contrasting with Gianquinto's jazz-influenced style. It ran through various time and key signatures.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraxas_(album)#cite_note-weinstein-4):36

The instrumental, " [Samba Pa Ti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samba_Pa_Ti_(instrumental) "Samba Pa Ti (instrumental)")" ("Samba for You"), was written by Santana when he saw a jazz saxophonist performing in the street outside his apartment.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraxas_(album)#cite_note-reiff-3)[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraxas_(album)#cite_note-reiff-3):[par. 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraxas_(album)#P1C)[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraxas_(album)#cite_note-weinstein-4):36 It was later covered by [José Feliciano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Feliciano "José Feliciano"), who added lyrics, and also by [Angélique Kidjo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ang%C3%A9lique_Kidjo "Angélique Kidjo"), who put lyrics in [Yoruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_language "Yoruba language"), on her album _Oyo_. It is also one of the tracks featured in [Nick Hornby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Hornby "Nick Hornby")'s book _[31 Songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/31_Songs "31 Songs")_.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraxas_(album)#cite_note-5)

## Cover art

The album cover features the 1961 painting _Annunciation_ by German-French painter [Mati Klarwein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mati_Klarwein "Mati Klarwein").[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraxas_(album)#cite_note-schnabel-6) According to the artist, it was one of the first paintings he did after relocating to [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City"). Carlos Santana reportedly noticed it in a magazine and asked that it be on the cover of the band's upcoming album.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraxas_(album)#cite_note-gallery-7) On the back of the record sleeve the cover art is just credited to 'MATI'. It is now considered a classic of rock album covers.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraxas_(album)#cite_note-schnabel-6)[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraxas_(album)#cite_note-schott-8)[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraxas_(album)#cite_note-lynch-9) Klarwein went on to design album artwork for many notable artists, including [Miles Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles_Davis "Miles Davis"), [Herbie Hancock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbie_Hancock "Herbie Hancock"), [Gregg Allman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregg_Allman "Gregg Allman"), and [Earth, Wind & Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth,_Wind_&_Fire "Earth, Wind & Fire").

Professional ratings

In 2003, the album was ranked No. 205 on _[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")_ magazine's list of [the 500 greatest albums of all time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone's_500_Greatest_Albums_of_All_Time "Rolling Stone's 500 Greatest Albums of All Time"),[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraxas_(album)#cite_note-RS500_2003-15) at No. 207 on the 2012 revision of the list,[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraxas_(album)#cite_note-RS500_2012-16) and then again at No. 334 in its 2020 release.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraxas_(album)#cite_note-RS500_2020-17) In 2000, it appeared at No. 202 in [Colin Larkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Larkin "Colin Larkin")'s _[All Time Top 1000 Albums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Time_Top_1000_Albums "All Time Top 1000 Albums")_.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraxas_(album)#cite_note-larkin_att1ka-18) The album was also included in the book _[1001 Albums You Must Hear Before You Die](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1001_Albums_You_Must_Hear_Before_You_Die "1001 Albums You Must Hear Before You Die")_.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraxas_(album)#cite_note-dimery-19) In 2015, the album was listed among _Billboard_'s 50 Essential Latin Albums of the 50 Past Years.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraxas_(album)#cite_note-billboard_latin-20)

Rock critic [Robert Christgau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Christgau "Robert Christgau"), in one of his capsule reviews in _[The Village Voice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Village_Voice "The Village Voice")_, at the time of the album's release, gave it a rating of only C+, which denotes "a not disreputable performance, most likely a failed experiment or a pleasant piece of hackwork".[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraxas_(album)#cite_note-CG-11)
