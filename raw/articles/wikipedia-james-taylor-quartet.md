# James Taylor Quartet (Wikipedia)

Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Taylor_Quartet (retrieved 2026-10-20)

The James Taylor Quartet (or JTQ) are a British four-piece jazz funk band formed in 1985 in Rochester, Kent, England, by Hammond organ player James Taylor following the break-up of his former band the Prisoners, and in the wake of Stiff Records' bankruptcy. The band consists of James Taylor (organ), Mark Cox (guitar), Andrew McKinney (bass), and Pat Illingworth (drums). Recordings and live performances often include vocalist Yvonne Yanney. Genres: acid jazz, jazz funk. Years active: 1985–present. Labels: Bruton, Polydor, Real Self, Ubiquity Records, Acid Jazz.

## Film theme beginnings
The band's first single, "Blow-Up" (a funked-up version of Herbie Hancock's main theme from the 1960s film), was released in 1987 on the Re Elect the President label, which would later become the Acid Jazz label. The track was championed by the NME and John Peel, appearing in Peel's Festive Fifty chart for 1987. The debut seven-track mini album, Mission Impossible (1987), predominantly comprised covers of 1960s film themes ("Alfie", "Mrs. Robinson", "Goldfinger") in a rough, up-tempo, almost punk-like style, focused primarily on Taylor's Hammond organ playing. Their second album, The Money Spyder (1987), was the soundtrack to an imaginary spy film. Their signature tune "The Theme from Starsky and Hutch" (1988), featuring Fred Wesley and Pee Wee Ellis of the JBs, was included on Wait a Minute (1988). Live album Absolute – JTQ Live (1991).

## Emergence of acid jazz
In the early 1990s the band changed direction and released a string of song-based albums for the fashionable soul and acid jazz scene in the UK, featuring vocalists such as Rose Windross (Soul II Soul), Alison Limerick and Noel McKoy. "Love the Life" reached the UK top 40; Supernatural Feeling (1993) reached the top 30. In the Hand of the Inevitable (1995) saw a return to the Acid Jazz label, where it remains the label's biggest selling album.

## Return to funk roots
Since then JTQ have returned to instrumental Hammond-led jazz funk. Covers ("Whole Lotta Love", "Dirty Harry", "Jesus Christ Superstar") are still recorded in the spirit of "Blow-Up", but albums are mainly original compositions. Music of Black Origin nomination for Whole Lotta Live (1998).

## Collaborations and other projects
Contributed to the soundtrack of the first Austin Powers film; collaborated with Tom Jones on Reload; featured on records by the Wonder Stuff, Manic Street Preachers, the Pogues, Kingmaker and U2. House band on Gaby Roslin's Channel 4 chat show (1996). From the late 1990s, Taylor composed library music for Bruton Music. Three albums under the name New Jersey Kings. 2005 performances with augmented horn section as the James Taylor Funk Orchestra; Nigel Price (guitar) replaced David Taylor in 2005. In 2018 they premiered Soundtrack from Electric Black with a full orchestra at Cadogan Hall (EFG London Jazz Festival).

Library: Retro Acid Jazz (Bruton, 1995) etc. Compilations include Hammond-Ology: The Best of the James Taylor Quartet (Sanctuary, 2001).

Past members include John Willmott, Graeme Flowers, Paul Carr, Chris Montague, David Taylor, Dominic Glover, Noel McKoy, Andrew McGuinness, Wolf Howard, Allan Crockford, Neil Robinson, Gary Crockett, Steve White. Website: jtq.co.uk.
