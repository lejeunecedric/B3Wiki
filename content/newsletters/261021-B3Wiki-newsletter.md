## New releases this week

Friday is Colemine day. Three records out October 23 from the Loveland, Ohio
label, and this column takes its Hammond where it can find it.

**Surprise Chef — Frogs In A Pond** (LP, out Friday) — Melbourne's
instrumental outfit are back with another LP of warm organ-led funk. If
you've worn out their earlier records, this is more of the same in the best
sense: the B-3 sits in the mix like an old armchair.
([Colemine](https://www.coleminerecords.com/))

**The Charities — Movin' On / I'm Still Learning How** (7", out Friday) —
two new sides of horn-backed soul from the Californian ensemble. Play the
A-side for the room, flip it for the ballad.
([Colemine](https://www.coleminerecords.com/))

**Adebowale and the Pioneers — Kilofese** (out Friday) — afrobeat from the
Chicago drummer, on Colemine's friends-of-the-label shelf. Worth headphones
on first listen; the percussion is layered deep.
([Colemine](https://www.coleminerecords.com/))

## Special

**This issue's special: Stax Records** (label)

**Stax Records** is the Memphis soul label founded by **Jim Stewart** in **1957** as Satellite Records, renamed **Stax** in September 1961; with its sister label Volt it defined the Southern soul sound out of a converted movie theater on McLemore Avenue. Stax's house band, **Booker T. & the M.G.'s**, was one of the first racially integrated groups of session musicians in the South, and its keyboard chair — Booker T. Jones on the Hammond B-3 and piano — turned the church-organ voice into the engine of hit instrumental R&B. "Green Onions" (1962) is the single most famous Hammond-led hit of the 1960s and a textbook of restraint: drawbar-set groove, Leslie speed swells, and space.
