# Hammond B-3 vs Combo Organs (Vox Continental, Farfisa)

The 1960s combo organs answered a different brief than the B-3: **portable, cheap, loud enough for a touring rock band** — not a church console. Wikipedia describes the Vox Continental (1962–1971) explicitly as "designed for touring musicians and as an alternative to the heavy Hammond organ." Farfisa's Combo Compact (1964) was built in direct response to the Continental.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-clonewheel-organ.md]

Web sources: [Vox Continental (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vox_Continental), [Farfisa (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farfisa)

## Dimensions of comparison

| Dimension | Hammond B-3 | Vox Continental (1962–71) | Farfisa Compact / FAST (1964–71) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sound generation | Electromagnetic [[tonewheel-generator|tonewheels]] (91 wheels, synchronous motor) | 12 top-octave transistor oscillators + frequency dividers | Transistor divider circuits — germanium in the Compact series, more stable **silicon** in the FAST series (1968–71) |
| Tone | Warm, full, complex; harmonically rich with crosstalk/leakage character | Sharper, thinner, reedy "garage rock" bark | Brighter and thinner than Hammond; distinctive biting transistor voice |
| Keyboards | Two 61-note manuals + 25-note pedalboard | Single manual (49 reverse-colored keys) or dual-manual Continental II | Single 4-octave manual (early Compacts have no bass section) |
| Drawbars | 9 per manual, full footage range 16'–1' | 6 metered sliders (16', 8', 4', IV mixture + voice bars) | Rocker tabs/boosters ("Multi-Tone Booster"), not true drawbars |
| Effects | [[vibrato-chorus|Vibrato/chorus]], [[harmonic-percussion|harmonic percussion]], key click | Single-speed single-intensity vibrato only (rate set by an internal pot) | Booster/overdrive-style effects; some later models add percussion-like sounds |
| Weight | ~425 lb (193 kg) with bench and pedalboard ^[raw/articles/wikipedia-clonewheel-organ.md] | Light, one-man carry; chrome Z-leg stand | Light; built for the van, not the truck |
| Price | $2,745 new (1955); a serious investment | ~$995 (early 1960s) | ~$495 for a Mini Compact (1966) — far cheaper than both rivals |
| Typical players/repertoire | [[jimmy-smith|Jimmy Smith]], jazz organ trio, gospel, blues | The Beatles, The Animals, The Doors, Iron Butterfly, Elvis Costello, Madness | Sam the Sham, Pink Floyd (early), Sly Stone, The B-52's, Suicide |

## Verdict / synthesis

These are **rivals in logistics, not in sound**. A combo organ cannot produce the Hammond's thick additive tonewheel mass, scanner vibrato, or pedalboard bass — nothing on a Continental or Farfisa replaces the left-hand-bass-plus-Leslie formula of an organ trio. What they offer instead is freedom: a band can carry, afford and amplify them anywhere, which is why the combo sound defines 1960s garage/beat and new wave while the B-3 defines jazz, soul and gospel. Nord's later C1 keyboard (2007) bundled Hammond, Vox and Farfisa emulations side by side precisely because players treat them as three distinct vintage colors rather than good/bad copies of one another.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-clonewheel-organ.md]
