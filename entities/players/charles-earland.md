# Charles Earland (1941–1999)

**Charles Earland** (May 24, 1941, Philadelphia – December 11, 1999, Kansas City, Missouri), American jazz organist and pianist nicknamed **"The Mighty Burner"** for his hard, simmering grooves. A first-generation disciple of [[jimmy-smith|Jimmy Smith]]'s soul-jazz school, he came to the organ from the saxophone chair.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-charles-earland.md]

## From saxophone to organ
- Learned saxophone in high school; played **tenor sax with [[jimmy-mcgriff|Jimmy McGriff]]** at age 17, and formed his first group in 1960.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-charles-earland.md]
- Switched to the organ after playing with guitarist Pat Martino, then joined **Lou Donaldson's band (1968–69)** — the Blue Note soul-jazz records *Say It Loud!* (1968), *Hot Dog* (1969) and *Everything I Play Is Funky* (1970) that broke him as an organist.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-charles-earland.md]
- Hammond Organ Co.'s own history lists him among the sixties soul-jazz legion — "Charles 'The Burner' Earland" — swinging behind [[jimmy-smith|Jimmy Smith]] as Top Cat.^[raw/articles/hammondorganco-hammond-story.md]

## Style and career
- Led his own group from 1970 (with Grover Washington, Jr. among the sidemen); the Prestige run — *Black Talk!* (1970), *Black Drops*, *Living Black!*, *Soul Story*, *Intensity*, *Live at the Lighthouse* — is the core of his organ legacy.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-charles-earland.md]
- Later moved toward electric keyboards: the disco hit **"Let The Music Play"** (Mercury, 1978, UK #46) was built on his synthesizer playing, alongside mid-70s/early-80s Billboard R&B chart entries.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-charles-earland.md]
- Toured relentlessly from 1988 until his death of heart failure at 58; a late highlight was the Berlin Jazz Festival in 1994.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-charles-earland.md]
- He is part of the soul-jazz legion filed in [[queries/b3-beyond-jimmy-smith|Who carried the B-3 beyond Jimmy Smith?]] — one of the Smith disciples (with [[richard-groove-holmes|"Groove" Holmes]], [[jack-mcduff|Jack McDuff]], [[don-patterson|Don Patterson]]) who made the Hammond the lead voice of 1960s soul jazz.^[raw/articles/vinyldiscovery-organ-jazz-soul-jazz.md]
