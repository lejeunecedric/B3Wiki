# Charles Earland (1941–1999)

**Charles Earland** (May 24, 1941, Philadelphia – December 11, 1999, Kansas City, Missouri), American jazz organist and pianist nicknamed **"The Mighty Burner"** for his hard, simmering grooves. A first-generation disciple of [Jimmy Smith](/entities/players/jimmy-smith.md)'s soul-jazz school, he came to the organ from the saxophone chair.[^wikipedia-charles-earland]

## From saxophone to organ
- Learned saxophone in high school; played **tenor sax with [Jimmy McGriff](/entities/players/jimmy-mcgriff.md)** at age 17, and formed his first group in 1960.[^wikipedia-charles-earland]
- Switched to the organ after playing with guitarist Pat Martino, then joined **Lou Donaldson's band (1968–69)** — the Blue Note soul-jazz records *Say It Loud!* (1968), *Hot Dog* (1969) and *Everything I Play Is Funky* (1970) that broke him as an organist.[^wikipedia-charles-earland]
- Hammond Organ Co.'s own history lists him among the sixties soul-jazz legion — "Charles 'The Burner' Earland" — swinging behind [Jimmy Smith](/entities/players/jimmy-smith.md) as Top Cat.[^hammondorganco-hammond-story]

## Style and career
- Led his own group from 1970 (with Grover Washington, Jr. among the sidemen); the Prestige run — *Black Talk!* (1970), *Black Drops*, *Living Black!*, *Soul Story*, *Intensity*, *Live at the Lighthouse* — is the core of his organ legacy.[^wikipedia-charles-earland]
- Later moved toward electric keyboards: the disco hit **"Let The Music Play"** (Mercury, 1978, UK #46) was built on his synthesizer playing, alongside mid-70s/early-80s Billboard R&B chart entries.[^wikipedia-charles-earland]
- Toured relentlessly from 1988 until his death of heart failure at 58; a late highlight was the Berlin Jazz Festival in 1994.[^wikipedia-charles-earland]
- He is part of the soul-jazz legion filed in [Who carried the B-3 beyond Jimmy Smith?](/queries/b3-beyond-jimmy-smith.md) — one of the Smith disciples (with ["Groove" Holmes](/entities/players/richard-groove-holmes.md), [Jack McDuff](/entities/players/jack-mcduff.md), [Don Patterson](/entities/players/don-patterson.md)) who made the Hammond the lead voice of 1960s soul jazz.[^vinyldiscovery-organ-jazz-soul-jazz]
