# Eddy Louiss (1941–2015)

French jazz organist, singer and pianist — born Édouard Louise in Paris on 2 May 1941 (the family name was changed from Louise to Louiss by his father Pierre, a trumpeter), died in Poitiers on 30 June 2015. Of Martinican ancestry, he is one of the great European voices on the [Hammond organ](/entities/instruments/hammond-b3.md): a fixture of French jazz who was, as Le Monde put it, "*Tout le swing du monde dans l'orgue Hammond*"[^wikipedia-eddy-louiss] — the beloved French counterpart to the American soul-jazz school of [Jimmy Smith](/entities/players/jimmy-smith.md) and [Jimmy McGriff](/entities/players/jimmy-mcgriff.md).

## Career
- 1950s–60s: studied trumpet and piano; played in his father's orchestra and in Paris clubs. As a singer, a member of **Les Double Six of Paris** (1961–1963) with Mimi Perrin, Ward Swingle and Christiane Legrand; during this period the organ became his primary instrument.[^wikipedia-eddy-louiss]
- **1964**: Prix Django Reinhardt.[^wikipedia-eddy-louiss]
- **1964–1977**: 13 years as accompanist to **Claude Nougaro** — the partnership that made him a household name in France — before leaving (a decision his son described as "not that easy") for a solo career.[^wikipedia-eddy-louiss]
- Jazz collaborations: Kenny Clarke, René Thomas, Johnny Griffin, Art Taylor, Dizzy Gillespie, Barney Kessel, Jean-Luc Ponty, John Surman, Stéphane Grappelli. In 1971 he was in the **Stan Getz quartet** (with René Thomas and Bernard Lubat) recording *Dynasty* (Verve, 1971).[^wikipedia-eddy-louiss]
- Led his own big band, **Multicolor Feeling / Multicolor Feeling Fanfare** (1989–), touring Europe; celebrated 50 years of career at the Olympia in 2010 with a reborn 60-musician Fanfare.[^wikipedia-eddy-louiss]
- Duo recordings with **Michel Petrucciani** (*Conférence de presse*, 1994 — Victoires du jazz 1995) and accordionist **Richard Galliano** (*Face to Face*, 2001). Later work (*Sang mêlé*, *Wébé*, *Récit proche*) mixed jazz with rock, world music and electronics.[^wikipedia-eddy-louiss]
- Health struggles: arterial complications of diabetes led to an amputation (en-Wiki: left leg, early 1990s; fr-Wiki: left foot, 2006 — see contradiction note), after which he appeared rarely; a 2009 boxed set (*D'un jour à l'autre*) marked his return.[^wikipedia-eddy-louiss]

## Contradictions
- **Amputation date/extent:** en-Wikipedia says left leg amputated in the early 1990s; fr-Wikipedia says amputated of the left foot in 2006 (Jazz in Marciac, August 2006) with a long rehabilitation. Flagged for review; the French source is more detailed and dated.

## Signature organ albums
- *Eddy Louiss orgue* vols. 1 & 2 (America, 1972) with Kenny Clarke, Jimmy Gourley, Guy Pedersen — pure Hammond trio dates
- *Bohemia After Dark* (1973)
- *Sang mêlé* (1987), *Multicolor Feeling Fanfare* (1989)

Like [Brian Auger](/entities/players/brian-auger.md), Louiss shows how thoroughly the Smith-rooted organ style took root in continental Europe in the 1960s — while [Jon Lord](/entities/players/jon-lord.md) carried it into British rock.
