# Joey DeFrancesco (1971–2022)

American jazz organist, trumpeter, saxophonist and occasional singer; born 10 April 1971 in Springfield, Pennsylvania, died 25 August 2022 in Phoenix, Arizona. Third generation of a jazz family — his father "Papa" John DeFrancesco was a working organist.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-joey-defrancesco.md]

## Career
- Playing [[hammond-b3|Hammond]] organ from age four; playing [[jimmy-smith|Jimmy Smith]] solos verbatim at five; fixture of the Philadelphia club scene by ten.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-joey-defrancesco.md]
- Signed to Columbia at 16; his debut _All of Me_ is credited with helping bring the organ back into jazz in the late 1980s.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-joey-defrancesco.md]
- Toured Europe with **Miles Davis** at 17 (keyboards on _Amandla_, 1989); founding member of The Free Spirits with John McLaughlin and Dennis Chambers at 22.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-joey-defrancesco.md]
- Recorded with his idol Jimmy Smith: _Incredible! Live at the San Francisco Jazz Festival_ (1999) and _Legacy_ (2004, Smith's last recording).^[raw/articles/wikipedia-joey-defrancesco.md]
- 30+ albums as leader, four Grammy nominations, 9-time _DownBeat_ Critics' Poll winner; inaugural inductee (2013) of the Hammond Hall of Fame alongside [[brian-auger|Brian Auger]], Billy Preston, Steve Winwood and Jimmy Smith.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-joey-defrancesco.md]

## Organs: tonewheel and New B-3
DeFrancesco played vintage tonewheel instruments and was also deeply involved in Hammond-Suzuki's modern digital instruments as a product consultant and endorser — his album with Tony Monaco was literally titled _A New Generation: Paesanos on the New B3_ (2003), and he endorsed the New B-3 line (see [[clonewheel-organ]]).^[raw/articles/wikipedia-joey-defrancesco.md] Jon Lord's heavily modified Deep Purple Hammond organ was later acquired by DeFrancesco (Wikipedia's Hammond organ article, citing Vail, _The Hammond Organ_: 186; confidence: medium).^[raw/articles/wikipedia-hammond-organ.md]

_JazzTimes_ called him "the best Hammond B3 organ player on the planet"; the _Chicago Tribune_ wrote that he "dominated the instrument and the field as no one of his generation has."^[raw/articles/wikipedia-joey-defrancesco.md]
