# Booker T. Jones (b. 1944)

**Booker Taliaferro Jones Jr.** (born 12 November 1944, Memphis, Tennessee) — musician, songwriter, producer and arranger, frontman of **Booker T. & the M.G.'s**, the house band of Stax Records. Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award. Since Steve Cropper's death in December 2025, the last surviving original M.G.[1]

## Career
- A musical child prodigy (oboe, sax, trombone, double bass, piano, church organ); entered professional music at 16 playing baritone sax on Satellite/Stax's first hit, "Cause I Love You" (Carla & Rufus Thomas).[1]
- Met Steve Cropper at Estelle Axton's Satellite Record Shop; Booker T. & the M.G.'s formed 1962 (Jones on organ, Cropper guitar, Lewie Steinberg/then Duck Dunn bass, Al Jackson Jr. drums).[1]
- Still in high school, co-wrote the band's instrumental **"Green Onions"** (1962) — a massive hit built on his organ riff, one of the most famous [Hammond](/entities/instruments/hammond-b3.md)-adjacent recordings in R&B.[1]
- Worked as Stax staff musician and arranger across the label's catalogue; later career spans Atlantic, A&M, Anti- and MCA.[1]

## Why he matters to this wiki
Jones is the bridge figure between the church-organ tradition and rock & roll: his organ playing took the instrument out of the gospel/jazz contexts of [Wild Bill Davis](/entities/players/wild-bill-davis.md) and [Jimmy Smith](/entities/players/jimmy-smith.md) and made it a lead voice on pop and soul radio, paving the road every rock organist from [Jon Lord](/entities/players/jon-lord.md) to [John Medeski](/entities/players/john-medeski.md) travelled. His style leans on the organ's bass-register attack rather than Smith's bebop pyrotechnics — the drawbar recipe documented on [Drawbars & Additive Synthesis](/concepts/drawbars.md) in its most economical form.
