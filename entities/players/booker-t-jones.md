# Booker T. Jones (b. 1944)

**Booker Taliaferro Jones Jr.** (born 12 November 1944, Memphis, Tennessee) — musician, songwriter, producer and arranger, frontman of **Booker T. & the M.G.'s**, the house band of Stax Records. Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award. Since Steve Cropper's death in December 2025, the last surviving original M.G.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-booker-t-jones.md]

## Career
- A musical child prodigy (oboe, sax, trombone, double bass, piano, church organ); entered professional music at 16 playing baritone sax on Satellite/Stax's first hit, "Cause I Love You" (Carla & Rufus Thomas).^[raw/articles/wikipedia-booker-t-jones.md]
- Met Steve Cropper at Estelle Axton's Satellite Record Shop; Booker T. & the M.G.'s formed 1962 (Jones on organ, Cropper guitar, Lewie Steinberg/then Duck Dunn bass, Al Jackson Jr. drums).^[raw/articles/wikipedia-booker-t-jones.md]
- Still in high school, co-wrote the band's instrumental **"Green Onions"** (1962) — a massive hit built on his organ riff, one of the most famous [[hammond-b3|Hammond]]-adjacent recordings in R&B.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-booker-t-jones.md]
- Worked as Stax staff musician and arranger across the label's catalogue; later career spans Atlantic, A&M, Anti- and MCA.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-booker-t-jones.md]

## Why he matters to this wiki
Jones is the bridge figure between the church-organ tradition and rock & roll: his organ playing took the instrument out of the gospel/jazz contexts of [[wild-bill-davis|Wild Bill Davis]] and [[jimmy-smith|Jimmy Smith]] and made it a lead voice on pop and soul radio, paving the road every rock organist from [[jon-lord|Jon Lord]] to [[john-medeski|John Medeski]] travelled. His style leans on the organ's bass-register attack rather than Smith's bebop pyrotechnics — the drawbar recipe documented on [[drawbars]] in its most economical form.
