# "Brother" Jack McDuff

**"Brother" Jack McDuff**, American soul-jazz organist (1926–2001, dates pending a dedicated source). A pivotal figure of the soul-jazz organ tradition: he switched from piano to Hammond in the 1950s and toured regularly throughout the 1960s and 1970s.[1]

## Why he matters
- The player who turned a young [Keith Emerson](/entities/players/keith-emerson.md) on to the Hammond with "Rock Candy" — Emerson's own origin story names McDuff as the trigger.[2]
- "Rock Candy" also appears among the jazz-blues organ recordings that shaped [Jon Lord](/entities/players/jon-lord.md).[3]
- Listed by Hammond Organ Co. among the sixties soul-jazz legion (with [Jimmy McGriff](/entities/players/jimmy-mcgriff.md), Charles "The Burner" Earland, Don Patterson, "Groove" Holmes, [Dr. Lonnie Smith](/entities/players/dr-lonnie-smith.md)) that swung with ferocious fervor behind [Jimmy Smith](/entities/players/jimmy-smith.md) as Top Cat.[4]
- Among the Jimmy Smith disciples named by the Vinyldiscovery soul-jazz survey; a Blue Note/Prestige staple whose records (e.g. _Ain't No Sunshine: Live in Seattle_) remain collector favorites.[5]
- Listed among the great jazz organists in the [Leslie](/entities/instruments/leslie-speaker.md) story — his name is inseparable from the classic Hammond + [Leslie](/entities/instruments/leslie-speaker.md) rig.[6]
- His band's guitarist [Dr. Lonnie Smith](/entities/players/dr-lonnie-smith.md) met young George Benson — McDuff's band was a proving ground for future stars.[7]
