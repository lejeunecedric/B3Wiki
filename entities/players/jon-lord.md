# Jon Lord (1941–2012)

**John Douglas "Jon" Lord** (9 June 1941, Leicester – 16 July 2012, London), English keyboardist and composer, co-founder (1968) of Deep Purple. His [Hammond](/entities/instruments/hammond-b3.md) playing during the band's hard-rock period was essential to their signature heavy sound and contributed to the early development of heavy metal; he pioneered fusing rock with classical/baroque forms.[^wikipedia-jon-lord]

## Background
- Classical piano from age five; Bach and Edward Elgar were constant reference points in his improvisation and arranging.[^wikipedia-jon-lord]
- Equally shaped by American jazz-blues organists — [Wild Bill Davis](/entities/players/wild-bill-davis.md), [Jimmy Smith](/entities/players/jimmy-smith.md), [Jimmy McGriff](/entities/players/jimmy-mcgriff.md) ("All About My Girl"), and [Jack McDuff](/entities/players/jack-mcduff.md) ("Rock Candy") — and by the classic [B-3/C-3](/entities/instruments/hammond-b3.md) + [Leslie](/entities/instruments/leslie-speaker.md) combination of 1950s–60s R&B organists. Also mentored early on by British organ pioneer Graham Bond, and impressed by Vanilla Fudge in 1967.[^wikipedia-jon-lord]

## Career milestones
- Early 1960s: London sessions (keyboard credit — disputed — on the Kinks' "You Really Got Me"), the Artwoods (organ-led blues-R&B), Santa Barbara Machine Head.[^wikipedia-jon-lord]
- 1968: co-founded Deep Purple; with drummer Ian Paice, the only continuous member 1968–1976 and 1984 until his retirement in February 2002.[^wikipedia-jon-lord]
- _Concerto for Group and Orchestra_ (1969) — the classical/rock fusion that first dented the UK charts for the band; key rhythm parts freed Ritchie Blackmore live and on record. Organ solos on "Highway Star", "Pictures of Home", "Lazy"; rhythmic underpinning on "Smoke on the Water" and "Space Truckin'".[^wikipedia-jon-lord]
- Discography highlights: _Deep Purple in Rock_ (1970), _Fireball_ (1971), _Machine Head_ (1972), _Burn_ (1974) — see [landmark Hammond albums query](/queries/hammond-landmark-albums.md).
- Gear: his 1973 replacement [Hammond C-3](/entities/instruments/hammond-b3.md) (bought from Christine McVie of Fleetwood Mac) was combined with an RMI Electra-Piano by technician Mike Phillips; he kept that C-3 until 2002, then passed it to successor Don Airey.[^wikipedia-jon-lord]
- After Deep Purple: Whitesnake, Paice Ashton Lord; later a composer of orchestral works (_Durham Concerto_, a reworked _Concerto for Group and Orchestra_, a Hammond organ concerto).[^wikipedia-jon-lord]

## Honors
- Honorary Fellow, Stevenson College Edinburgh (2010); honorary Doctor of Music, University of Leicester (2011); posthumously inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame with Deep Purple (8 April 2016).[^wikipedia-jon-lord]
