# Jon Lord (1941–2012)

**John Douglas "Jon" Lord** (9 June 1941, Leicester – 16 July 2012, London), English keyboardist and composer, co-founder (1968) of Deep Purple. His [[hammond-b3|Hammond]] playing during the band's hard-rock period was essential to their signature heavy sound and contributed to the early development of heavy metal; he pioneered fusing rock with classical/baroque forms.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-jon-lord.md]

## Background
- Classical piano from age five; Bach and Edward Elgar were constant reference points in his improvisation and arranging.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-jon-lord.md]
- Equally shaped by American jazz-blues organists — [[wild-bill-davis|Wild Bill Davis]], [[jimmy-smith|Jimmy Smith]], [[jimmy-mcgriff|Jimmy McGriff]] ("All About My Girl"), Jack McDuff ("Rock Candy") — and by the classic [[hammond-b3|B-3/C-3]] + [[leslie-speaker|Leslie]] combination of 1950s–60s R&B organists. Also mentored early on by British organ pioneer Graham Bond, and impressed by Vanilla Fudge in 1967.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-jon-lord.md]

## Career milestones
- Early 1960s: London sessions (keyboard credit — disputed — on the Kinks' "You Really Got Me"), the Artwoods (organ-led blues-R&B), Santa Barbara Machine Head.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-jon-lord.md]
- 1968: co-founded Deep Purple; with drummer Ian Paice, the only continuous member 1968–1976 and 1984 until his retirement in February 2002.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-jon-lord.md]
- _Concerto for Group and Orchestra_ (1969) — the classical/rock fusion that first dented the UK charts for the band; key rhythm parts freed Ritchie Blackmore live and on record. Organ solos on "Highway Star", "Pictures of Home", "Lazy"; rhythmic underpinning on "Smoke on the Water" and "Space Truckin'".^[raw/articles/wikipedia-jon-lord.md]
- Gear: his 1973 replacement [[hammond-b3|Hammond C-3]] (bought from Christine McVie of Fleetwood Mac) was combined with an RMI Electra-Piano by technician Mike Phillips; he kept that C-3 until 2002, then passed it to successor Don Airey.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-jon-lord.md]
- After Deep Purple: Whitesnake, Paice Ashton Lord; later a composer of orchestral works (_Durham Concerto_, a reworked _Concerto for Group and Orchestra_, a Hammond organ concerto).^[raw/articles/wikipedia-jon-lord.md]

## Honors
- Honorary Fellow, Stevenson College Edinburgh (2010); honorary Doctor of Music, University of Leicester (2011); posthumously inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame with Deep Purple (8 April 2016).^[raw/articles/wikipedia-jon-lord.md]
