# John Medeski (b. 1965)

**Anthony John Medeski** (born 28 June 1965, Louisville, Kentucky) is an American jazz keyboardist and composer, veteran of New York's 1990s avant-garde jazz scene and best known as a member of **Medeski Martin & Wood** (MMW, formed New York 1991 with Billy Martin and Chris Wood).[1]

## The Hammond in his toolkit
Medeski plays an unusually eclectic array of keyboards — acoustic piano (a Steinway Artist), melodica, mellotron, clavinet, Wurlitzer, Moog Voyager, Vox Continental — but the [Hammond B-3](/entities/instruments/hammond-b3.md) organ is the anchor voice of MMW's groove-based records and of his side projects.[1] His organ work spans:

- **MMW** — steady international touring through funk, jazz and jam scenes since the mid-1990s; albums on Gramavision and Blue Note (_Shack-man_, _Combustication_, _The Dropper_).
- **With John Scofield** — _A Go Go_ (1998) and the Medeski Scofield Martin & Wood albums (_Out Louder_, 2006; _Juice_, 2014).
- **The Word** (2000) — a blues-gospel project with Robert Randolph and the Dickinson brothers, putting the B-3 back in sacred-adjacent territory.
- **John Medeski & The Itch** (2006, with Eric Krasno and Adam Deitch) — billed explicitly around his B-3 playing.
- Session work: Morphine's _The Night_ (1999), the Dirty Dozen Brass Band's _Buck Jump_ (1999), the Bob Dylan biopic soundtrack _I'm Not There_ (2006), Coheed and Cambria's _The Afterman_ albums (2012).[1]

## Why he matters to this wiki
Medeski is the clearest case of the [B-3](/entities/instruments/hammond-b3.md) surviving inside the post-[1990s jam/acid-jazz](/concepts/clonewheel-organ.md) economy: a working organist whose sound is built on vintage drawbar language (see [Drawbars & Additive Synthesis](/concepts/drawbars.md)) yet whose scene grew up alongside digital emulations. He bridges the soul-jazz lineage documented in [Jimmy Smith](/entities/players/jimmy-smith.md) and the revival infrastructure of [Hammondbeat Records](/entities/labels/hammondbeat.md).
