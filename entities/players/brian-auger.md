# Brian Auger (b. 1939)

**Brian Albert Gordon Auger** (born 18 July 1939, Hammersmith, London), English jazz-rock keyboardist specializing in the [[hammond-b3|Hammond organ]]. Incorporates jazz, early British pop, R&B, soul and rock; Grammy-nominated.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-brian-auger.md] _Source article carries a Wikipedia BLP citation-needed banner — details below lean on its single source._

## Career milestones
- 1965: session on the Yardbirds' "For Your Love" — brought in to play Hammond but settled for the only available keyboard, a harpsichord.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-brian-auger.md]
- 1965: formed the Steampacket with Long John Baldry, Julie Driscoll, Vic Briggs and Rod Stewart — no official recordings at the time (contractual problems), though nine promotional tracks surfaced in 1970 on BYG.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-brian-auger.md]
- With Julie Driscoll and Trinity: UK No. 5 hit with Dylan's "This Wheel's on Fire"; NBC's _33⅓ Revolutions Per Monkee_ (1969); _Streetnoise_ (1969). Reunited with Driscoll (Julie Tippetts) for _Encore_ (1978).^[raw/articles/wikipedia-brian-auger.md]
- 1970: formed jazz-fusion band **Brian Auger's Oblivion Express**, which cultivated future Average White Band drummers Robbie McIntosh and Steve Ferrone and guitarist Jim Mullen.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-brian-auger.md]
- Later: Maestro tour (mid-1980s), keyboards for Italian singer Mango (_Odissea_, 1986), musical director of German TV series _Villa Fantastica_ (_Super Jam_, 1990), toured/recorded with Eric Burdon (_Access All Areas_, 1993).^[raw/articles/wikipedia-brian-auger.md]
- 2005: revived Oblivion Express with son Karma Auger (drums) and daughter Savannah Auger (vocals); still touring and recording into the 2010s–2020s (_Language of the Heart_, 2012).^[raw/articles/wikipedia-brian-auger.md]
- Has worked with Rod Stewart, Tony Williams, Jimi Hendrix, John McLaughlin, Sonny Boy Williamson II, and Eric Burdon.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-brian-auger.md]
