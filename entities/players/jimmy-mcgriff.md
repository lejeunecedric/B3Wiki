# Jimmy McGriff (1936–2008)

**James Harrell McGriff** (April 3, 1936 – May 24, 2008), American hard bop and soul-jazz organist and organ-trio bandleader from Philadelphia. One of the most recorded disciples of [[jimmy-smith|Jimmy Smith]], with a blues/gospel/"fatback groove" sound distinct from Smith's bebop virtuosity.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-jimmy-mcgriff.md]

## Early years
- Born in Germantown, Philadelphia; played piano from age five, later vibes, alto sax, drums and upright bass. Served as a military policeman in the Korean War, then two years as a Philadelphia police officer.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-jimmy-mcgriff.md]
- Drawn to the organ after hearing Richard "Groove" Holmes play at his sister's wedding; Holmes became his teacher and friend (they recorded together for Groove Merchant in 1973). Childhood friend and neighbor of [[jimmy-smith|Jimmy Smith]]; the two played together once in 1967.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-jimmy-mcgriff.md]
- Bought his first [[hammond-b3|Hammond B-3]] in 1956, studied at Juilliard, and privately with Milt Buckner, [[jimmy-smith|Jimmy Smith]], and Sonny Gatewood.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-jimmy-mcgriff.md]

## Career milestones
- 1961–62: instrumental cover of Ray Charles' "I've Got a Woman" on Jell Records, picked up by Sue Records after local airplay; the follow-up album produced the hit "All About My Girl".^[raw/articles/wikipedia-jimmy-mcgriff.md]
- 1962–65: series of Sue albums ending with _Blues for Mister Jimmy_.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-jimmy-mcgriff.md]
- 1966 onward: star attraction of producer Sonny Lester's Solid State label — big band (_The Big Band_, 1966), organ-and-blues-band albums (_Honey_, _A Thing to Come By_), funk (_Electric Funk_, 1970), pop covers, and singles like "The Worm" and "Step One".^[raw/articles/wikipedia-jimmy-mcgriff.md]
- Settled in Newark, NJ; ran his own supper club, The Golden Slipper (recorded _Black Pearl_ and _Chicken Fried Soul_ there). Performed regularly with Buddy Rich's band from 1969.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-jimmy-mcgriff.md]
- His Philadelphia combo featured tenor saxophonist [[charles-earland|Charles Earland]], who soon switched to organ and became a renowned organist in his own right.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-jimmy-mcgriff.md]
- Later labels: Groove Merchant, Milestone, Telarc; active until 2007.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-jimmy-mcgriff.md]
