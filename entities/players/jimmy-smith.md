# Jimmy Smith (1928-2005)

Born **James Oscar Smith, December 8, 1928** in Norristown, Pennsylvania; died **February 8, 2005** in Scottsdale, Arizona. American jazz organist who single-handedly turned the [Hammond B-3](/entities/instruments/hammond-b3.md) into a jazz instrument and bridged jazz and 1960s soul music.[^wikipedia-jimmy-smith]

## Early years
- Self-taught piano; at nine he won a Philadelphia radio talent contest as a boogie-woogie pianist.[^wikipedia-jimmy-smith]
- After the U.S. Navy he studied at the Royal Hamilton College of Music (1948) and the Leo Ornstein School of Music in Philadelphia (1949).
- Explored the [Hammond organ](/entities/manufacturers/hammond-organ-company.md) in 1951; played piano then organ in Philly R&B bands (Don Gardner and the Sonotones, 1951-54).
- **Switched to organ permanently in 1954 after hearing [Wild Bill Davis](/entities/players/wild-bill-davis.md) play** — the most influential gig-jumping decision in organ history.[^wikipedia-jimmy-smith]

## Style
- Virtuoso improvisation on the [B-3](/entities/instruments/hammond-b3.md) combined with **hornet-like single-note lines**: bebop vocabulary for fast tempos; walking-bass pedal work on ballads; pedals for attack emphasis on uptempo pieces to imitate a string bass.[^wikipedia-jimmy-smith]
- Signature drawbar recipe = **first three drawbars on the "B" preset, upper manual, with 3rd-harmonic [percussion](/concepts/harmonic-percussion.md) added**. Frequently quoted as "888000000" (16' + 5 1/3' + 8' at full volume).
- Used the [harmonic percussion](/concepts/harmonic-percussion.md) aggressively as a percussive horn attack.[^wikipedia-hammond-organ]
- Almost always performed in a **trio** (organ, guitar, drums) — the format that made the B-3 affordable and club-friendly.[^wikipedia-jimmy-smith]

## Career milestones
- 1956: signed by Blue Note Alfred Lion after hearing him in a Philadelphia club. ~40 Blue Note sessions in 8 years. Albums: _A New Sound... A New Star..._, _The Champ!_, _The Sermon!_, _House Party_, _Home Cookin'_, _Midnight Special_, _Back at the Chicken Shack_, _Prayer Meetin'_.
- 1962: jumped to Verve. _Bashin'_ (with the Oliver Nelson big band), _The Cat_ (Lalo Schifrin big-band arrangements), _The Dynamic Duo_ and _Further Adventures of Jimmy and Wes_ (with Wes Montgomery), _The Boss_ (with George Benson), _Who is Afraid of Virginia Woolf?_.
- 1972: opened a supper club at 12910 Victory Blvd, North Hollywood. Recorded _Root Down_ there — a live album that became **a seminal influence on later generations of funk and hip-hop**; the Beastie Boys sampled its bassline for their "Root Down" and tipped Smith in the lyrics.[^wikipedia-jimmy-smith]
- 1980s-1990s revival: Blue Note, Verve, Elektra, Milestone. Recorded with Quincy Jones, Frank Sinatra, **Michael Jackson** (on the title track of _Bad_), Dee Dee Bridgewater, and **Joey DeFrancesco** (a protege whose own wiki page has not yet been added — pending a dedicated source).[^wikipedia-jimmy-smith]
- 2000: last album, _Dot Com Blues_ (Blue Thumb/Verve), with B. B. King, Dr. John, Etta James.[^wikipedia-jimmy-smith]
- 2005: posthumously released _Legacy_ with Joey DeFrancesco. Died in his sleep of natural causes shortly before a planned joint tour.[^wikipedia-jimmy-smith]

## Honors
- **2005 NEA Jazz Masters Award** — the highest U.S. honor for a jazz musician.[^wikipedia-jimmy-smith]

## Influence
- The next generation of jazz organists: Jimmy McGriff, Brother Jack McDuff, Don Patterson, Richard "Groove" Holmes, **Joey DeFrancesco**, Tony Monaco, Larry Goldings.
- Rock keyboardists who openly cited him: **Jon Lord, Brian Auger, Keith Emerson**.
- Downstream: **Medeski, Martin & Wood**; the entire **acid-jazz** scene of the 1990s (Smith is widely called the "father of acid jazz"); the **Beastie Boys** via the _Root Down_ sample.[^wikipedia-jimmy-smith]
