# Mother Earth (US band)

**Mother Earth** was an eclectic American blues-rock band formed in 1967 in California (frontwoman Tracy Nelson, after she moved from Madison, Wisconsin to San Francisco). Not to be confused with the British acid-jazz band also called Mother Earth (1991–1996, Acid Jazz Records, organist Bryn Barklam).[^wikipedia-mother-earth-american-band]

## Key facts
- Played the Avalon Ballroom and Fillmore Auditorium in the late 1960s; included on the soundtrack of the 1968 film _Revolution_.[^wikipedia-mother-earth-american-band]
- Signed to Mercury Records and recorded four albums.[^wikipedia-mother-earth-american-band]
- After the first album the band moved its base from the Bay Area to a farm outside Nashville, Tennessee; the nucleus solidified around Nelson, keyboardist Andy McMahon and guitarist John "Toad" Andrews.[^wikipedia-mother-earth-american-band]

## The Hammond / keyboard chair
- Often remembered as Tracy Nelson's vehicle, but _[Living with the Animals](/entities/albums/living-with-the-animals.md)_ (1968) is a true group effort; guest guitar by Mike Bloomfield.[^rockasteria-mother-earth-living-with-animals]
- The keyboard chairs rotated: early members Wayne Talbert and Ira Kamin (1967), then **[Mark Naftalin](/entities/people/mark-naftalin.md)** (ex-Paul Butterfield Blues Band) on piano, organ and keyboards in the 1968 lineup; session organ by Barry Goldberg also appears on the album.[^wikipedia-mother-earth-american-band][^rockasteria-mother-earth-living-with-animals]
- The Hammond here is a supporting soul voice behind Nelson's vocals — church-and-Chicago tradition, not the lead-instrument style of [Jon Lord](/entities/players/jon-lord.md) or [Keith Emerson](/entities/players/keith-emerson.md).
