# The James Taylor Quartet (JTQ)

British four-piece jazz-funk band formed in 1985 in Rochester, Kent, by **Hammond organist James Taylor** (b. — see note) after the break-up of his former band the Prisoners and Stiff Records' bankruptcy. Current lineup: James Taylor (organ), Mark Cox (guitar), Andrew McKinney (bass), Pat Illingworth (drums), with vocalist Yvonne Yanney on many recordings. Genres: acid jazz, jazz funk; active 1985–present.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-james-taylor-quartet.md]

JTQ is the band that gave the modern Hammond revival its template — so much so that the revival's own record label, [[hammondbeat|Hammondbeat Records]], began in 1997 as the JTQ fan site **JTQgroove**.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-hammondbeat.md]

## The Hammond-led sound
- Debut single **"Blow-Up"** (1987) — a funked-up Herbie Hancock film theme on the label that became Acid Jazz Records; championed by NME and John Peel (Festive Fifty 1987).^[raw/articles/wikipedia-james-taylor-quartet.md]
- *Mission Impossible* (1987): 1960s film-theme covers ("Alfie", "Goldfinger") in a "rough, up-tempo, almost punk-like style... primarily focused on Taylor's Hammond organ playing". *The Money Spyder* (1987): imaginary spy-film soundtrack of Taylor originals.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-james-taylor-quartet.md]
- Signature tune **"The Theme from Starsky and Hutch"** (1988) with Fred Wesley and Pee Wee Ellis of the JBs.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-james-taylor-quartet.md]

## Phases
1. **Film-theme beginnings** (1987–91): instrumental organ-funk, strong live reputation, *Absolute – JTQ Live* (1991).
2. **Acid-jazz vocal era** (early–mid 90s): song-based UK soul/acid-jazz albums with Rose Windross, Alison Limerick, Noel McKoy; "Love the Life" top 40; *In the Hand of the Inevitable* (1995) — still the Acid Jazz label's biggest-selling album.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-james-taylor-quartet.md]
3. **Return to funk roots** (late 90s–): instrumental Hammond-led jazz funk; *Hammond-Ology: The Best of the James Taylor Quartet* (Sanctuary, 2001) collects the organ era. Also library music for Bruton, the New Jersey Kings side project, and the Austin Powers soundtrack.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-james-taylor-quartet.md]

## Legacy
JTQ's success created the market the next-generation organ bands fed on: Italy's [[link-quartet|The Link Quartet]] (JTQ's opening act on tour) and the whole [[hammondbeat]] roster cite the band directly. In the wider Hammond lineage, JTQ is the 1980s/90s UK bridge between the 1960s [[booker-t-jones|soul organ tradition]] and the club era — the same bridge [[john-medeski|Medeski]] was building in the US jam scene.
