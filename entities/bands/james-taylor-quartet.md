# The James Taylor Quartet (JTQ)

British four-piece jazz-funk band formed in 1985 in Rochester, Kent, by **Hammond organist James Taylor** (b. — see note) after the break-up of his former band the Prisoners and Stiff Records' bankruptcy. Current lineup: James Taylor (organ), Mark Cox (guitar), Andrew McKinney (bass), Pat Illingworth (drums), with vocalist Yvonne Yanney on many recordings. Genres: acid jazz, jazz funk; active 1985–present.[1]

JTQ is the band that gave the modern Hammond revival its template — so much so that the revival's own record label, [Hammondbeat Records](/entities/labels/hammondbeat.md), began in 1997 as the JTQ fan site **JTQgroove**.[2]

## The Hammond-led sound
- Debut single **"Blow-Up"** (1987) — a funked-up Herbie Hancock film theme on the label that became Acid Jazz Records; championed by NME and John Peel (Festive Fifty 1987).[1]
- *Mission Impossible* (1987): 1960s film-theme covers ("Alfie", "Goldfinger") in a "rough, up-tempo, almost punk-like style... primarily focused on Taylor's Hammond organ playing". *The Money Spyder* (1987): imaginary spy-film soundtrack of Taylor originals.[1]
- Signature tune **"The Theme from Starsky and Hutch"** (1988) with Fred Wesley and Pee Wee Ellis of the JBs.[1]

## Phases
1. **Film-theme beginnings** (1987–91): instrumental organ-funk, strong live reputation, *Absolute – JTQ Live* (1991).
2. **Acid-jazz vocal era** (early–mid 90s): song-based UK soul/acid-jazz albums with Rose Windross, Alison Limerick, Noel McKoy; "Love the Life" top 40; *In the Hand of the Inevitable* (1995) — still the Acid Jazz label's biggest-selling album.[1]
3. **Return to funk roots** (late 90s–): instrumental Hammond-led jazz funk; *Hammond-Ology: The Best of the James Taylor Quartet* (Sanctuary, 2001) collects the organ era. Also library music for Bruton, the New Jersey Kings side project, and the Austin Powers soundtrack.[1]

## Legacy
JTQ's success created the market the next-generation organ bands fed on: Italy's [The Link Quartet](/entities/bands/link-quartet.md) (JTQ's opening act on tour) and the whole [Hammondbeat Records](/entities/labels/hammondbeat.md) roster cite the band directly. In the wider Hammond lineage, JTQ is the 1980s/90s UK bridge between the 1960s [soul organ tradition](/entities/players/booker-t-jones.md) and the club era — the same bridge [Medeski](/entities/players/john-medeski.md) was building in the US jam scene.
