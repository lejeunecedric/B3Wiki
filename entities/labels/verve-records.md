# Verve Records

**Verve Records** is an American jazz label **founded in 1956 by producer Norman Granz**, created to record his managed star **Ella Fitzgerald** (*Ella Fitzgerald Sings the Cole Porter Song Book* was its first release); under Granz and later Creed Taylor it absorbed Granz's Clef/Norgran catalogues and now claims **the world's largest jazz catalogue**, owned by Universal Music Group.[^wikipedia-verve-records]

## Why it matters to the Hammond story

When [Jimmy Smith](/entities/players/jimmy-smith.md) left Blue Note in 1964, **Verve was the label that carried him to mass popularity** — *The Cat* (1964, with Lalo Schifrin's big-band arrangements), *Orgelblues*, *Got My Mojo Workin'* and the strutting singles era of *The Cat*/*Peter Gunn* LPs turned Smith into an international concert attraction and pushed the B-3 into crossover pop territory.[^wikipedia-jimmy-smith]

Verve and its international imprint network also documented the European organ tradition — most famously French organist [Eddy Louiss](/entities/players/eddy-louiss.md), whose 1971 quartet record with Stan Getz (*Dedicace* lineage) remains a reference of French Hammond jazz, released through Verve's catalogue lineage in France.[^wikipedia-eddy-louiss]
