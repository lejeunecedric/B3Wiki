# Blue Note Records

**Blue Note Records** is an American jazz record label founded in **1939** in New York by German-Jewish emigrants **Alfred Lion** and Max Margulis, with **Francis Wolff** joining in 1939–41; the name comes from the "blue notes" of jazz and the blues.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-blue-note-records.md] After early 10-inch releases of emerging talent (Horace Silver, Clifford Brown), it grew into *the* definitive label of modern jazz, renowned for production quality, Rudy Van Gelder's engineering and Reid Miles's cover design.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-blue-note-records.md]

## Why it matters to the Hammond story

Blue Note is where the **soul-jazz organ** was codified as a commercial and artistic force. **Jimmy Smith signed in 1956** and "performed on the label's first 12″ LP album of new recordings"; his Blue Note run through the late 1950s–60s (era classics like *Back at the Chicken Shack*) defined the modern B-3 trio format and made the organ a headline instrument rather than a novelty.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-blue-note-records.md]^[raw/articles/wikipedia-jimmy-smith.md]

Two decades later, Blue Note's 1968–70 "soul-jazz to funk" roster kept the torch: **Dr. Lonnie Smith** recorded *Move Your Hand*, *Turn it Out* and *Drives* there alongside guitarist **George Benson** and saxophonist **Lou Donaldson**, and later returned to the label for his acclaimed late-career trilogy (*Evolution*, *All in My Mind*, *Breathe*, 2016–2018).^[raw/articles/wikipedia-dr-lonnie-smith.md]

## Hammond-relevant roster highlights

- [[jimmy-smith|Jimmy Smith]] — *The Sermon!*, *Back at the Chicken Shack*, *Midnight Special*: the blueprint of the organ trio^[raw/articles/wikipedia-jimmy-smith.md]
- [[dr-lonnie-smith|Dr. Lonnie Smith]] — late-60s funk organ session era; 21st-century Blue Note comeback^[raw/articles/wikipedia-dr-lonnie-smith.md]
- Related soul-jazz organists on the label and its era: Baby Face Willette, Big John Patton, Reuben Wilson
