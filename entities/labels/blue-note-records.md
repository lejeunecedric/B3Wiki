# Blue Note Records

**Blue Note Records** is an American jazz record label founded in **1939** in New York by German-Jewish emigrants **Alfred Lion** and Max Margulis, with **Francis Wolff** joining in 1939–41; the name comes from the "blue notes" of jazz and the blues.[^wikipedia-blue-note-records] After early 10-inch releases of emerging talent (Horace Silver, Clifford Brown), it grew into *the* definitive label of modern jazz, renowned for production quality, Rudy Van Gelder's engineering and Reid Miles's cover design.[^wikipedia-blue-note-records]

## Why it matters to the Hammond story

Blue Note is where the **soul-jazz organ** was codified as a commercial and artistic force. **Jimmy Smith signed in 1956** and "performed on the label's first 12″ LP album of new recordings"; his Blue Note run through the late 1950s–60s (era classics like *Back at the Chicken Shack*) defined the modern B-3 trio format and made the organ a headline instrument rather than a novelty.[^wikipedia-blue-note-records][^wikipedia-jimmy-smith]

Two decades later, Blue Note's 1968–70 "soul-jazz to funk" roster kept the torch: **Dr. Lonnie Smith** recorded *Move Your Hand*, *Turn it Out* and *Drives* there alongside guitarist **George Benson** and saxophonist **Lou Donaldson**, and later returned to the label for his acclaimed late-career trilogy (*Evolution*, *All in My Mind*, *Breathe*, 2016–2018).[^wikipedia-dr-lonnie-smith]
