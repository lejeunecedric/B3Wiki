# Laurens Hammond (1895–1973)

American mechanical engineer and prolific inventor, born 1895 in Evanston, Illinois. Cornell engineering graduate (1916), WWI veteran, and founder of the [[hammond-organ-company|Hammond Clock Company]] (1928).

## Path to the organ
- Invented a **synchronous motor** that ran silently and steadily on the new 60-cycle household AC supply — the key enabler of both his silent electric clock and the later organ.
- Other inventions: one of the first successful **3D movie systems**, an automated electric bridge table (a Depression-era product whose decline pushed him to look for a new hit).
- Inspiration for the organ: the musical tones produced by the moving gears of his electric clocks; he combined a $15 second-hand piano with a compact tonewheel generator descended from Cahill's Telharmonium.
- Not a musician himself — he had the company's assistant treasurer (trained organist W. L. Lahey) help voice the instrument.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-hammond-organ.md]

## The organ
Filed the patent on **April 24, 1934** (co-inventor John M. Hanert), unveiled April 1935, first [[hammond-b3|Model A]] shipped June 1935. Henry Ford placed the first order (six organs) before production even started — but serial #1 went to a Kansas City dealer and ended up in the Smithsonian.^[raw/articles/hammondorganco-hammond-story.md]

To cut costs he used a 25-note pedalboard instead of the church-standard 32 — it became the de facto standard for decades.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-hammond-organ.md]

## Legacy
He declined to market Don [[donald-leslie|Leslie]]'s rotating speaker and even made the console connectors "Leslie-proof" — a rare misjudgment, as the [[leslie-speaker|Leslie speaker]] became inseparable from the organ's identity.
