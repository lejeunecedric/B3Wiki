# Hammond B-3 (1954–1975)

The most popular Hammond organ ever, in continuous production from 1954 to early 1975 (the C-3 is the identical internal in a church-style cabinet with "modesty panels").

## What defines it
- Two 61-key manuals, 25-note pedalboard, expression pedal, 12 reverse-color preset keys per manual — the console formula dating back to the 1935 Model A.
- **[Touch-Response Percussion](/concepts/harmonic-percussion.md)** — the 1954 addition that made the B-3 *the* jazz organ.
- **[Vibrato/Chorus](/concepts/vibrato-chorus.md)** scanner.
- Nine [drawbars](/concepts/drawbars.md) per manual, tonewheel generation ([Tonewheel Generator](/concepts/tonewheel-generator.md)) until 1975.
- Nearly always paired with a [Leslie 122](/entities/instruments/leslie-speaker.md).

## Cultural weight
The B-3 became the centerpiece of the **organ trio** (organ, drums, guitar or sax) — cheaper for jazz clubs than a big band. [Jimmy Smith](/entities/players/jimmy-smith.md)'s playing (the classic "888000000" drawbar setting) inspired a generation, and the instrument spread through R&B, rock (esp. prog rock), gospel, and reggae in the 1960s–70s.[1]

The last units were assembled from leftover parts and are considered inferior to earlier builds.[1]
