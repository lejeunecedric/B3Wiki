# Leslie Speaker

Combined amplifier + rotating loudspeaker, invented by [[donald-leslie|Donald Leslie]] (production from 1941) to give the Hammond organ the spatial richness of a pipe organ.

## How it works
- **Treble:** rotating horn above a stationary compression driver.
- **Bass:** rotating drum/baffle beneath a stationary woofer.
- The moving sound sources create **Doppler pitch shifts** and tremolo — the most distinctive part of the effect is actually the *speed transition*, not either steady state.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-hammond-organ.md]
- Two speeds: **chorale** (slow) and **tremolo** (fast), toggled via the console's half-moon switch or a pedal.

## Key models
| Model | Notes |
|---|---|
| **122** | Balanced signal, for console organs (B-3/C-3/A-100) — the classic pairing |
| **147** | Unbalanced signal, spinets (with adapter) |
| **Pro-Line** | Portable solid-state series, popular with gigging bands in the 1970s |
| **Heritage** | Modern reissues (Hammond-Suzuki), tube preamp, 122/147 switchable |

## History notes
Hammond refused it 1940, "Leslie-proof" connectors were defeated, official support only came in **1966**; first integrated Leslie in a Hammond was the T-200 (1968). Hammond bought the brand in 1980; **Hammond-Suzuki** owns it since 1992 and still builds 122 reissues plus a Leslie stomp-box simulator. Electronic imitators: Uni-Vibe, Neo Ventilator.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-leslie-speaker.md]
