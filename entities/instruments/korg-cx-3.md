# Korg CX-3 (1980)

Single-manual analog clonewheel organ from [[korg|Korg]] — the company first dedicated Hammond-style instrument and the one credited, by journalist Gordon Reid, with having "come close to emulating the true depth and passion of a vintage Hammond."^[raw/articles/wikipedia-clonewheel-organ.md]

## Position in the family
- **Korg CX-3** (1980) — the analog original.
- **Korg BX-3** (1980) — dual-manual sibling, also analog.
- **Korg New CX-3** / **New BX-3** (2000) — re-released with updated modeling and effects for the 2000s.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-clonewheel-organ.md]

## Why it matters
The CX-3 and the [[roland-corporation|Roland VK-1]] (released the same year) mark the moment where early analog "Hammond-shaped" clonewheels started to credibly imitate a real B-3 — after decades of synthesizer-voiced attempts that failed to capture the upper harmonics and Leslie behavior. Both were widely adopted by touring bands and club-date organists who could not lug a real B-3 around.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-clonewheel-organ.md]
