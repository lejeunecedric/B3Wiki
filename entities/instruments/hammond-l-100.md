# Hammond L-100

The **L-100 series** is Hammond's compact, cheaper spinet line — an economy version launched alongside the M-100 series, with cost-cutting changes so it could retail under $1,000 (simpler two-variation vibrato plus chorus, cheaper expression pedal, self-starting single-switch motor). It sold particularly well in the UK, where several notable British musicians used it instead of a B-3 or C-3. In the wiki's story it matters for one reason above all: it was **Keith Emerson's first Hammond and his stunt organ**.[^wikipedia-hammond-organ][^wikipedia-keith-emerson][^musicradar-emerson-rig]

## The "poor man's" Hammond
- A spinet with shorter manuals, considered a "poor man's Hammond" next to the [B-3](/entities/instruments/hammond-b3.md)-family consoles; Emerson called the [C-3](/entities/instruments/hammond-b3.md) he added around 1968 "far superior" and used the L-100 to "throw around and make it feed back".[^wikipedia-keith-emerson]
- It shares the [tonewheel generator](/concepts/tonewheel-generator.md) and synchronous-motor drive of the big consoles — which is exactly where its distinctive stage pathology comes from.[^musicradar-emerson-rig]

## The Emerson instrument
- Emerson acquired his first L-100 at age 15–16, on hire purchase, after hearing [Jack McDuff](/entities/players/jack-mcduff.md) play "Rock Candy"; with The Nice he ran an L-100 and a C-3 side by side, facing each other, and abused the L-100 — knives in the keys, pushing it over, riding it, triggering feedback through a fuzzbox placed near the speakers.[^wikipedia-keith-emerson]
- **The "wailing" effect**: a mod (wired by his UK and US techs, including Al Goff) kept power on the L-100's amplifier when the power switch was switched off; the capacitor-start AC motor driving the [tonewheel generator](/concepts/tonewheel-generator.md) then lost sync and surged, producing an eerie wail "as if the organ was in pain from Emo's knife attacks".[^musicradar-emerson-rig]
- He owned many L-100s over the years, replaced regularly through structural and component failure from the stage act; his main [C-3](/entities/instruments/hammond-b3.md)s ("Tarkus" and "Pictures at an Exhibition") got the serious mods — high-gain spring reverb, variable percussion and chorus/vibrato controls, refiltered generator, effects loops.[^musicradar-emerson-rig]
- One L-100 caught fire on stage during a 1990s ELP show in Boston — the oil-filled motor-starting capacitor shorted and exploded, igniting the organ scrim; the audience thought it was part of the show. The burned remains are preserved by EMEAPP (Harleysville, PA), alongside Emerson's "Tarkus" C-3 and twin [Leslie 122](/entities/instruments/leslie-speaker.md)s.[^musicradar-emerson-rig]
