# Hammond L-100

The **L-100 series** is Hammond's compact, cheaper spinet line — an economy version launched alongside the M-100 series, with cost-cutting changes so it could retail under $1,000 (simpler two-variation vibrato plus chorus, cheaper expression pedal, self-starting single-switch motor). It sold particularly well in the UK, where several notable British musicians used it instead of a B-3 or C-3. In the wiki's story it matters for one reason above all: it was **Keith Emerson's first Hammond and his stunt organ**.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-hammond-organ.md]^[raw/articles/wikipedia-keith-emerson.md]^[raw/articles/musicradar-emerson-rig.md]

## The "poor man's" Hammond
- A spinet with shorter manuals, considered a "poor man's Hammond" next to the [[hammond-b3|B-3]]-family consoles; Emerson called the [[hammond-b3|C-3]] he added around 1968 "far superior" and used the L-100 to "throw around and make it feed back".^[raw/articles/wikipedia-keith-emerson.md]
- It shares the [[tonewheel-generator|tonewheel generator]] and synchronous-motor drive of the big consoles — which is exactly where its distinctive stage pathology comes from.^[raw/articles/musicradar-emerson-rig.md]

## The Emerson instrument
- Emerson acquired his first L-100 at age 15–16, on hire purchase, after hearing [[jack-mcduff|Jack McDuff]] play "Rock Candy"; with The Nice he ran an L-100 and a C-3 side by side, facing each other, and abused the L-100 — knives in the keys, pushing it over, riding it, triggering feedback through a fuzzbox placed near the speakers.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-keith-emerson.md]
- **The "wailing" effect**: a mod (wired by his UK and US techs, including Al Goff) kept power on the L-100's amplifier when the power switch was switched off; the capacitor-start AC motor driving the [[tonewheel-generator|tonewheel generator]] then lost sync and surged, producing an eerie wail "as if the organ was in pain from Emo's knife attacks".^[raw/articles/musicradar-emerson-rig.md]
- He owned many L-100s over the years, replaced regularly through structural and component failure from the stage act; his main [[hammond-b3|C-3]]s ("Tarkus" and "Pictures at an Exhibition") got the serious mods — high-gain spring reverb, variable percussion and chorus/vibrato controls, refiltered generator, effects loops.^[raw/articles/musicradar-emerson-rig.md]
- One L-100 caught fire on stage during a 1990s ELP show in Boston — the oil-filled motor-starting capacitor shorted and exploded, igniting the organ scrim; the audience thought it was part of the show. The burned remains are preserved by EMEAPP (Harleysville, PA), alongside Emerson's "Tarkus" C-3 and twin [[leslie-speaker|Leslie 122]]s.^[raw/articles/musicradar-emerson-rig.md]
