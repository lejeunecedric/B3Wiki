# Farfisa

**Farfisa** (Fabbriche Italiane Fisarmoniche — "Italian accordion factories") is an Italian musical-instrument company formed by the merger of three accordion manufacturers; it moved into electronic instruments in the late 1950s and introduced its combo organs in the early 1960s as an answer to the [[vox|Vox Continental]].^[raw/articles/wikipedia-farfisa.md]

## The combo-organ rival

Farfisa is the Hammond story's great *counter-example*: cheap, transistorized, portable organs that took the sound of the electric keyboard to bands the B-3 could never reach — on price alone.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-farfisa.md] Key points:

- The **Compact** line was introduced in **1964**, produced at Aspio Terme; cheap Italian labour let Farfisa undercut the competition, which drove its commercial success.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-farfisa.md]
- Popular ranges: the Compact series, the **Professional**, the **FAST** and the **VIP**.
- Users spanned Pink Floyd, Sam the Sham, Country Joe and the Fish, Sly Stone, Elton John, Blondie, Suicide, the B-52s and Philip Glass.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-farfisa.md]
- As the Hammond's popularity *rose* in 1970s rock groups, Farfisa declined — and responded with Hammond-emulating models, electronic pianos and synthesisers; a brief new-wave revival in the late 1970s ended with final models in the early 1980s. The company survives as a consumer-electronics maker.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-farfisa.md]

The contrast is laid out in [[b3-vs-combo-organs|B-3 vs Combo Organs]]: portability and price vs depth of tone.
