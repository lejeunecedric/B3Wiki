# Vox

**Vox** is a British musical-equipment manufacturer **founded in 1957 by Thomas Walter Jennings** in Dartford, Kent — most famous for the AC15/AC30 guitar amplifiers used by the Beatles, the Rolling Stones, the Kinks and the Yardbirds, and for the wah-wah pedal.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-vox.md] Since 1992 Vox has been owned by Japanese electronics company Korg (see [[korg]]).^[raw/articles/wikipedia-vox.md]

## Why it matters to the Hammond story

Vox built the definitive transistor **combo organ**: the **Vox Continental of 1962**, which featured drawbars and used transistors to generate sound.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-vox.md] Like the Farfisa, it is the lightweight, cheap counterpoint to the [[hammond-b3|B-3]] (see [[b3-vs-combo-organs]]):

- John Lennon played a Continental onstage with the Beatles during "I'm Down", including at Shea Stadium in 1965.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-vox.md]
- Alan Price of the Animals played the Continental on the group's first two albums before switching to a Gibson G-101; Doug Ingle's Iron Butterfly used it on "In-A-Gadda-Da-Vida".^[raw/articles/wikipedia-vox.md]

The transistor-drawbar concept — voice selection at a fraction of the tonewheel weight — foreshadows the trade-offs catalogued in [[clonewheel-organ|Clonewheel Organ]] and [[b3-vs-clonewheels|B-3 vs Clonewheels]].
