# Vox

**Vox** is a British musical-equipment manufacturer **founded in 1957 by Thomas Walter Jennings** in Dartford, Kent — most famous for the AC15/AC30 guitar amplifiers used by the Beatles, the Rolling Stones, the Kinks and the Yardbirds, and for the wah-wah pedal.[^wikipedia-vox] Since 1992 Vox has been owned by Japanese electronics company Korg (see [Korg](/entities/manufacturers/korg.md)).[^wikipedia-vox]

## Why it matters to the Hammond story

Vox built the definitive transistor **combo organ**: the **Vox Continental of 1962**, which featured drawbars and used transistors to generate sound.[^wikipedia-vox] Like the Farfisa, it is the lightweight, cheap counterpoint to the [B-3](/entities/instruments/hammond-b3.md) (see [B-3 vs Combo Organs (Vox Continental, Farfisa)](/comparisons/b3-vs-combo-organs.md)):

- John Lennon played a Continental onstage with the Beatles during "I'm Down", including at Shea Stadium in 1965.[^wikipedia-vox]
- Alan Price of the Animals played the Continental on the group's first two albums before switching to a Gibson G-101; Doug Ingle's Iron Butterfly used it on "In-A-Gadda-Da-Vida".[^wikipedia-vox]

The transistor-drawbar concept — voice selection at a fraction of the tonewheel weight — foreshadows the trade-offs catalogued in [Clonewheel Organ](/concepts/clonewheel-organ.md) and [B-3 vs Clonewheels](/comparisons/b3-vs-clonewheels.md).
