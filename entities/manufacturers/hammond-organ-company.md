# Hammond Organ Company

Chicago-based manufacturer, founded 1928 as the **Hammond Clock Company** by [Laurens Hammond](/entities/people/laurens-hammond.md). Produced an estimated **two million organs** — "probably the most successful electronic organs ever made."

## Timeline
- **1935** — first organ, the Model A, ships in June.
- **1936** — FTC false-advertising complaint; famous blind listening tests vs. a $75,000 Skinner pipe organ at Rockefeller Chapel. 1938 cease-and-desist on some claims — but the publicity sold more organs than the legal fees cost.[^wikipedia-hammond-organ]
- **1954** — the [B-3](/entities/instruments/hammond-b3.md) and C-3 introduce harmonic percussion.
- **1968** — first factory-integrated Leslie (T-200 spinet), after decades of official hostility toward [Leslie](/entities/people/donald-leslie.md)'s speaker.
- **1970s** — abandons tonewheels for integrated circuits; the new organs flop.
- **1985/86** — company goes out of business.
- Brand passes to the **Suzuki Musical Instrument Corporation**, which still builds digital Hammonds and Leslies today.[^wikipedia-hammond-organ]

## Market strategy
Originally targeted **churches** as a cheap pipe-organ alternative — not professional musicians (Hammond saw no return there). The jazz/R&B market adopted it on its own. Marketing tools: dedicated dealers, the bi-monthly _Hammond Times_ newsletter, family-centered ads.[^wikipedia-hammond-organ]

A pivotal grassroots moment: Rev. Clarence Cobbs of Chicago's First Church of Deliverance replaced his piano with a Hammond and used it on his radio broadcasts — creating the gospel-organ tradition that fed jazz (Wild Bill Davis, Fats Waller) and eventually nearly every genre.[^hammondorganco-hammond-story]
