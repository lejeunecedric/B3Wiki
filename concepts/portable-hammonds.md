# Portable Hammonds: factory portables vs chops

> Why the ~425 lb [B-3](/entities/instruments/hammond-b3.md) console is rarely the one on stage, and what players do about it.

## The problem
A full [B-3](/entities/instruments/hammond-b3.md) console plus [Leslie](/entities/instruments/leslie-speaker.md) weighs roughly 425 lbs, has no handles, and is shaped like furniture. Larry Goldings notes the practical consequence: touring players usually rely on the venue rider to supply the organ, and otherwise bring something smaller.[^npr-blogsupreme-hammond-b3] Add the geography problem — European venues are often small, old, and full of stairs (cellar clubs, museum rooms, a ship moored in Lyon where the only staircase was exactly as wide as a flight case stood on end) — and a console is simply not an option.[^doctormix-rarest-hammond]

A third, subtler problem is **mains frequency**: a US tonewheel organ runs on 110 V / 60 Hz. Brought to Europe's 50 Hz mains without a cycle converter, the synchronous motor turns at the wrong speed and the organ plays **flat** — because [tone frequency is slaved to motor speed](/concepts/tonewheel-generator.md).[^doctormix-rarest-hammond] European-market Hammonds (and chopped conversions) run on 220 V / 50 Hz.

## The responses

### 1. Chop it ("chopped" / portable conversions)
Take the console apart, discard or shrink the cabinet, and rebuild the generator, manuals and pedalboard into a portable case. This is decades-old working-musician culture (the "Oakland Chop" being a well-known tradition documented on YouTube) — Gregg Allman's stories of hauling B-3s up venue stairs (see [Hammond B-3](/entities/instruments/hammond-b3.md), history section) are the motivation in one sentence.[^wikipedia-hammond-organ]

- **Keyboard Specialties (St Petersburg, Florida)** — a backline builder who produced a handful of "stealth organ" conversions: an entire 1956 [C3](/entities/instruments/hammond-b3.md) rebuilt down into a portable flight case, originally flat black (hence "stealth"). One of them became [Brian Auger](/entities/players/brian-auger.md)'s touring organ.[^doctormix-rarest-hammond]
- **Bertram music (Germany)** — known in the second-hand market for chopping [A-100](/entities/instruments/hammond-b3.md)/B-3 consoles into 220 V / 50 Hz portables; from the 1960s the Freiburg Musikhaus Bertram re-cased full wooden-console Hammonds into ~100 kg transportable "Bertram-Umbau" instruments with dismountable bass pedals — see [Musik-Bertram](/entities/manufacturers/musik-bertram.md).[^doctormix-rarest-hammond]

### 2. Buy a factory portable
- **Hammond-Suzuki Portable B-3mk2** — the modern flagship: two 61-note manuals, 25-note pedalboard, 96 digital tonewheels, dual drawbar sets and a two-rotor simulation; the factory answer to "B-3 sound, moved by two people."
- **Hammond Porta-B (2019)** — a lighter, cheaper portable shown at NAMM 2019; criticized by reviewers and the B3 Guys demo for **poor internal speakers and no physical Leslie** — the portability/faithfulness trade-off in its sharpest form.

Both sit in the [digital emulation](/concepts/clonewheel-organ.md) lineage that began with the Korg CX-3 and XK-3.

## Synthesis
The portables landscape splits along the same line as the [tonewheel-vs-clonewheel](/comparisons/b3-vs-clonewheels.md) divide: chops keep the real [Tonewheel Generator](/concepts/tonewheel-generator.md) (with its leakage, its quirks, its 50/60 Hz cycle problem) in a case you can get down a staircase; factory portables keep the spec sheet and lose the moving parts. Auger's stealth C3 — a chopped organ refinished in Drum Workshop's "Broken Glass" sparkle, carried on its end down a Lyon ship's ladder — is the chops argument made flesh.[^doctormix-rarest-hammond]
