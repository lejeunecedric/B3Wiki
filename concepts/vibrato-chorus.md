# Vibrato/Chorus Scanner

The Hammond's internal modulation effect, developed in the early 1940s and put into production right after WWII (models BV/CV = vibrato only; BCV/DV = vibrato + chorus).^[raw/articles/wikipedia-hammond-organ.md]

## How it works
A **scanning delay line**: a capacitor bank is scanned electronically in sequence, so the signal passes through a continuously varying delay — a phase-modulation vibrato. The **chorus** setting mixes the delayed (modulated) signal with the dry signal, producing a shimmering doubling effect.

- B-2/C-2 (1949): vibrato selectable **per manual**.
- B-3/C-3 (1954): V1–V3 / C1–C3 depth selector.
- Distinct from the [[leslie-speaker|Leslie]]: the scanner is pitch/phase modulation inside the organ; the Leslie is physical acoustic rotation outside. Players use both — e.g. vibrato off + Leslie tremolo, or C3 + chorale for gospel warmth.
