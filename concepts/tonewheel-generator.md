# Tonewheel Generator

The Hammond's sound source until 1975: a bank of metal **tonewheels** ("phonic wheels") spinning near electromagnetic pickups, each shaped to induce a voltage at a specific frequency — one tone per wheel.

## How it works
- All wheels are driven by a **synchronous motor** locked to the 60 Hz mains supply — which is why Hammonds hold pitch perfectly but only work where the mains frequency matches (50/60 Hz models differ).
- Descends from Cahill's 1897 **Telharmonium** (alternator-based tone generation), miniaturized: Laurens Hammond replaced huge alternators with small wheels + an amplifier.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-hammond-organ.md]
- Each note is a mix of wheel signals; the organ is thus a real **additive synthesizer** (see [[drawbars]]).
- Keyboards derive notes from a 12-wheel master octave via gearing, adding characteristic **crosstalk and gear-murmur** that players now consider part of the sound.

## Why it matters
- The "warmth" everyone chases (including every clonewheel emulation) comes from this electromechanical imperfection: crosstalk between pickups, wheel flutter, key click from keying transients.
- Hammond abandoned tonewheels for integrated circuits in the 1970s — the products flopped and the company died in 1985; the lesson: the flaws *were* the product.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-hammond-organ.md]
