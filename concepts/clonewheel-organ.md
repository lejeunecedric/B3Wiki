# Clonewheel Organ

A **clonewheel organ** is any electronic instrument built to emulate the sound of the electromechanical [[tonewheel-generator|tonewheel]] organs the [[hammond-organ-company|Hammond Organ Company]] made from the 1930s to the 1970s — but without the actual spinning wheels. The name is a pun on "tonewheel."^[raw/articles/wikipedia-clonewheel-organ.md]

## Why it exists
A B-3 + bench + pedalboard weighs ~425 lb / 193 kg, and a [[leslie-speaker|Leslie]] speaker adds more on top — and the whole rig can break in transit because vintage Hammonds are electromechanical and parts are scarce. Clonewheels trade some authenticity for weight savings and reliability.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-clonewheel-organ.md]

## Three generations
1. **First "clones" (1970s)** — used synthesizer voices; criticized for weak upper harmonics and a fake-sounding Leslie. Slowly converging on the real sound.
2. **Early analog emulation (1980)** — [[korg-cx-3|Korg CX-3]] and [[roland-corporation|Roland]] VK-1. "Fairly realistic" but still lossy in the nuance. Jazz and blues organists kept playing real B-3s on recordings.
3. **1990s-2000s DSP/sampling** — [[hammond-xk-3|Hammond-Suzuki XK-3]], [[nord-c2d|Clavia Nord C Series]], [[korg-cx-3|Korg New CX-3]], [[roland-corporation|VK-7/VK-8]]. Algorithms now model tonewheel **leakage / crosstalk**, dirty key contacts, key click, the tube-amp growl, and the rotating Leslie.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-clonewheel-organ.md]

## Formats
- **Integrated keyboard** (one chassis) — [[korg-cx-3|Korg CX-3]], [[hammond-xk-3|Hammond XK-3]], [[nord-c2d|Nord C2D]].
- **Rack module + MIDI controller** — E-MU B-3 module, Voce microB, GSi VB3-driven racks.
- **Software ("virtual synth")** — Native Instruments B4 (discontinued), GSi VB3, setBfree (open source).

The PARI.E K-61 is the rare outlier: it uses real modern electromagnetic tonewheels, not DSP.

## What is intentionally NOT reproduced
A _Keyboard Magazine_ "Clonewheel Heaven" review (Nov 2004) argued that digital simulations cannot fully recreate "the complex interplay of variables that create the 'Hammond sound'" — specifically the tonewheel leakage and the way a Leslie behaves in a real room. Many jazz/blues organists therefore still pull a vintage B-3 out for studio work, even if they gig with a clone.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-clonewheel-organ.md]
