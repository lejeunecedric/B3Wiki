# Clonewheel Organ

A **clonewheel organ** is any electronic instrument built to emulate the sound of the electromechanical [tonewheel](/concepts/tonewheel-generator.md) organs the [Hammond Organ Company](/entities/manufacturers/hammond-organ-company.md) made from the 1930s to the 1970s — but without the actual spinning wheels. The name is a pun on "tonewheel."[1]

## Why it exists
A B-3 + bench + pedalboard weighs ~425 lb / 193 kg, and a [Leslie](/entities/instruments/leslie-speaker.md) speaker adds more on top — and the whole rig can break in transit because vintage Hammonds are electromechanical and parts are scarce. Clonewheels trade some authenticity for weight savings and reliability.[1]

## Three generations
1. **First "clones" (1970s)** — used synthesizer voices; criticized for weak upper harmonics and a fake-sounding Leslie. Slowly converging on the real sound.
2. **Early analog emulation (1980)** — [Korg CX-3](/entities/instruments/korg-cx-3.md) and [Roland](/entities/manufacturers/roland-corporation.md) VK-1. "Fairly realistic" but still lossy in the nuance. Jazz and blues organists kept playing real B-3s on recordings.
3. **1990s-2000s DSP/sampling** — [Hammond-Suzuki XK-3](/entities/instruments/hammond-xk-3.md), [Clavia Nord C Series](/entities/instruments/nord-c2d.md), [Korg New CX-3](/entities/instruments/korg-cx-3.md), [VK-7/VK-8](/entities/manufacturers/roland-corporation.md). Algorithms now model tonewheel **leakage / crosstalk**, dirty key contacts, key click, the tube-amp growl, and the rotating Leslie.[1]

## Formats
- **Integrated keyboard** (one chassis) — [Korg CX-3](/entities/instruments/korg-cx-3.md), [Hammond XK-3](/entities/instruments/hammond-xk-3.md), [Nord C2D](/entities/instruments/nord-c2d.md).
- **Rack module + MIDI controller** — E-MU B-3 module, Voce microB, GSi VB3-driven racks.
- **Software ("virtual synth")** — Native Instruments B4 (discontinued), GSi VB3, setBfree (open source).

The PARI.E K-61 is the rare outlier: it uses real modern electromagnetic tonewheels, not DSP.

## What is intentionally NOT reproduced
A _Keyboard Magazine_ "Clonewheel Heaven" review (Nov 2004) argued that digital simulations cannot fully recreate "the complex interplay of variables that create the 'Hammond sound'" — specifically the tonewheel leakage and the way a Leslie behaves in a real room. Many jazz/blues organists therefore still pull a vintage B-3 out for studio work, even if they gig with a clone.[1]
