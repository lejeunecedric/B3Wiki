# Query: How did the Hammond organ become the church instrument — and how did the church feed everything else?

## Answer in brief

The Hammond was designed as an affordable pipe-organ substitute for churches, adopted first by Black congregations in Chicago, carried into gospel by radio-savvy pastors, and then exported to jazz, soul, R&B and rock by players who grew up on the church bench.

## The Chicago entry point

In 1939, Pastor Clarence Cobbs of the First Church of Deliverance in Chicago bought one of the earliest Hammonds — deliberately, as a marketing move. Cobbs was a radio pioneer (his broadcast started in 1935, when hardly any African-American pastors were on air), and listeners intrigued by the "strange new sounds" on the radio drove to the church to hear the organ in person: streets lined with cars for blocks, celebrities in the pews — Billie Holiday reportedly showed up with her dog in her purse.[^npr-hammond-gospel-hit-train]

Gospel writer Bob Marovich's summary of why the instrument stuck: "A pipe organ couldn't shout, but a Hammond organ could shout." Its warm, warbling sound spread to churches beyond Chicago and became the default soundtrack of the brand-new genre called gospel. Organist James Bryson Jr. (First Church of Deliverance): "You are not going to find too many black churches that don't have a Hammond organ."[^npr-hammond-gospel-hit-train]

## Church → jazz → everything

- The church-to-jazz jump is the classic route: [Wild Bill Davis](/entities/players/wild-bill-davis.md) tried the Hammond in a jazz setting after hearing it in church, with Fats Waller and [Jimmy Smith](/entities/players/jimmy-smith.md) building the movement — per the Hammond company's own history.[^wikipedia-hammond-organ]
- The 1960s saw the church palette "bleed over onto the jazz, blues, R&B and even pop charts."[^npr-hammond-gospel-hit-train]
- Soul-jazz players were raised on it: [Dr. Lonnie Smith](/entities/players/dr-lonnie-smith.md) heard organs in churches and record stores long before he ever touched one in Buffalo, and described the instrument as "me, and as an extension of me."[^nprillinois-dr-lonnie-smith-obituary]
- The lineage continues on the modern scene: [Cory Henry](/entities/players/cory-henry.md) came up playing gospel in Brooklyn before leading The Funk Apostles.[^sfjazz-cory-henry-funk-apostles]
- [Booker T. Jones](/entities/players/booker-t-jones.md) bridges the church/R&B organ tradition to rock.[^wikipedia-booker-t-jones]

## The retreat — and the parts problem

As popular music moved away from the Hammond sound in the 1980s–90s, churches followed: mega-churches bought smaller keyboards that "sound more like what you'd hear on the radio" — Marovich again: "A Hammond organ sounds like grandma's church." Meanwhile the parts pipeline dried up: Jerry Welch, who bought the Hammond Organ Company's entire repair-parts inventory when it folded in 1985, went from 80–100 orders a day to 10–12 a week — "literally selling ourself out of business."[^npr-hammond-gospel-hit-train]

The instrument's church standing survives in pockets like First Church of Deliverance itself, where Bryson vows to "reinvent it and bring it back."
